# LONGING RESEARCH — Setting a Security's Execution and Judgment Weights

Purpose: propose and defend a **procedure** by which a fictional research institution assigns, to each security it lists, a fixed pair of weights — an execution share and a judgment share — that describe the practice the security stands for.

This is a **procedure design task, not a valuation task.** You are not asked to set any security's weights. You are asked how they should be set so that the result is careful, precise, and defensible.

---

# 1. Fixed context — do not argue with it

A fictional research house publishes prices for securities that each stand for a human practice: writing a letter, meeting a person, asking after someone, and others. Prices fall over thirty years, 1996–2026, in three markets — Japan, Korea, the United States.

The mechanism that moves them is a set of technology waves. Each wave has two effects in order: **arrival**, which creates or **restores** a way of reaching a person, and **substitution**, which lets a person do alone what needed someone else. The following are decided and are not open in this round:

1. **Restoration counts as arrival.** A technology that gives back a capacity illness, distance or circumstance had taken — a speech decoder for a person who lost speech to ALS is the reference case — acts in the arrival phase, like a technology that creates a new mode of connection.
2. **Substitution counts substitution of judgment.** What the substitution channel measures is the system deciding what the person could have decided — the content, the consequential choice. Handing over the *doing* while keeping the choice is not, by itself, substitution.
3. **Every security carries two fixed weights that are part of its definition:** an **execution share** and a **judgment share**, a pair summing to one. They describe how much of the practice is doing and how much is deciding. They do not vary with the era.
4. **What varies with the era is measured separately:** the security's **exposure** to execution restored and its **exposure** to judgment substituted. That measurement is not this round's subject.
5. **A security's weights may be revised only under two conditions fixed in advance:** (a) the security's own definition changes — a step of the practice is added or removed; (b) the same security's weights disagree across the three markets. Revision because the generated record fits badly is **rejected**; the weights must not be tuned to outcomes.

Known hard cases the procedure must handle, from the record:

- Some execution has a restoring side: voicing a sentence the person chose; reading a text aloud in a voice learned from another person.
- Some execution hands over the deliberation whole: sending *how are you* and letting a system translate it hands over the work of deciding how to ask after someone, so it is judgment substituted, not execution.
- Some judgment is kept but narrowed: a system's interpretation of another person's message is read before the original, and the person's own decision changes without any step being handed over.
- The reference security is **LETTER**: a personally composed, physically handwritten message intended for a particular person and released to that person through a delivery route.

---

# 2. What this round is NOT for

Do not set weights for any security. If you use a security as a worked example, mark every number as illustrative.

Do not propose what the market should do with the weights, how exposure is measured, or how prices are formed. Those are decided or open elsewhere.

Do not revisit the five fixed points above. If you think one is wrong, say so in one paragraph at the end under *Objections*, and design for it as given.

Do not assess whether the artistic premise is sound.

---

# 3. Rules of engagement

**Design for precision, not speed.** The user's condition on this work is that the weights be decided *very carefully and precisely*, because a loosely set weight carries its error into every era's reading for thirty years.

**Prefer a procedure that can be repeated by someone else** and reach the same pair within a stated tolerance. Say what the tolerance is.

**Separate the sorting rule from the counting rule.** How an act or a step is classified as execution or judgment is one question; how the classifications are turned into a pair summing to one is another. Answer both.

**Say what your procedure cannot decide.** A case your rule leaves ambiguous should be named as such, with the fallback you propose.

**State your training cut-off** if any claim depends on knowledge of real practices after it.

---

# 4. Required output

## Section 1 — The unit of classification

What is the thing that gets labelled execution or judgment: the whole practice, an occasion of it, a step within an occasion, a decision point? Define it so that LETTER decomposes into a finite, listable set. Show the decomposition for LETTER.

## Section 2 — The sorting rule

State the test that classifies one unit as execution or judgment. The record's candidate is *whether the step handed over contained more than one live option*. Adopt, refine or replace it. Apply it to the three hard cases above and say what each becomes.

If a unit can be partly both, say how the part is set and by whom.

## Section 3 — The counting rule

State how classified units become a pair summing to one. Equal count, weighted by time, weighted by consequence, weighted by how often the step is the reason the practice fails — or something else. Say what the weighting assumes and what it distorts.

## Section 4 — Who sets it, and how disagreement is resolved

The user makes the final call. Propose what is put in front of them: a single proposed pair, a range, several independent classifications reconciled, or a decomposition they weigh themselves. Say how two people who disagree on one step's label reach one pair.

## Section 5 — Cross-market consistency

A security lists separately in Japan, Korea and the United States. State whether the procedure yields one pair for all three or one per market, and how it detects the disagreement that condition (b) makes a ground for revision.

## Section 6 — The revision procedure

Given conditions (a) and (b) only, state what a revision record contains, who may initiate it, what evidence satisfies each condition, and what happens to readings already published under the old pair.

## Section 7 — Failure modes

List the ways this procedure could produce a confident wrong pair, ranked by how hard each is to detect afterwards.

## Objections

Optional, at most one paragraph.

# 5. Form

Plain prose and tables. No code. Number every section as above so responses can be compared side by side. Illustrative numbers must be marked *illustrative*.
