# LONGING RESEARCH — What Modernization Took, and How the Literature Reads the Author's View

Purpose: gather **academic sources and considered opinions** on two questions, for a fictional research institution whose securities stand for human experiences that modernization removes.

1. **What did modernization take from people before AI?** From the Luddites (1811) to about 2020, wave by wave.
2. **How does published thought read the author's view below?** Where does it agree, where does it complicate, and what does the author's view leave out?

This is a **collection and critique round, not a selection round.** Nothing you write will be adopted from this round. The author decides afterwards.

---

# 1. Fixed context — do not argue with it

LONGING RESEARCH is an artwork. It presents itself as a dry financial research terminal: indices, price charts, analyst reports. The assets it covers are human experiences, not companies. Prices decline over the long run because the author believes romance is genuinely disappearing; nobody inside the fiction argues this, and the decline must come from structural forces the model publishes.

The work is built **top-down**. Before any security is chosen, the author analyses what romance is, what is human, and what we are handing over to AI and to modernization. The abstracted concepts become an ontology; the securities come last. This round feeds that analysis.

## The author's view, as stated

1. **Origin.** Something is romantic when its origin is a person's own judgment and reflection. The medium does not matter: letters, text messages and favours are the same. A conversation among gathered people whose source is "the AI said…" is not romantic.
2. **Exchange as mutual bonds.** What people give and receive is issuing bonds — debts — to each other. Love, understanding and trust arise only by owing each other. Solitary reflection is romantic; shared reflection is more.
3. **Balances.** Love, understanding, trust and belief are balances. The main problem is that they are decreasing — through light facts and rumours from social media and AI — rather than that hatred and misunderstanding are increasing.
4. **What is human.** What a person does, and what a person does with a person. Mistakes and inefficiency are human and romantic: someone walks forty minutes home instead of taking a ten-minute bus, to think the whole way. Efficiency and rationality are not romance.
5. **A test for AI.** Does it gather people or scatter them into individualism? Does it grow critical thought and reflection or replace it? Time saved by AI and spent with people can increase romance.
6. **Drift through comfort.** We are neither robbed nor giving freely. Like Odysseus's men on Circe's island, comfort — algorithmic feeds, personalised and generated content — makes things drift, until we give up even the sense of what we are losing.
7. **Gathering.** Gathering, deliberating together and going back and forth is what made people human; this has declined as people work and talk with AI each on their own.
8. **The market's floor.** The financial market's origin is mutual belief and credit; all its complex, mathematical structure stands on it.

# 2. What this round is NOT for

Do not choose securities, indices or factors. Do not propose prices, coefficients or a model.

Do not assess whether the artistic premise is sound, and do not argue that romance is or is not disappearing.

**Do not invent sources.** A work you believe exists but cannot verify is wanted, marked as such. A confident citation of a work that does not exist, or a wrong author, year or claim, is the failure mode. Earlier rounds in this project received fabricated series marked as verified.

---

# 3. Rules of engagement

**Mark each source:** `verified` — you are confident of author, title, year and the claim you attribute; `probable` — you believe it exists but are unsure of a detail; `unverified` — you recall it but cannot confirm it.

**Separate the source's claim from your reading of it.** Write what the work argues, then, separately, how it bears on the author's view.

**Say where evidence is contested.** For empirical questions — television and social capital, smartphones and youth wellbeing, filter bubbles, cognitive offloading to AI — give both sides.

**Range widely.** Philosophy, sociology, history, anthropology, economics, media studies, psychology. Classical and non-Western thought is welcome.

## B. The author's view against published thought

For each of the eight points: works that support it, works that complicate or contradict it, and the strongest objection a careful critic would raise. Then: **what the literature contains that the author's view lacks** — concepts, distinctions or losses the eight points do not name.

## C. Recurring concepts
