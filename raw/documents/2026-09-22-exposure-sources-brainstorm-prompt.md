# LONGING RESEARCH — Candidate Sources for Per-Era Exposure

Purpose: **collect**, as widely as possible, kinds of data from which a fictional research institution could measure, year by year from 1996 to 2026 and in Japan, Korea and the United States, two things about a human practice: how much of its *doing* technology has restored, and how much of its *deciding* technology has taken over.

This is a **collection round, not a selection round.** Breadth is wanted. Nothing you list will be adopted from this round; the list will be narrowed later by quantity and quality.

---

# 1. Fixed context — do not argue with it

A fictional research house publishes prices for securities that each stand for a human practice. Each security carries two fixed weights that are part of its definition — an **execution share** (how much of the practice is doing) and a **judgment share** (how much is deciding), summing to one. Those weights are not this round's subject.

What varies with the era, and what this round is about, are two **exposures** per security, measured per year:

- **Exposure to execution restored** — how far technology of the period gave back the *doing* of this practice to people who had lost it or never had it: illness, distance, disability, cost, literacy. The reference case is a speech decoder for a person who lost speech to ALS.
- **Exposure to judgment substituted** — how far technology of the period decided, in this practice, what the person could have decided: the content, the consequential choice. Handing over the doing while keeping the choice does **not** count here. Letting a system translate *how are you* **does** count, because the deliberation over how to ask after someone is handed over whole.

The reference security is **LETTER**: a personally composed, physically handwritten message intended for a particular person and released to that person through a delivery route. Use it as the worked case throughout; where a source would cover other practices too — meeting, calling, gifting, asking after someone — say so.

The span is thirty years, 1996–2026, and the markets are Japan, Korea and the United States. A source that covers one market or part of the span is still wanted; say what it covers.

---

# 2. What this round is NOT for

Do not choose. Do not rank, except as asked in Section 4. Do not say which source *should* be used.

Do not invent a series. A source you believe exists but cannot verify is wanted, marked as such. A confident description of a series that does not exist is the failure mode.

Do not propose how exposures enter the price, or how the two weights are set.

Do not assess whether the artistic premise is sound.

---

# 3. Rules of engagement

**Go wide before going deep.** Official statistics, academic surveys, industry disclosures, platform data, assistive-technology registries, postal and telecom records, clinical and disability data, patent and product timelines, digitised corpora, oral-history archives, qualitative studies. A kind of data you would not normally think of as a measurement is in scope if it varies by year.

**Mark status per row:** `verified` — you are confident the source exists with that name and coverage; `probable` — something of this kind exists but you are unsure of its exact form; `speculative` — a kind of data that would serve if anyone had collected it. All three are wanted.

**Say what each source actually observes.** Most sources observe adoption or use, not restoration or substitution. State the inference needed to get from what the source counts to the exposure, and how weak it is.

**State your training cut-off** and mark anything after it as outside what you can attest.

---

# 4. Required output

## Section 1 — Sources for execution restored

One table, one row per candidate:

| kind of data | source name | publisher or holder | markets | first year | last year | what it observes | inference to the exposure | status |

Aim for breadth: at least fifteen rows if you can, across at least five kinds of data.

## Section 2 — Sources for judgment substituted

Same table, same aim. Note especially any source that separates *who wrote the content* from *who sent it*, or that records assistance at the level of the decision rather than the keystroke.

## Section 3 — Sources that observe both, or the boundary between them

Any source in which the same act can be read as restoration or as substitution depending on its intensity — autocomplete, predictive text, voice synthesis from a learned voice, translation, summarisation. Say what in the source would let the two be told apart, if anything.

## Section 4 — A first sort by quantity and quality

Not a selection. For each row above, two marks:

- **Quantity** — how many year-market cells of the ninety (30 years × 3 markets) the source fills.
- **Quality** — how many inference steps stand between what it counts and the exposure, and whether the definition holds steady across the span.

Present as a list grouped by kind of data, so that a later round can compare kinds before comparing sources.

## Section 5 — What no source covers

State plainly the years, markets and exposures for which you found nothing, and what kind of data *would* have covered them.

---

# 5. Form

Tables and short prose. No code. Number every section as above so responses can be compared side by side.
