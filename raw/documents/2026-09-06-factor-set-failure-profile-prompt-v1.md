# LONGING — Factor Set Failure Profile Review

Round 3.

Purpose: map what breaks under each of six fixed factor sets, without choosing among them.

---

# 1. What this round is for

Two prior rounds ran.

Round 1 reviewed a first market model and concluded: reduce.

Round 2 proposed that complexity could return if it were causally identified, and put that question to six independent reviewers. Each reviewer returned a different factor set. The sets range from five factors to twelve. They are irreconcilable by argument — each round of advocacy has produced one additional opinion, not a converging one.

This round forbids advocacy.

You are given six factor sets, fixed. Your task is to take each set as given and report, set by set, what specifically breaks under it — which securities it prices wrongly, which events it cannot attribute, where it fails to identify, where it double counts, and what it structurally cannot say. You are not asked whether a set is good. You are asked what a set costs.

The output of this round is six failure profiles, laid side by side, so that a choice can be made by someone comparing costs rather than by a seventh round of argument.

---

# 2. What this round is NOT for

Do not propose a seventh factor set.

Do not propose a hybrid or a merged set.

Do not rename a factor as a way of repairing it, outside the one section reserved for that below.

Do not rank the six sets.

Do not name a preferred set, a preferred count, or a preferred architecture, anywhere in your answer, including in passing.

Do not treat a fatal flaw in a set as an invitation to fix it in the same breath you report it. Record it. Reporting is the task.

Do not recommend expanding the security universe. Whether to expand it is a separate decision, taken elsewhere, and out of scope here.

If you believe the framing of this round is itself wrong, say so once, in the section reserved for refusals, and nowhere else.

---

# 3. Background

LONGING is a research-oriented interactive artwork presented through the visual and institutional language of a financial research terminal.

Its securities are not companies. They are human practices whose social value may be changing as technology and culture change: Handwritten Letter, Waiting for a Reply, Unplanned Phone Call, Unplanned Visit, Aimless Walking, Film Photography, Physical Media, Serendipitous Discovery, Handmade Object, Live Performance, Solitude.

The surface is dry, rigorous, financial. The subjects are fragile, subjective, and hard to quantify. That contrast is intentional. The guiding principle is that an extremely calculated system attempts to price things that resist calculation.

LONGING carries a quiet bias toward the human, but the model must remain able to disagree with the artist. Film photography must be capable of a genuine multi-year bull market if the modeled world supports it.

Prices strike weekly. Formal research publishes monthly and sets an anchor against which the market price can diverge. Every weekly price move is published as an exact attribution ledger, a set of named lines that sum to the total return with no unexplained remainder. Macro-like structural factors move whole groups of securities with no security-specific news required. Securities also carry their own fundamentals — security-level, not shared — and market positioning is tracked separately from underlying belief, so a bearish consensus that is already fully positioned for decline can still produce a violent rally on a small positive surprise.

This is what you need to reason about consequences. It is not the full design brief.

# 4. Rules of engagement

**The six sets are presented blind.** They are labelled SET A through SET F. No model name and no origin is given. One of these sets may be your own earlier proposal. Do not attempt to identify authorship. Do not favor a set because its reasoning resembles your own. Judge only what is on the page.

**The security universe is fixed at eleven for this round.** The eleven are listed in Section 3. Do not recommend expanding it. Where a set cannot be identified at eleven securities — where two or more factors move the same eleven securities in ways too similar to separate — record that as a failure mode of that set, and name the specific factors that collapse into each other. Identification failure at eleven securities is a finding for this round. Expanding the universe is not this round's remedy for it.

**No new sets, no hybrids, no renaming as repair,** except in the one section below reserved for a minimal repair.

# 5. The six sets

Each set is reproduced with its factors, one-line definitions, and its stated grouping where one exists. Treat all six as equally serious proposals.

---

## SET A — 8 factors

No grouping stated.

1. **Synthetic Substitution** — human generation, labor, or presence functionally replicated by non-human systems without loss of primary utility.
2. **Temporal Compression** — systemic intolerance for latency, delay, and asynchronous interaction.
3. **Variance Elimination** — structural removal of physical, navigational, and probabilistic chance via prediction and routing systems.
4. **Social Atomization** — decline of spontaneous, low-stakes community density, trust, and default physical proximity.
5. **Attention Scarcity** — aggregate fragmentation of human focus and saturation of cognitive carrying capacity.
6. **Friction Premium** — cyclical market willingness to reward effort, inconvenience, and resistance to optimization.
7. **Performative Legibility** — degree to which a practice can be signaled, aestheticized, and broadcast for social capital.
8. **Physical Grounding** — systemic demand for a localized, non-digital, spatial carrier for a practice.

---

## SET B — 12 priced factors in three blocks, plus separate mechanisms

**Block I — Displacement** (fair value, predominantly secular):

- **F1 Latency Collapse** — reduction in the socially normal interval between initiating an exchange and completing it.
- **F2 Functional Substitution** — degree to which a non-human process produces a recognizable output at a fidelity the recipient accepts.
- **F3 Predictive Pre-selection** — degree to which outcomes, matches and encounters are forecast, ranked, filtered or pre-selected.
- **F4 Coordination Formalization** — degree to which human contact requires prior mutual scheduling, availability signalling or explicit consent.
- **F5 Dematerialization** — substitution of a physical carrier or embodied co-presence by a non-physical equivalent.

**Block II — Conditions** (fair value, mixed secular and cyclical):

- **F6 Attentional Slack** — quantity of uncommitted, non-monetized, sustainably continuous attention available to the median participant.
- **F7 Spatial Proximity Structure** — mean physical distance and access cost between socially connected people and shared public space.
- **F8 Tie Density** — number and strength of durable, non-professional, geographically proximate ties.
- **F9 Generalized Trust** — willingness to accept unmonitored exposure to strangers and unverified contact.
- **F10 Commodification / Enclosure** — degree to which a practice becomes a priced, platform-intermediated service with a rent-extracting counterparty.

**Block III — Valuation regime** (multiple, predominantly cyclical):

- **F11 Friction Premium** — current willingness to pay a premium for practices requiring effort, patience, skill or irreversible commitment.
- **F12 Status Coding** — how the practice currently reads as a social signal.

Outside the priced set: **F13 Positioning / Consensus Crowding**, carried as a price-only security-level state variable rather than a priced factor; and four security-level mechanisms — Infrastructure Viability with threshold effects, a Scarcity transform, Cultural Memory, and Replacement Resistance / Reversibility.

Note carried by this set: F4 and F9 are declared but run unpriced initially, on the stated ground that their support in an eleven-security universe is too thin.

## SET C — 5 factors

No grouping stated.

- **FE Friction Elimination** — systemic design removing temporal delay, logistical effort, and unpredictability from human action.
- **AD Agency Displacement** — human decision-making, creativity, or presence replaced by algorithmic or automated proxies.
- **MD Material Decoupling** — shift from atomic, embodied, location-bound interaction to dematerialized or remote equivalents.
- **SR Social Revaluation** — change in social status, perceived authenticity, or cultural prestige, independent of functional utility.
- **AS Attention Scarcity** — competitive pressure on cognitive bandwidth, and the resulting premium on low-friction or deeply immersive experience.

Note carried by this set: Friction / Inconvenience Premium is deliberately demoted to a security-level fundamental rather than priced as a factor.

---

## SET D — 9 factors

No grouping stated.

- **F1 Temporal Compression** — systematic reduction in the interval between desire, initiation, and completion.
- **F2 Human Act Substitution** — human performance replaced by non-human agents while preserving functional output.
- **F3 Intermediation Density** — fraction of human contact passing through an algorithmic or platform layer.
- **F4 Uncertainty Suppression** — reduction in residual chance, ambiguity, and open-endedness via prediction and pre-selection.
- **F5 Physical Carrier Displacement** — dematerialized or remote equivalents substituted for physical carriers and embodied co-presence.
- **F6 Coordination Formalization** — spontaneous or loosely synchronized contact replaced by explicit scheduling and optimized matching.
- **F7 Friction Valuation** — current willingness to pay a premium, or extract a discount, for practices imposing effort or inconvenience.
- **F8 Scarcity Revaluation** — change in unit value attached to remaining instances as prevalence declines, defined net of the quantity effect.
- **F9 Social Legibility** — net social interpretation of the practice as authentic, intimate, elite, nostalgic, performative, or obsolete.

---

## SET E — 12 factors in three layers

**Layer 1, Structural** (secular, slow):

- **S1 Time Compression** — latency between human intention and outcome eliminated across the social environment.
- **S2 Human Substitution** — expressive or performative content of a human act reproduced by non-human means.
- **S3 Mediation** — human contact intermediated by a third-party system controlling access, discovery, routing or representation.
- **S4 Uncertainty Compression** — outcomes and encounters pre-determined, predicted or optimized before the human act occurs.

**Layer 2, Experiential** (medium-speed, cultural-economic):

- **E1 Friction Premium** — current willingness to reward or penalize practices requiring effort, skill, patience or commitment.
- **E2 Physicality Premium** — current valuation of embodied, material, physically present experience relative to remote equivalents.
- **E3 Social Legibility** — degree to which a practice confers positive or negative social interpretation on its practitioner.
- **E4 Economic Time Scarcity** — degree to which discretionary time is experienced as insufficient because of labour market conditions.
- **E5 Demographic Cohort Replacement** — younger cohorts entering with different baseline relationships to practices.
- **E6 Attention Scarcity** — fragmentation of attention by competing demands; average duration of uninterrupted attention.
- **E7 Social / Institutional Trust** — trust in strangers and institutions sufficient to engage in practices requiring vulnerability.

**Layer 3, Reflexive** (market-internal):

- **R1 Positioning / Crowding** — degree to which participants are already positioned for a particular outcome.

---

## SET F — 10 factors: structural pressures plus valuation regimes

**Structural pressures:**

- **P1 Time Compression** — supply-side removal of latency between desire, action and outcome by infrastructure and defaults.
- **P2 Attention Capture** — share of waking attention claimed by engineered engagement streams.
- **P3 Discretionary Time Poverty** — economic and calendar scarcity of unhurried hours: work intensity, commuting, care burden, precarity. Labelled a hybrid.
- **P4 Human Substitution** — capability and economic incentive to produce the output without a human performer.
- **P5 Mediation & Extraction** — intensity of commercial intermediary layers: ranking, tracking, surveillance, data extraction.
- **P6 Uncertainty Compression** — systematic removal of chance, ambiguity and unsolved search.
- **P7 Encounter Erosion** — rising cost of unscheduled human and worldly contact: distance, third places, rent, scheduling norms, relational density, trust.
- **P8 Materiality & Permanence Displacement** — physical carriers and irreversible artifacts replaced by dematerialized, editable equivalents.

**Valuation regimes** (demand-side, mean-reverting):

- **P9 Patience Regime** — demand-side discount rate on slow, delayed, effortful rewards.
- **P10 Authenticity & Status Cycle** — whether effort, rarity, analog-ness and intimacy read as authentic and high-status, or as obsolete.

# 6. Required output

Answer in exactly this structure.

## Section 1 — Failure profile, one per set, six times

Use an identical template for each set, so the six can be laid side by side. For SET X:

**(a) Mispricing.** Name at least two of the eleven securities this set prices wrongly. Say in which direction. Say which factor is doing the damage, or which missing factor lets it happen.

**(b) Unattributable events.** Give at least two concrete events that could plausibly occur in this world and that this set cannot cleanly attribute — the move would land in a residual, or split across factors in a way that misdescribes the cause.

**(c) Identification failure at eleven securities.** Name any factors whose exposure vectors cannot be separated across these eleven securities, and say which pairs or groups collapse.

**(d) Double counting.** Name any place where one real-world cause enters the ledger twice.

**(e) What this set can never say.** State one true thing about these practices that this set structurally cannot express.

**(f) Falsification.** Of the standard tests — film photography must be able to revive; at least one structural winner must emerge; collinearity must stay bounded; attribution must sum exactly; the model must sometimes contradict the artist — name which this set fails or is at risk on, and why.

## Section 2 — Shared versus specific failure

Across the six profiles, separate the failures that appear in every set — properties of the problem, not of any set — from the failures specific to particular sets. This is the section that tells the reader what no choice of set can fix.

## Section 3 — Failure cost under this work's purpose

LONGING is an artwork, not a fund. Some statistical failures are artistically tolerable and some artistic failures are fatal. For each failure class identified above, say which it is, and why. Be explicit that a failure being statistically severe does not make it artistically severe, and the reverse.

## Section 4 — Minimal repair

For each set, name the single smallest change that removes its worst failure, and state what that change costs. One change per set. Say what each change breaks or complicates elsewhere in the set. This is the only section in which a modification may be proposed.

## Section 5 — What would settle it

Describe what evidence, simulation, or test would discriminate between these sets — something that could actually be run, not an appeal to judgment.

## Section 6 — Refusals

State anything you declined to answer, and why. If you believe the framing of this round is itself wrong, say so here.
