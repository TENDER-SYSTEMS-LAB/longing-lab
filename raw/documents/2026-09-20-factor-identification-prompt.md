# LONGING — Factor Identification Round

Round 4.

Purpose: determine what a three-market universe can and cannot identify, and derive the largest defensible factor set from that, rather than from preference.

---

# 1. What this round is for

Three rounds have run on the factor question and none produced a set.

Round 1 reviewed a first market model and said: reduce. Round 2 asked whether complexity could return once causally identified, and six reviewers returned six irreconcilable sets, from five factors to twelve. Round 3 forbade advocacy and mapped what each set costs. Eight reviewers produced eight failure profiles. Still no set.

The pattern is clear: asking which factors are *right* produces one more opinion per round.

Two things have changed since, and together they make a different question askable.

**The universe tripled.** The same practices are now listed separately in three markets — Japan, Korea, the United States. Eleven practices become thirty-three securities. Round 3's sharpest objection was that eleven constituents cannot distinguish a common shock from a security-specific one; one reviewer put it as *a factor supported on two constituents is not a macro factor, it is a security-specific effect with ambitions.*

**The markets are not copies.** A practice dies at a different speed in each country, because the technologies that created and then replaced it arrived years apart there. Early in the history the three markets are weakly correlated; late in it they move as one.

That makes **identification** an answerable question rather than a matter of taste. A factor is identifiable if the data this universe generates can distinguish it from every other factor and from idiosyncratic noise. That is a property of the design, not of the reviewer.

So this round asks: **what can this universe identify?**

# 3. Background

**The work.** A fictional independent research institution covering human practices and conditions whose value is changing — handwritten letters, unplanned calls, unarranged visits, waiting without contact, solitude, boredom, film photography, physical media, handmade objects, aimless night walks, and private reflection. Its surface is a dry financial research terminal: indices, quotes, analyst reports, an attribution ledger. It publishes numbers and never argues.

**Span.** Thirty years, 1996 to 2026, weekly strikes with monthly formal research.

**Markets.** Three — Japan, Korea, the United States. The same eleven practices list separately in each, with their own float, their own fundamental, and their own events. Thirty-three securities.

**Numeraire.** All three quote against one unit, STANDARD RETURN: a fixed basket of AI-mediated acts valued by what it hands back in spared time and spared effort. The unit appreciates as tools improve, so a practice can fall in quoted terms while being unchanged in itself. A companion index quoted at a frozen yardstick is published beside the headline.

**What drives the world.** A stack of technology waves — dial-up, mobile telephony, broadband, the social web, smartphones, messaging, AI. Each wave has an arrival phase that creates new ways to reach a person, and a later substitution phase that lets a person manage without one. Arrival raises a practice before substitution takes it. Arrival dates differ by market early in the history and coincide late in it.

**What is outstanding.** Each security carries a float: the total attention people have placed in each other through that practice, in that market. It is a balance, not a count. It grows by issuance and falls when someone redeems early because they can now manage alone.

**The ledger.** Each week the institute publishes an attribution of that week's move: a line for the numeraire's revision, a line for substitution exposure, one line per priced factor, a line for positioning, a line for the reflexive component, and `Unexplained`. The ledger opens near **nine** factor lines and the number grows as the universe grows. Which nine has never been decided. That is what this round exists to inform.

**Two positions held by the work's author**, carried into this round as stated context and not open to challenge here:

1. **Factors may outnumber securities.** The binding constraint is whether the listed universe can express a factor, not how the two counts compare. A working ratio of one factor per three securities was explicitly rejected.
2. **Listing choice determines which factors can exist**, rather than the reverse. Which practices are listed is a precondition for which factors are available, not a consequence of them.

# 5. Required output

## Section 1 — The identification frontier

Given thirty-three securities across three markets, eleven practices each, with weekly observations over roughly 1,565 periods:

State the maximum number of factors that can be separately identified, and the conditions under which that maximum holds. Distinguish clearly between:

- factors identified by **cross-sectional** variation within one market
- factors identified by **cross-market** variation in the same practice
- factors identified only by **time-series** variation and therefore confounded with anything else that moves slowly

Say which of the three is the binding constraint here, and why.

## Section 2 — What the three-market structure buys, exactly

The design claims that three markets make local and common factors structurally separable. Test that claim.

State the conditions under which it is true. State the conditions under which it fails — in particular, what happens to identification as the markets converge, since cross-market return correlation in this world runs from roughly +0.2 early to +0.9 late.

If convergence destroys the identification that early divergence provides, say so plainly and state when it does.

## Section 3 — The nine lines

Derive, from Sections 1 and 2 rather than from taste, a set of factor lines the opening ledger can support.

For each line: what it is, what observable distinguishes it from every other line, which securities load on it and with what sign, and what would make it unidentifiable.

If the defensible number is not nine, say what it is and why. If different numbers are defensible early and late in the history, give both and say when the change occurs.

## Section 4 — The promotion schedule

The ledger's line count is a function of universe size, not a constant. Specify the rule: what must be true before a declared-but-unpriced factor is promoted to a priced line.

State it as a test that could be run, not as a judgement call.

## Section 5 — What this universe cannot identify

Name the factors that plausibly operate in this world and that this universe **cannot** separate, no matter how the model is specified. For each, say what it is confounded with, and what change to the listing — not to the model — would separate it.

This section is the direct consequence of the author's second position, that listing determines which factors can exist. Treat it as the most important section in this round.

## Section 6 — Double counting

The numeraire, the substitution channel, and the float all derive from the same underlying capability series. Identify every place where a proposed factor line would count the same movement twice, and state how to prevent it.

## Section 7 — What would settle it

Describe a test, simulation, or estimation procedure that would discriminate between candidate factor sets using data this world can generate. It must be something that could actually be run.

## Section 8 — Refusals

State anything you declined to answer and why. If you believe this round's framing is wrong, say so here, and only here.
