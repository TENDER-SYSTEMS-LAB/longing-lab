## Section 1 — The identification frontier

Assumptions stated because the specification is silent on them: returns (or float changes, or quoted prices against STANDARD RETURN) are treated as generated by an approximate linear factor structure plus idiosyncratic residual; identification means that there exists a linear contrast in the 33-series panel that is nonzero for one candidate factor and zero for every other candidate and for a mean-zero residual, at least in a subsample of positive length; no specific volatilities, correlations, or signal-to-noise ratios are used as if measured.

With \(N=33\) series and \(T\approx 1565\) weekly observations the covariance matrix of the panel has rank at most 33. A static exact-factor representation can therefore contain at most 32 common factors plus residuals. That bound is not the relevant one. The relevant bound is the number of *separately contrastable* economic factors given the designed sources of variation.

Three sources exist.

**Cross-sectional variation within one market.** Eleven practices share a market, a technology calendar, and a numeraire. Any factor that loads with *different signs or different ranks* across those eleven names can be isolated by a within-market contrast (long some practices, short others, market-neutral and numeraire-neutral). The maximum number of such mutually orthogonal within-market factors is 10 (eleven names minus one degree of freedom for the market-wide residual). In practice the number that remain distinguishable after imposing economic sign restrictions is smaller; the design supplies eleven distinct loadings, not eleven freely estimated ones.

**Cross-market variation in the same practice.** The same practice is listed three times. Early in the sample the three listings experience staggered arrival and staggered substitution. A factor that is common across markets can be isolated by an equal-weighted (or float-weighted) basket of the three listings; a factor that is local can be isolated by a long-short pair (or a three-name residual after removing the common component). With three markets the maximum number of mutually orthogonal *market-local* factors per practice is 2. Across eleven practices that is 22 local slots, but they are not all independent of the within-market slots already counted: a Korea-only shock to “waiting without contact” is both a local factor and a within-Korea cross-sectional factor.

**Time-series variation only.** Anything that moves all 33 names in the same direction on the same calendar, or that moves so slowly that its path is collinear with the numeraire’s appreciation or with the cumulative substitution path, cannot be separated from those two series by cross-section or by cross-market contrast. It is identified only as a residual time-series factor and is therefore confounded with every other slow common shock.

The binding constraint is the **within-market cross-section of eleven practices**, not the three-market dimension and not \(T\). Eleven names can support only a handful of *distinct loading patterns*. Three markets multiply the number of *local* versions of those patterns; they do not multiply the number of *types* of pattern. Time-series length is ample for estimating a static loading matrix once the patterns are specified; it does not create new patterns. Hence the frontier is set by how many economically distinct loading vectors the eleven practices can present, times the early-sample ability of three markets to split each vector into a common part and a local part.

A working upper bound that can be stated without inventing numbers: on the order of one common factor per distinct within-market loading pattern, plus at most two local versions of each pattern, subject to the further restriction that late-sample convergence (see Section 2) collapses the local versions. That is a design property, not a sample-size property.

## Section 2 — What the three-market structure buys, exactly

The claim that three markets make local and common factors structurally separable is true only while the markets are not copies of one another.

**Conditions under which it holds.**  
A common factor is a shock whose arrival date and whose substitution date are the same (up to a known lag) in all three markets. A local factor is a shock whose date differs by enough weeks that a time dummy for “this market, this window” is not collinear with the same dummy in the other two markets. Early in the history the specification states that arrival dates differ by years. That staggered timing supplies an exclusion restriction: the Japan listing of a practice can load on a Japan-arrival dummy while the Korea and US listings of the same practice load at zero in that window. Under that restriction the three-market panel separates a common wave from a local wave by ordinary least squares on dated dummies, or by a factor model with time-varying loadings that are allowed to switch at known technology dates. The same logic applies to substitution dates.

**Conditions under which it fails.**  
As arrival and substitution dates coincide, the exclusion restriction disappears. When cross-market return correlation moves from the early-sample regime (described as roughly +0.2) to the late-sample regime (described as roughly +0.9), a local residual and a common residual become observationally equivalent in any window that is short relative to the remaining sample. In the limit of identical calendars and correlation near 1, the three listings of a practice are three noisy observations of one series; the local-versus-common decomposition is no longer identified. Identification of *local* factors is therefore front-loaded: it is a property of the first decade-plus and decays as the three technology calendars lock together.

Convergence does not destroy identification of *common* factors; it strengthens it (three observations of the same shock). It destroys identification of *local* factors. The destruction is gradual rather than a single date; it is complete once the three markets share the same arrival and substitution windows for the waves that still have remaining substitution left to run. After that point a “Korea factor” cannot be distinguished from a Korea-overweighted common factor plus noise.

The three-market structure therefore buys a *temporary* partition of each within-market loading pattern into common and local components. The partition is available early and is not available late. That is the exact content of the design.

## Section 3 — The nine lines

The opening ledger is described as opening near nine factor lines. Sections 1 and 2 do not generate nine as a unique number; they generate a *class* of lines that the panel can support and a statement of when the class shrinks.

What the panel can support at the opening (early sample, markets still staggered):

1. **Numeraire revision.** Observable: the published change in the STANDARD RETURN basket. Distinguisher: it is the only line that is known by construction and is not estimated from the 33 securities. Every security loads negatively (quoted price falls when the yardstick appreciates), with loadings that differ only by how much of the practice is already substitutable. Unidentifiable only if the institute stops publishing the basket.

2. **Substitution exposure (common).** Observable: a dated, market-common measure of the current wave’s substitution phase (the specification supplies the wave stack). Distinguisher: loads negatively on practices that the current wave replaces and near zero on practices the wave does not replace; the same loading vector appears in all three markets once that wave has arrived everywhere. Unidentifiable if substitution dates are unknown or if every practice is equally substitutable (the listing would then have failed to create differential loadings).

3. **Arrival exposure (common).** Observable: dated arrival of a wave that *creates* a practice before later substituting it. Distinguisher: opposite sign to substitution on the same names, and present only in windows before substitution dominates. Separable from substitution because arrival and substitution are sequenced, not simultaneous, in the design. Unidentifiable if a wave’s two phases are collapsed into one dummy.

4. **Within-market practice-group factor (common across markets).** A loading pattern that splits the eleven practices into groups that the technology stack treats differently (for example practices that require a specific person’s presence versus practices that do not). Distinguisher: a contrast that is long one group and short the other *inside each market*, then averaged across markets. Sign pattern is a property of the listing, not of estimation. Unidentifiable if all eleven practices load identically on every wave (again a listing failure).

5. **Local residual of the same practice-group split.** The same contrast, formed inside one market and orthogonalized to the three-market average. Distinguisher: nonzero only in the market whose calendar is still offset. Unidentifiable after calendars lock (Section 2).

6. **Positioning.** Observable in the design as a published line; economically it is the component of float change that is not explained by issuance or by redemption forced by substitution. Distinguisher: it moves when holdings are rearranged without a change in the technology stack or the numeraire. Unidentifiable if float is not published separately from price.

7. **Reflexive component.** Specified as a ledger line. Distinguisher: it is the part of this week’s move that is a function of last week’s published attribution (or of last week’s quoted change). It is identified by the lag structure the institute itself creates. Unidentifiable if the institute does not publish the previous ledger, or if agents do not condition on it.

8. **Unexplained.** The residual after the preceding lines. It is identified as whatever is left; it is not a factor in the economic sense.

That is eight named lines plus the residual. A ninth line that the early panel can still support is a **second within-market split** provided the eleven practices present two linearly independent contrast vectors (two different groupings that technology treats differently). If they present only one, the ninth line is not separately identified and the opening ledger has seven priced lines plus unexplained. The specification does not list the eleven loading vectors, so whether one or two independent within-market splits exist is a property of the listing that is not given here; it cannot be filled in silently.

Late in the history the local line (item 5) loses identification. The opening count and the late count therefore differ by the local slots. The change occurs as the three calendars coincide for the remaining waves — described in the specification as the late period in which cross-market correlation approaches the high regime. After that date the ledger can keep the local line only as a vanishingly small residual; it cannot keep it as a priced, separately attributed factor.

No other count is derived from Sections 1 and 2. Nine is consistent with two within-market splits plus the local version of one of them, plus the six lines that are either published (numeraire, positioning, reflexive, unexplained) or dated by the wave stack (arrival, substitution). It is not uniquely implied.

## Section 4 — The promotion schedule

A declared-but-unpriced factor is promoted to a priced line when all four of the following are true in a rolling window of fixed length (the length is a design choice; the test does not require a specific number of weeks):

- **Exclusion.** There exists a dated dummy or a constructed series for the candidate such that, in a regression of the 33 returns (or float changes) on the already-priced lines plus the candidate, the candidate’s coefficient is nonzero on a nonempty subset of names and the already-priced lines’ coefficients are unchanged up to sampling error.  
- **Differential loading.** The candidate’s estimated loading vector is not a scalar multiple of any already-priced loading vector (a rank test on the stacked loading matrix).  
- **Cross-market partition, while it lasts.** If the candidate is claimed to be local, the same regression run market by market produces a significant coefficient in one market and a coefficient indistinguishable from zero in the other two, in a window that still contains staggered dates. If the candidate is claimed to be common, the three market-level coefficients are indistinguishable from each other.  
- **Stability after publication.** After the line is added to the published ledger, the unexplained series does not immediately load on the new line in a way that merely reallocates the old residual (a post-publication encompassing test). This last clause is required because the reflexive line exists; publication itself can create a factor.

The test is a sequence of linear restrictions on a panel regression that the institute can run every month when formal research is published. It does not require a judgement about importance.

## Section 5 — What this universe cannot identify

These factors can operate in the world the specification describes and cannot be separated by any model written on the 33 listed series.

- **A practice that is not listed.** Any human activity that is neither one of the eleven nor the STANDARD RETURN basket is absorbed into the numeraire, into substitution, or into unexplained. It is confounded with whatever listed practice happens to covary with it. Separation requires listing that activity as a thirty-fourth (or thirty-fifth) security in at least one market.

- **A market that is not listed.** A China-only or Europe-only technology calendar cannot be distinguished from a global wave plus noise. Confounded with the common arrival/substitution lines. Separation requires listing the same eleven practices in a fourth market whose calendar is still offset.

- **Within-practice heterogeneity that the listing treats as one security.** “Handwritten letters” as a single name cannot separate letters between intimates from letters between institutions, or letters that are kept from letters that are discarded. Those two sub-practices can have opposite loadings on the same wave. The difference is confounded with idiosyncratic residual of the single listed name. Separation requires splitting the name into two securities.

- **A slow demographic or cohort factor that is common to all three markets and all eleven practices.** Fertility, aging, urbanization, and the share of people who live alone move on the same calendar as the late, locked technology stack. They are collinear with the numeraire’s trend and with cumulative substitution. Confounded with numeraire revision and with common substitution. Separation requires either a listed security whose float is *not* attention between persons (so that the demographic loading is not mechanical) or an external dated series that is excluded from the numeraire by construction.

- **A factor that arrives and substitutes on the same date in all three markets from the first week of the sample.** No staggered exclusion restriction exists. It is confounded with the first common wave. Separation requires a market whose technology calendar is known to differ, i.e., a listing change.

- **Intent versus capability.** The specification’s float is attention placed, not attention desired. A change in how much people *want* a practice, holding capability fixed, is not separately listed. It is confounded with positioning and with the reflexive line. Separation requires a second published quantity per security that is not float and not price (for example a survey of unmet demand). That is a listing of a new object, not a new factor on the existing objects.

Each of the above is a direct consequence of the author’s second position: the listed universe determines which factors can exist. Changing the model specification does not create the missing contrast.

## Section 6 — Double counting

The numeraire, the substitution channel, and the float are transformations of one underlying capability series.

Places where a proposed line would count the same movement twice:

- **Numeraire revision and common substitution.** Both rise when tools improve. If substitution exposure is constructed from the same basket that defines STANDARD RETURN, the two lines are the same series with opposite sign conventions. Prevention: define substitution exposure as the *differential* loading across the eleven practices after the numeraire has already been removed; do not put the basket itself on the substitution line.

- **Substitution and float redemption.** Float falls when someone can manage alone. That fall is the quantity counterpart of substitution. Attributing both a substitution factor return and a float-change effect to the same week double-counts the redemption. Prevention: the substitution line attributes the *price* (or quoted-return) effect of capability; the float is used only to weight the securities and to construct the positioning residual. Issuance and redemption are not given their own priced factor line.

- **Arrival and the opposite of substitution.** If arrival is coded as “not-yet-substituted,” it is one minus the substitution dummy. Prevention: arrival is dated only in the window between a wave’s arrival and the start of that wave’s substitution phase; after substitution begins, the arrival dummy is set to zero.

- **Positioning and reflexive.** If agents reposition because last week’s ledger told them to, the two lines split one event. Prevention: positioning is the contemporaneous residual of float after issuance, redemption, and the dated technology dummies; reflexive is the coefficient on the *lagged published* attribution. They are separated by the lag the institute itself inserts.

- **Any local factor and the common factor of the same name, late in the sample.** After convergence the local line is a noisy copy of the common line. Prevention: the promotion test in Section 4 retires the local line when the cross-market partition restriction fails.

No other double-count is implied by the objects the specification names.

## Section 7 — What would settle it

A procedure that can be run on data this world generates:

Construct the 33-series panel of weekly quoted changes (and, separately, of weekly float changes). Partition the sample into an early window in which the specification says calendars are staggered and a late window in which they are not.

In each window estimate a static approximate-factor model by principal components (or by maximum likelihood) on the residuals after subtracting the published numeraire revision. Record the number of eigenvalues that exceed the Bai-Ng or Onatski threshold. That number is the statistical dimension; it is not yet an economic set.

Then impose the economic restrictions the design supplies: dated arrival dummies, dated substitution dummies, the published positioning residual, and the lagged ledger (reflexive). Estimate the restricted model by GLS or by OLS on the stacked panel. Perform the four promotion tests of Section 4 on every candidate line that is not already in the restricted model, including every local version of every common line.

Compare two nested specifications that differ by exactly one candidate line. The data discriminate if the encompassing test (or a Vuong test on the unexplained series) rejects one specification in the early window, the late window, or both. Repeat after dropping each market in turn; if dropping one market changes which line survives, that line was identified by cross-market variation and will not survive convergence.

The procedure uses only series the institute already publishes or can construct from the wave calendar it already posits. It does not require a preferred set; it reports which restrictions the panel accepts.
