## A. Critique of the thirty

* **1 (Asking a stranger the way)**: Overlaps significantly with **28 (Asking a question of strangers)** and **22 (Small talk at a counter)** under the broader umbrella of public belief and stranger interaction.
* **3 (Confiding a personal matter and asking advice)**: High overlap with **19 (Listening to someone's trouble)**; these represent two sides of the same transactional exchange (issuing vs. holding the bond).
* **6 (Choosing a gift for someone)**: Barely passes *origin* test if defined loosely; risks overlapping with **26 (Making something by hand for someone)** regarding deliberate effort versus algorithmic curation.
* **10 (Talking until late)**: Audience clarity issue—the act itself sounds like a duration rather than a structured practice; overlaps with **15 (Talking a disagreement through)** and **19 (Listening to someone's trouble)**.
* **11 (Introducing two people)**: Barely passes *issue* test for direct 2-person bond issuance; it facilitates a third-party bond, making its bond-issuance mechanism indirect compared to other practices.
* **13 (Learning a craft from a senior)**: Fails distinction test against **14 (Teaching someone by showing)**; they are mirror actions of a single pedagogical practice.
* **17 (Meeting at an agreed time without tracking)**: Fails *act vs. form* framing; "without tracking" defines the practice by the absence of a device rather than a positive human act.
* **21 (Reading aloud to someone)**: Weak coverage on bond strength for adults; carries low weight inside the work unless specifically framed around children or dependent care.
* **23 (Treating someone, "next time it's on me")**: Overlaps heavily with **24 (Lending to a friend informally)** as informal credit/reciprocity ledgers.
* **29 (Keeping a friendship at a distance)**: Meaning inside the work is too broad; it functions more as an umbrella state composed of **4**, **8**, and **9** rather than a distinct practice with steps.

## C. Coverage check

* **Thinnest Balance**: **Belief** (the conviction that people will gather again and someone will help). While *Love* and *Understanding* are heavily represented by interpersonal and emotional exchanges, *Belief* relies on broader civic or social friction, which modern conveniences rapidly eliminate.
* **Thinnest Channel**: **Restoration** (+ gives back what distance or illness took). Most technology-driven practices emphasize *Device* (− takes effort) or *Substitution* (− takes judgment).
* **Missing Domains of Human Life**:
* **Sacred/Ritual & Mortality**: Practices surrounding grief, dying, passage rites, and shared spiritual/communal commitments.
* **Physical Labor & Danger**: Shared physical exertion, collective danger, and manual interdependence (e.g., agricultural harvesting, crisis response).
* **Civic & Commercial Friction**: Local bargaining, informal community arbitration, and unstructured physical public assembly.
