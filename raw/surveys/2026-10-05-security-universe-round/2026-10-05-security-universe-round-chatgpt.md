## B. New candidates

The **N numbers are references for this round**. These remain candidates, including alternatives to broad entries in the current list.

Each loss entry identifies a difference that a record could distinguish; it does not claim that such a dataset already exists. Historical directions cover possible paths within **1811–2026**. **Hypothesis** marks an interpretation requiring investigation. **Rising in reach** means that additional human exchanges become possible; it does not establish a rise in the total frequency of the practice across all media and populations.

For every negative channel below, the specified human role or judgment must actually be displaced. Assistance with wording, coordination, access, or execution is insufficient by itself.

### Evidence and its limits

- **E1 — `verified`: cheaper post and correspondence.** London Museum records British mail increasing from 76 million letters in 1839 to 169 million in 1840. It also describes Londoners exchanging personal news and arranging meetings through the post. This supports a substantial arrival in correspondence and coordination. Total mail includes business letters; it does not establish how often people reconciled or formed bonds. [London Museum](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/penny-post-sparked-letter-writing-revolution/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

- **E2 — `verified`: communication access and restoration.** Gallaudet’s historical exhibit describes how the 1964 TTY development enabled Deaf users to contact friends and clubs that also had the equipment. ASHA describes communication supports that help people express themselves and make their own decisions, including devices used after loss of voice. These establish concrete routes for human exchange. Whether a particular case is arrival or restoration depends on what illness or distance had previously removed. [Gallaudet University](https://gallaudet.edu/museum/exhibits/history-through-deaf-eyes/awareness-access-and-change/how-ttys-made-telephones-accessible-to-the-deaf/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

- **E3 — `verified`: new meetings can accompany the loss of an intermediary.** Rosenfeld, Thomas, and Hausen’s 2019 study documents the rise of meeting partners online and declining mediation by friends and family among US heterosexual couples. It supports the coexistence of an expanding route to a new pair and a shrinking intermediary role. It does not measure the total number of invitations or the aggregate balance of love. [web.stanford.edu](https://web.stanford.edu/~mrosenfe/Rosenfeld_et_al_Disintermediating_Friends.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

- **E4 — `verified`: people finding peers with similar illnesses.** Pew’s 2011 research combines a national US survey with accounts from members of the National Organization for Rare Disorders. It describes internet-enabled routes for patients to find and help one another. This supports new occasions for N12; it does not establish a worldwide historical trend or make every information exchange a bond. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2011/02/28/peer-to-peer-health/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

- **E5 — `verified`: human assistance and an artificial alternative within one service.** Be My Eyes’ March 2023 announcement describes its human volunteer service, founded in 2015, and an AI feature for answering questions about images. This is a company source verifying the offered channels. It does not demonstrate a decline in human calls, and volunteer registrations are not completed exchanges. [bemyeyes.com](https://www.bemyeyes.com/news/introducing-be-my-ai-formerly-virtual-volunteer-for-people-who-are-blind-or-have-low-vision-powered-by-openais-gpt-4/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

- **E6 — `verified`: networked play as a place for human relationships.** Pew’s 2015 US teen research documents play with existing friends, online-only friends, and other people. It also describes how networked games removed the need to occupy the same room. This supports a substantial new domain of interpersonal play, with limits on age, geography, and the period observed. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2015/08/06/chapter-3-video-games-are-key-elements-in-friendships-for-many-boys/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

- **E7 — `verified`: historical opportunities for music together; historical concern about replacement.** The Musical Instrument Museum describes nineteenth-century US family ensembles, less expensive domestic pianos, and mail-order instruments. The Library of Congress documents Sousa’s 1906 argument that mechanical music threatened human musical activity. The first supports expanded opportunities; the second verifies an articulated fear of loss. Neither establishes that recordings caused a measured decline in joint music-making. [Musical Instrument Museum](https://mim.org/music-at-home-100-years-ago/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## C. Coverage check

### Balances

**Belief remains the thinnest balance in its specific meaning of expecting people to return and help.** There are many initial encounters and invitations. There are fewer distinct practices whose central act establishes what happens after the first meeting, after an absence, or when helping becomes inconvenient.

| Balance | Coverage in the combined list | Remaining weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Love** | Gifts, affectionate contact, celebration, companionship, prayer, and relief for carers provide several different routes. | Sustained family responsibilities and intimate negotiation are less explicit than expression and support. |
| **Understanding** | Confiding, instruction, peer inquiry, interpretation, joint creation, and examining shared loss provide broad coverage. | Many records could still collapse into “having a conversation” unless the distinct steps remain visible. |
| **Trust** | Appointments, informal lending, work handovers, vouching, representation, mediation, and commercial agreements provide varied routes. | Trust across substantial differences in power or background remains less developed than trust among peers and familiar people. |
| **Belief** | Neighbourly help, check-ins, a continuing welcome, recruitment, and hospitality provide several carriers. | Initial help and admission are better represented than dependable return, renewed participation, and continued help over time. |

Religious conviction does not fill the belief column by itself. N24 feeds the stated balance only insofar as a person expects another human to remain available or return; its religious content is not sufficient.

### Channels

**Restoration remains the thinnest channel in the diversity of its mechanisms.** N7, N8, N10, and N24 make it explicit, alongside existing communication and care practices. Much of this coverage still depends on restoring communication across distance or after speech loss.

Arrival has several distinct routes through correspondence, new encounters, peer support, play, recruitment, and hospitality. Device exposure appears in monitoring, automated handovers, accompaniment, configuration, and repair. Substitution of judgment appears across advice, review, representation, mediation, interpretation, and creative partnership.

Restoration through mobility, rehabilitation, and assistance with participating in shared work remains less developed. Merely attaching the word “accessible” to a practice would not resolve this: a particular act must return after illness or distance removed it.

The combined list contains credible positive histories. It does not establish that each proposed practice has increased overall. The historical evidence is strongest for particular communication expansions and newly reachable exchanges; several additional “rising” entries remain hypotheses.

### Human life still missing or lightly represented

| Area | Remaining gap |
|---|---|
| **Parenting and household life** | Raising children, negotiating household responsibilities, and changing relations between adult children and ageing parents are mostly implicit within broad care and conversation categories. |
| **Intimacy and separation** | Negotiating desire, boundaries, commitment, and the ending of a relationship remain lightly represented. Asking for a date covers only an opening. |
| **Agriculture and shared material work** | Working land together, coordinating a harvest, maintaining shared resources, and collective building are largely absent. Work additions lean toward organisations, services, and knowledge work. |
| **Civic disagreement across unfamiliar groups** | Mediation and representation are present, but repeated cooperation with people outside one’s familiar social circle remains thin. A common cause does not necessarily supply that difference. |
| **Religious and ceremonial life** | Shared prayer appears, while mentoring in faith, preparing a rite for another person, and responsibility across generations remain largely implicit. Attendance alone would not fill the gap. |
| **Childhood and later life as active participation** | Play and care can include these stages, but the list does not yet make children’s or older people’s contributions as helpers, teachers, and organisers especially visible. |
| **Cultural and historical variation** | The verified historical anchors are concentrated in Britain and the United States. They do not establish the same sequence elsewhere, or across different classes and household arrangements. |

The fifth loss layer, **the sense of loss itself**, also remains thin. N13 addresses it directly. Fewer letters, visits, or games cannot establish that people have stopped noticing their absence; that is a different comparison from the decline of the acts.
