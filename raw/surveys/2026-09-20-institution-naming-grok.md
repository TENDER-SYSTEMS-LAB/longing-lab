**Limits, stated once.** This is not a trademark, domain, or exchange-code search. Judgements rest on web-visible entities (newspaper lists, agency histories, index/ticker pages), naming-convention inference, and explicit gaps. A name marked *unknown, needs a register search* is usable. A name asserted clear that later turns out to be a real paper is not.

## Section 1 — The Japanese publisher

Eight candidates. Script / romanisation / gloss; one line on form; collision report.

### 1. 商況日報 — *Shōkyō Nippō* — Commercial-Conditions Daily
Plausible: 日報 is a live newspaper suffix (岩手日報, 新潟日報); 商況 is the Meiji market-conditions word used by the house that became Nikkei (商況社).

- **Exists?** No paper of this exact title found on current or defunct Japanese newspaper lists. 商況社 *did* exist: it published 中外商業新報, Nikkei’s ancestor. That is lineage association, not title collision.
- **TM:** Unknown. Classes 16 (print) and 36/41 (financial info / publication) would matter in Japan. 商況 is descriptive; distinctiveness of the pair would be the issue.
- **Domain:** `shokyo-nippo.co.jp` / `.jp` — not checked live; likely open or parked. Low public profile.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high on “no living paper.” Residual risk is the 商況社 historical echo. Basis: Japanese newspaper lists and Nikkei corporate history.

### 2. 帝都経済新聞 — *Teito Keizai Shimbun* — Imperial-Capital Economic Newspaper
Plausible: 帝都 was the official designation of Tokyo into the early 1940s and survived in firm names; X経済新聞 is the standard economic-paper form.

- **Exists?** Exact title not found. 帝国データバンク (Teikoku Databank) is a different word and a real, prominent credit bureau — do not shorten this paper to 帝経 in a way that collides with 帝国.
- **TM:** Unknown. 帝都 is less common now; that helps distinctiveness and dates the house.
- **Domain:** `teito-keizai.co.jp` — unknown.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high on title vacancy. Basis: no hit on newspaper lists; 帝国データバンク is the nearby occupied mark.

### 3. 京浜経済新聞 — *Keihin Keizai Shimbun* — Keihin Economic Newspaper
Plausible: block-paper geography. 中部経済新聞 is a real regional economic daily; this is the Kantō-belt analogue that does not appear to exist.

- **Exists?** Exact title not found. 京浜東北線 is a JR line — geographic, not a publisher. Post-2000 local sites in the みんなの経済新聞 network use *[place] Keizai Shimbun*; 京浜経済新聞 was not among those found.
- **TM:** Unknown. 京浜 is geographic and weak alone; the full four-character-plus-新聞 string is the mark.
- **Domain:** `keihin-keizai.co.jp` — unknown.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Basis: newspaper lists + Minkei member list; not a register search.

### 4. 東京市況新報 — *Tōkyō Shikyō Shimpō* — Tokyo Market-Conditions Gazette
Plausible: 市況 = quotations; 新報 is the older gazette form (時事新報, 東洋経済新報).

- **Exists?** Exact string not found. Morphologically adjacent to 中外物価新報 / 中外商業新報 (Nikkei line, 1876–1942). That is the risk.
- **TM:** Unknown. High chance a clearance lawyer would flag proximity to the Nikkei ancestral titles even if the string is free.
- **Domain:** unknown.
- **Confidence:** Medium on vacancy; **high on “too close to Nikkei’s dead names.”** Keep out of the first-choice tier.

### 5. 本邦商業新報 — *Honpō Shōgyō Shimpō* — Domestic Commercial Gazette
Plausible: 本邦 is formal “this country”; 商業新報 is the exact prewar economic-paper pattern.

- **Exists?** Exact title not found. 本邦 appears in many corporate and legal phrases; 商業新報 as a standalone tail is historically loaded (see Nikkei ancestor).
- **TM:** Unknown. Descriptive compound; registrability in Japan may be thin.
- **Domain:** unknown.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Same ancestral-form risk as (4), slightly lower because 本邦 is not 中外.

### 6. 通商日報 — *Tsūshō Nippō* — Commerce Daily
Plausible: 通商 is ministry-register Japanese (通商産業); 日報 as above.

- **Exists?** No Japanese national paper found. 通商 / 通商日報 strings appear in Chinese-language and Taiwan trade contexts — cross-language occupation, not a Tokyo evening paper, but not clean.
- **TM:** Unknown in JP/KR/US/EU. Class 16/35/36.
- **Domain:** `tusho-nippo` variants — unknown; CJK domains may be taken in TW/CN.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low. Prefer a Japanese-only string.

### 7. 東都商報 — *Tōto Shōhō* — Eastern-Capital Commercial Report
Plausible: 東都 is a literary name for Tokyo; 商報 is a short trade-sheet suffix.

- **Exists?** Exact Japanese paper not found. 商報 is heavily occupied in Chinese (香港商報 and others). Weak as an international mark.
- **TM / domain:** Occupied risk in Chinese-language classes and `.hk` / `.cn`.
- **Confidence:** Low-medium. Discard for international use; usable only as a domestic short title.

### 8. 公示経済新聞 — *Kōji Keizai Shimbun* — Public-Notice Economic Newspaper
Plausible: 公示 is the legal-notice word; dry, institutional, not clever.

- **Exists?** Exact title not found. 官報 is the real official gazette — different word, same semantic neighbourhood. A reader could misfile this as a government sheet.
- **TM:** 公示 is descriptive. Weak mark.
- **Domain:** unknown.
- **Confidence:** Medium on vacancy; **high on “sounds like the Official Gazette.”** Second-tier only.

**Recommended publisher tier:** 商況日報, 帝都経済新聞, 京浜経済新聞.  
**Usable with caution:** 本邦商業新報.  
**Not first-choice:** 東京市況新報, 通商日報, 東都商報, 公示経済新聞.

## Section 2 — The United States ratings house

Two-surname form. Period surnames. Pair-level collision first; then whether either name evokes a known house.

### 1. Colton & Reeve
- **Exists?** No ratings house, index, or prominent financial firm under the pair. Isolated people named Colton Reeve / Colton Langford exist; not a house.
- **Evocation:** Neither name is Standard, Poor, Dow, Jones, Moody, Fitch, Dun, Bradstreet, Duff, Phelps.
- **TM:** Unknown. USPTO classes 36 and 42 would matter. Pair is distinctive enough to be searchable.
- **Domain:** `coltonreeve.com` / `coltonandreeve.com` — unknown.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high on “no famous entity.” Basis: web search of the pair + known NRSRO list.

### 2. Garrison & Holt
- **Exists?** Pair not found as a ratings or index house. Holt & Langford is a UK lettings firm (different order, different trade). Garrison is a common surname and a military word.
- **Evocation:** Clean of the Big Three. Holt is common; not a problem at pair level.
- **TM / domain:** Unknown.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.

### 3. Prentice & Ward
- **Exists?** Pair not found. **Prentice-Hall** is a major publisher (now under Pearson/others) — first-name evocation, not the pair. Montgomery Ward is a dead retailer — second-name evocation only.
- **Evocation:** Mild, via Prentice-Hall. Flag it.
- **TM / domain:** Unknown. Prentice-Hall’s residual marks are in class 16/41, not 36, but a clearance search should still run.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### 4. Osgood & Hale
- **Exists?** Pair not found as a firm of standing. Hale is everywhere (including clothing/scam sites using Hale). Osgood is New England period stock.
- **Evocation:** Clean of ratings houses.
- **TM / domain:** Hale alone is crowded; the pair is the mark.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### 5. Langford & Ellis
- **Exists?** Pair not found as ratings. Nearby: Langford Rae and Langford Russell (UK estate agents); Whitaker Ellis (Oregon concrete, 1997); Brackett & Ellis (Texas law).
- **Evocation:** Ellis is common in law-firm names. Not a ratings collision.
- **TM / domain:** Nearby Langford-* firms make EU/UK class 36 noisier than US class 36.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### 6. Whitaker & Crane
- **Exists?** Pair not found. Whitaker & Warburton (US immigration law, 2025); Whitaker Ellis (concrete); Crane Co. is a real industrial company.
- **Evocation:** Crane is the problem — a listed industrial name. Do not use if the house will appear next to equity tickers.
- **Confidence:** Medium on pair vacancy; **reject from recommended tier because of Crane Co.**

### 7. Benton & Carr
- **Exists?** Pair not found. Both surnames are common; Carr appears in many law firms.
- **Evocation:** Low for ratings; high generic-surname risk (weak mark).
- **Confidence:** Medium-low on protectability.

### 8. Merritt & Shaw
- **Exists?** Pair not found. Shaw is heavily occupied (Shawmut, Shaw Communications, etc.).
- **Evocation:** Shaw is the risk, same class of problem as Crane.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low. Second-tier.

### 9. Ashford & Bell
- **Exists?** Pair not found. Bell is occupied at industrial scale (AT&T/Bell, Bell Labs). Ashford is a UK place and a retail brand.
- **Evocation:** Bell is disqualifying for a clean recommended list.
- **Confidence:** Pair vacant; **reject recommended tier.**

### 10. Hale & Whitmore
- **Exists?** Pair not found as ratings. Harrington & Hale / Bywhitmore are contemporary retail/scam-adjacent sites — different strings, ugly neighbourhood.
- **Evocation:** Low for ratings; messy web neighbourhood.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low.

**Recommended house tier:** Colton & Reeve, Garrison & Holt, Osgood & Hale.  
**Usable with a note:** Prentice & Ward (Prentice-Hall), Langford & Ellis (UK estate agents).  
**Keep out of recommended:** Whitaker & Crane, Ashford & Bell, Merritt & Shaw.

## Section 3 — The three index names

### Korea — state-issued acronym (KOSPI form)

KOSPI = *Korea Composite Stock Price Index*. The Korean official name sits beside the acronym (한국종합주가지수). Same structure: expansion first, acronym as the public name.

The number of constituents, if any, is a function of how many names the issuing authority lists on the official board — KOSPI itself is a full-board composite, not a fixed count; KOSPI 200 is the fixed-count child. For this market, a full-board composite is the closer analogue. If a numbered child is needed later, the number should be the count of names that meet a liquidity-and-continuity screen, not a homage.

| Acronym | Expansion | Collision |
|---|---|---|
| **KONCI** | Korea National Composite Index | No Korean benchmark found. KONIX was a late-1980s UK games hardware firm — different string, dead. |
| **KOCTI** | Korea Composite Transaction Index | No index found. CTI is a generic trigram (many firms). Full five-letter string is the mark. |
| **KOCMI** | Korea Composite Market Index | CMI is a crowded trigram. Full string not found as a Korean official index. |
| **KORXI** | Korea Reference Index | Looks invented; weaker “state issued” feel than KONCI/KOCTI. |
| **KOCPI** | Korea Official Composite Price Index | **One letter from KOSPI.** Confusion risk. Do not use. |

Korean script for the recommended expansion:

- KONCI — 한국국가종합지수 (*Hanguk Gukga Jonghap Jisu*) — National Composite Index  
- KOCTI — 한국종합거래지수 (*Hanguk Jonghap Georae Jisu*) — Composite Transaction Index  

**Pick:** KONCI as headline (state voice), KOCTI as alternate (market-operations voice). Both need a KIPO / KRX code search before use.

### Japan — `<publisher> Average`

Nikkei form: publisher short name + 平均 / Average, with an optional constituent count (225). The count is a function of how many liquid names the paper elects to keep in a price-average basket so the series is continuous. 225 is taken. For a thinner listed market, a smaller fixed basket is the honest 1996-period choice: **100** or **120**, not 225, not 500.

Tied to Section 1 recommendations:

| Publisher | Index | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 商況日報 | **商況平均** / Shokyo Average; optional **商況100** | Short name is two morae; works like 日経. |
| 帝都経済新聞 | **帝都平均** / Teito Average; optional **帝都100** | Short, dated, institutional. Watch 帝国データバンク when abbreviated. |
| 京浜経済新聞 | **京浜平均** / Keihin Average; optional **京浜120** | Geographic short name; 京浜東北線 is the nearby occupied phrase. |

Do not use 日経平均, 東証平均, or “Dow” in the Japanese title (Nikkei did, 1975–1985, and then dropped it).

### United States — `<ratings house> <number>`

S&P form: house + constituent count. The number is a function of how many names the house rates into the headline average. S&P 500 / 100 / 400 and Dow 30 are taken as *conventions* even where the exact house-plus-number string is free. Do not use 30, 100, 400, or 500 if the point is to look like a 1996 house that is not pretending to be those series. **80** or **120** is the right magnitude for a specialist average.

| House | Index | Collision |
|---|---|---|
| Colton & Reeve | **Colton & Reeve 80** | String not found as an index. |
| Garrison & Holt | **Garrison & Holt 120** | String not found. |
| Osgood & Hale | **Osgood & Hale 80** | String not found. |

A mid-cap companion, if needed later, takes a different number from the same function (e.g. 80 headline, 250 broad) — not 400.

## Section 6 — Refusals and residual risk

**Could not check**
- Live registers: JPO, KIPO, USPTO, EUIPO, WIPO Madrid.
- Full WHOIS for every obvious domain (`.co.jp`, `.jp`, `.co.kr`, `.com`, `.net`).
- Bloomberg/Reuters/KRX/TSE/NYSE/NASDAQ mnemonic tables beyond the public headline codes above.
- Dead paper titles that never reached Wikipedia or the 日本の新聞一覧 pages.
- Common-law unregistered use in the US.
- Fictional entities in unindexed print.

**Needs a professional clearance search before use**
- Every recommended publisher title in Japan, class 16 and 36/41.
- Colton & Reeve, Garrison & Holt, Osgood & Hale in USPTO class 36 (and EUIPO if the composite will be printed in Europe).
- KONCI / KOCTI at KIPO and as a KRX index code.
- LGXC and the three national tickers against terminal vendors.
- 帝都平均 specifically against 帝国データバンク and any 帝* financial marks.

**Low enough risk to proceed on, as working names inside the work**
- 商況日報 → 商況平均 / SKYO  
- 帝都経済新聞 → 帝都平均 / TEIT  
- Colton & Reeve 80 / CR80  
- KONCI / KONC  
- LONGING COMPOSITE / LGXC  

These are the ones that look like 1996 institutions, do not wink, and did not turn up as living papers, NRSROs, or headline indices on a public search. They are not cleared. They are the shortlist to take to a clearance search.
