**A. What modernization took before AI**

The following synthesizes waves of modernization from the Luddites (~1811) to roughly 2020. Each entry notes approximate dates, what it gave, what it took or changed (mapped where possible to the author’s terms: personal judgment/reflection, mutual exchange/bonds, the four balances of love/understanding/trust/belief, gathering, inefficiency/friction, time, sense of loss), key sources with status, and strength of evidence. Credit’s evolution is treated separately at the end.

**Credit’s change (personal networks → institutional/impersonal)**

Pre-modern and early-modern credit was heavily interpersonal: reputation, reciprocity, kinship, neighbourhood standing, and ongoing mutual obligations determined access. Standing and trustworthiness were assessed through social knowledge, not abstract scores. (Verified: historical work on early modern Europe and colonial America; CEPR synthesis on embedded credit systems.)

From the mid-19th century, mercantile agencies (e.g., Lewis Tappan’s Mercantile Agency, 1841) and later consumer credit bureaux abstracted reputation into reports and ordinal ratings. By the 20th century (especially post-computerisation), institutional credit scoring became dominant. Trust shifted from person-to-person bonds and mutual knowledge to impersonal institutional mediation and data. This reduced reliance on (and practice of) personal judgment of character and reciprocal personal credit networks, while expanding scale and reducing certain local frictions. (Verified: Josh Lauer on credit reporting; Bruce Carruthers on the “economy of promises”; Warwick working papers on 19th-century bank trust.)

**B. The author’s view against published thought**

For each of the eight points: supporting works, complicating/contradicting works, and the strongest careful objection. Then overall gaps.

1. **Origin in personal judgment/reflection (not “the AI said…”)**  
   Supports: Borgmann (focal practices require skilled engagement, not device consumption); Turkle (technology can substitute for reflective conversation); Weber (rationalisation replaces traditional/charismatic judgment with calculable rules).  
   Complicates: McLuhan (the medium itself reshapes judgment; content is secondary); some cognitive-offloading literature that finds net gains when tools free capacity for higher reflection.  
   Strongest objection: Personal judgment has always been socially scaffolded (language, tradition, tools); pure origin in “one’s own” reflection is an idealisation that understates distributed cognition.

2. **Exchange as mutual bonds / owing each other**  
   Supports: Reciprocity literature (Mauss *The Gift* tradition; Putnam on norms of reciprocity); historical personal credit as mutual obligation.  
   Complicates: Modern large-scale cooperation often relies on impersonal institutions and generalised trust rather than dyadic debts (North, institutional economics).  
   Strongest objection: Mutual bonds can become burdensome or exclusionary; impersonal systems enabled larger, more inclusive cooperation.

3. **Balances (love, understanding, trust, belief) decreasing via light facts/rumours rather than rising hatred**  
   Supports: Putnam (declining trust and social capital); some social-media studies on shallow information and reduced trust; Gerbner “mean world.”  
   Complicates: Evidence on trust trends is mixed by country and measure; some data show stable or rising generalised trust in certain periods/places; hatred/misunderstanding also rise in polarised media environments.  
   Strongest objection: The primary empirical story may be polarisation and affective hostility more than simple depletion of positive balances; “light facts” can also spread accurate information rapidly.

4. **What is human: doing, doing-with-persons; mistakes/inefficiency romantic; efficiency not**  
   Supports: Borgmann (devices conceal engagement; focal things restore “good burdens”); Rosa (resonance vs. acceleration/alienation); Thompson (loss of task-oriented time); Simmel (blasé attitude as protective efficiency in metropolis).  
   Complicates: Efficiency has enabled leisure, health, and new forms of human activity; many romantic traditions (Romanticism itself) coexisted with or reacted to industrial efficiency.  
   Strongest objection: Romanticising inefficiency risks aestheticising hardship or excluding those who gain from efficiency (e.g., reduced domestic labour, mobility for the disabled or remote).

5. **AI test: gather vs. scatter; grow critical thought vs. replace; time saved spent with people can increase romance**  
   Supports: Turkle (technology can isolate while promising connection); Haidt (phone-based childhood reduces in-person gathering); Rosa (acceleration reduces resonance).  
   Complicates: Digital tools have enabled new gatherings (diaspora communities, specialised interest groups); time-use data sometimes show complementarity rather than pure substitution.  
   Strongest objection: The net effect is empirical and contingent on design and use; “gathering” can occur online, and offline gathering has declined for many non-tech reasons (work hours, urban form, dual careers).

6. **Drift through comfort (Circe / algorithmic feeds)**  
   Supports: Postman (amusing ourselves); Rosa (dynamic stabilisation through escalation); comfort literature on persuasive design.  
   Complicates: Comfort and personalisation can free attention for valued activities; users often exercise agency.  
   Strongest objection: “Drift” implies passivity; many users actively curate or resist; historical analogues (novels, radio) also produced moral panics about passive consumption that proved overstated.

7. **Gathering, deliberating, going back-and-forth made people human; declined with solitary AI work**  
   Supports: Arendt (action in the public realm of plurality); Habermas (communicative action); Putnam (decline of face-to-face association); historical accounts of agora, salon, coffee-house.  
   Complicates: Solitary reflection has deep philosophical value (Montaigne, Descartes); digital tools enable asynchronous deliberation at scale.  
   Strongest objection: Humanity is not solely constituted by physical co-presence; literacy and print already enabled solitary yet culturally connected thought.

8. **Market’s floor is mutual belief and credit**  
   Supports: Historical credit literature (personal trust as foundation); some economic sociology (Granovetter embeddedness).  
   Complicates: Modern markets rest heavily on formal institutions, law, and state-backed money, not primarily dyadic belief.  
   Strongest objection: Scale and complexity required impersonal mechanisms; personal mutual belief alone cannot support large markets without high transaction costs and exclusion.

**What the literature contains that the author’s view lacks**

- **Acceleration and dynamic stabilisation** (Rosa): modernity requires perpetual growth/speed merely to stay stable; resonance is the counter-concept.  
- **Device paradigm vs. focal things/practices** (Borgmann): technology’s pattern is to make commodities available without engagement; focal practices restore skilled, communal involvement.  
- **Disenchantment / iron cage** (Weber): rationalisation removes magic and ultimate values from public life, pushing them into private/mystical spheres.  
- **Blasé attitude and metropolitan intellectualisation** (Simmel): protective indifference arising from overstimulation and money economy.  
- **Social capital as networks + norms of reciprocity + trust** (Putnam): measurable decline with specific mechanisms (TV time, generational change).  
- **Medium effects independent of content** (McLuhan, Postman): the form itself reshapes cognition and discourse.  
- **Cognitive and relational deskilling** (Turkle; some AI-offloading studies): practice atrophies when outsourced.  
- **Contested empirical magnitudes**: many claimed losses (TV → social capital; smartphones → youth mental health) are real in direction but modest or confounded in size; alternative explanations (economic precarity, reduced unsupervised play, dual-earner time poverty) matter.  
- Non-Western and classical resources (gift economies, Confucian relational ethics, Aristotelian *philia* and *phronesis*) that enrich reciprocity and practical judgment beyond the eight points.  
- Positive affordances and complementarity: technologies often expand rather than simply subtract (new publics, reduced isolation for some groups).

**C. Recurring concepts (proposals for ontology)**

These abstract concepts recur across the sources and can feed an ontology of what modernization removes or transforms:

- **Reciprocity / mutual obligation** (Mauss tradition; Putnam; historical credit): bonds created by giving and owing. Sources: Putnam *Bowling Alone*; personal-credit histories.  
- **Focal practice / focal thing** (Borgmann): skilled, engaging activities and objects that gather people and demand presence, versus devices that deliver commodities without engagement.  
- **Resonance** (Rosa): responsive, transformative world-relation (horizontal/social, diagonal/material, vertical/existential) opposed to alienation under acceleration.  
- **Friction / good burden / inefficiency** (Borgmann; Thompson; author): the resistance and time that enable judgment, presence, and mutual adjustment.  
- **Gathering / plurality / public realm** (Arendt; Habermas secondary; Putnam): co-presence and deliberation among distinct persons.  
- **Disenchantment / rationalisation** (Weber): progressive removal of magic, tradition, and ultimate values by calculable efficiency.  
- **Blasé attitude / protective intellectualisation** (Simmel): emotional detachment as adaptation to overstimulation and impersonal exchange.  
- **Social capital** (Putnam): networks + reciprocity norms + trust that enable cooperation.  
- **Medium effects / form over content** (McLuhan, Postman): the channel itself reshapes scale, pace, and cognitive habits.  
- **Deskilling / cognitive offloading** (Luddite historiography; Turkle; AI studies): atrophy of judgment and skill when processes are automated or mediated.  
- **Time discipline / acceleration** (Thompson; Rosa): shift from task-oriented or resonant time to clocked, escalatory time.  
- **Aura / presence** (Benjamin): uniqueness of the here-and-now encounter diminished by technical reproducibility.
