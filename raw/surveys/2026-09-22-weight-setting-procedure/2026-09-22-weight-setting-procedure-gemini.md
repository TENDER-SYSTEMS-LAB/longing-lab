## Section 1 — The unit of classification

The unit of classification is the **canonical functional step**.

A practice cannot be classified as a monolithic whole because every human practice involves both agency (judgment) and mediation (execution). Nor can an individual "occasion" serve as the unit, as contextual variance between occasions would yield unstable, non-repeatable weights.

A **canonical functional step** is defined as an irreducible, discrete operation within a practice’s standardized end-to-end workflow, without which the primary intent of the practice cannot be fulfilled.

### Decomposition of the Reference Security: LETTER

Defined as: *A personally composed, physically handwritten message intended for a particular person and released to that person through a delivery route.*

```
[LETTER Workflow]
├── Step 1: Intention & Audience Selection (Identifying recipient and core motivation)
├── Step 2: Content Conception & Formulation (Determining ideas, tone, and framing)
├── Step 3: Textual Composition (Drafting specific words and syntax)
├── Step 4: Physical Inscription (Handwriting words onto paper via physical medium)
├── Step 5: Packaging & Addressing (Enveloping, sealing, and addressing the physical medium)
├── Step 6: Dispatch & Transmission (Handing over to logistics/postal service)
└── Step 7: Physical Delivery & Presentation (Transport and arrival at recipient)

```

1. **Step 1: Intention & Audience Selection** — Identifying the recipient and the specific interpersonal motivation for contact.
2. **Step 2: Content Conception & Framing** — Determining what ideas, feelings, or information to convey and selecting the emotional tone.
3. **Step 3: Textual Composition** — Selecting precise words, phrasing, and structural sequence.
4. **Step 4: Physical Inscription** — Rendering the composed text into physical handwriting onto paper.
5. **Step 5: Encapsulation & Addressing** — Enveloping, sealing, and inscribing the recipient's logistical address.
6. **Step 6: Dispatch** — Depositing the artifact into a postal or courier delivery infrastructure.
7. **Step 7: Physical Transit & Handover** — Transporting and placing the artifact into the recipient's possession.

## Section 2 — The sorting rule

### The Test

The candidate test (*whether the step handed over contained more than one live option*) is necessary but insufficient; execution steps often involve local, non-consequential options (e.g., choosing which pen to use or which street to drive down during delivery).

The refined sorting rule is the **Irreducible Consequential Option Test**:

> A step is classified as **Judgment** if and only if selecting among its available options requires evaluating interpersonal consequence, intent, or semantic meaning, such that delegating the choice alters the agency or voice of the practitioner.
> A step is classified as **Execution** if its options are purely instrumental, mechanical, or logistical, such that any two competent agents executing the step according to the practitioner's parameters yield an equivalent interpersonal outcome.

Steps are binary at the atomic level. If a proposed step appears to contain both judgment and execution, it is improperly decomposed and must be split into two sub-steps until each sub-step evaluates to a purely binary result.

```
                  [Step under Evaluation]
                             │
            Does selecting options require evaluating
            interpersonal intent, semantic meaning,
                   or social consequence?
                             │
               ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
              YES                          NO
               │                           │
        [CLASSIFIED AS]             [CLASSIFIED AS]
           JUDGMENT                    EXECUTION

```

### Application to Hard Cases

1. **Hard Case 1: Execution with a restoring side (Speech decoders for ALS; vocal cloning aloud)**
* *Classification:* **Execution**.
* *Rationale:* Translating neural signals or typed text into audible sound restores the physical transmission channel (mechanical output). Provided the system does not select or alter the semantic content or intent of the sentences, it evaluates as Execution under the test.


2. **Hard Case 2: Handing over deliberation whole (Sending "how are you" translated/generated by system)**
* *Classification:* **Judgment**.
* *Rationale:* Generating how to ask after someone delegates the selection of tone, phrasing, and relational framing. Because the system decides the semantic structure and interpersonal posture that the person would have decided, the step contains live interpersonal options and delegates agency.


3. **Hard Case 3: Judgment kept but narrowed (Reading interpreted summaries before original text)**
* *Classification:* **Judgment (Influenced / Narrowed)**.
* *Rationale:* The act of interpretation pre-filters the semantic options presented to the human decision-maker. Because the interpretation constrains the framing and emotional salience of the incoming choice, it operates directly on the deliberation step itself, modifying the Judgment step rather than shifting it into Execution.

## Section 3 — The counting rule

To derive an **execution share ($W_E$)** and **judgment share ($W_J$)** where $W_E + W_J = 1.0$, the procedure uses an **Irreplaceability Weighting Model**.

Counting steps equally is flawed because taking a letter to a mailbox (Execution) does not carry the same structural weight as deciding to forgive or confront someone in the message (Judgment). Simple time-weighting is also rejected, as physical inscription takes longer than the decision to write, yet time spent does not equal human agency.

### The Algorithm

1. Each canonical step $i$ is assigned a raw **Agency Criticality Weight ($c_i$)** on an ordinal scale from 1 to 5 by evaluating:
* *Distortion Cost:* If this step is altered or fails, how fundamentally is the human intention behind the practice corrupted?


2. Calculate the sums for Execution steps ($E$) and Judgment steps ($J$):

$$\text{Sum}_E = \sum_{i \in E} c_i, \quad \text{Sum}_J = \sum_{i \in J} c_i$$


3. Derive final shares:

$$W_E = \frac{\text{Sum}_E}{\text{Sum}_E + \text{Sum}_J}, \quad W_J = \frac{\text{Sum}_J}{\text{Sum}_E + \text{Sum}_J}$$



### Worked Example: LETTER *(Illustrative)*

| Step | Type | Description | Criticality Weight ($c_i$) *(Illustrative)* |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Judgment | Intention & Audience Selection | 5 |
| 2 | Judgment | Content Conception & Framing | 5 |
| 3 | Judgment | Textual Composition | 4 |
| 4 | Execution | Physical Inscription | 2 |
| 5 | Execution | Encapsulation & Addressing | 1 |
| 6 | Execution | Dispatch | 1 |
| 7 | Execution | Physical Transit & Handover | 1 |

$$\text{Sum}_J = 5 + 5 + 4 = 14 \quad \text{(Illustrative)}$$

$$\text{Sum}_E = 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 5 \quad \text{(Illustrative)}$$

$$\text{Total} = 19 \quad \text{(Illustrative)}$$

$$W_J = \frac{14}{19} \approx 0.74 \quad \text{(Illustrative)}$$

$$W_E = \frac{5}{19} \approx 0.26 \quad \text{(Illustrative)}$$

### Assumptions and Distortions

* **Assumption:** Human agency is concentrated in intent and semantic formulation rather than physical labor.
* **Distortion:** Underweights the ritualistic or tactile value that physical execution itself may hold for certain practitioners (e.g., the therapeutic act of handwriting).

## Section 4 — Who sets it, and how disagreement is resolved

### Submission Structure

The research panel submits a **Reconciled Tri-Panel Dossier** to the final decision-maker. The dossier contains:

1. The canonical step decomposition.
2. Independent classifications from three blind evaluators.
3. A single proposed weight pair with a calculated variance range.

```
[Research Panel: 3 Blind Evaluators]
                 │
   (Independent Decompositions & Scores)
                 │
                 ▼
     [Reconciliation Protocol]
                 │
   ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
   │ Consensus (Δ ≤ 0.05)      │ Disagreement (Δ > 0.05)
   │                           │
   ▼                           ▼
[Generate Dossier]     [Structured Delphi Round]
   │                           │
   └─────────────┬─────────────┘
                 │
                 ▼
      [Final Decision-Maker]

```

### Reconciliation Protocol

* Three domain analysts independently decompose the practice and score $c_i$ for each step.
* **Inter-Rater Tolerance Target:** $\Delta \le 0.05$ on final shares.
* **Disagreement Resolution:** If two evaluators disagree on a step's classification or if scores yield a share delta $> 0.05$:
1. **Decomposition Check:** Determine if the step is compound. If so, split it into binary sub-steps.
2. **Delphi Round:** Evaluators defend their Criticality Weights ($c_i$) using historical and behavioral references until scores converge within $\pm 1$ point on all steps.
3. **Tie-Breaker:** If consensus is not reached, the median step score is used. The user receives the point-estimate pair along with the min/max range generated by the panel.

## Section 5 — Cross-market consistency

The procedure enforces a **Single Global Baseline Pair** across Japan, Korea, and the United States, unless a cultural practice divergence fundamentally changes the canonical steps themselves.

```
                       [Evaluate Security Across Markets]
                                       │
                    Do canonical workflow steps differ structurally
                       between US, JP, and KR markets?
                                       │
                      ┌────────────────┴────────────────┐
                     NO                                YES
                      │                                 │
           [Assign Single Baseline]         [Flag Cross-Market Disagreement]
             (US = JP = KR Pair)                        │
                                            [Initiate Condition (b) Review]
                                                        │
                                            [Output Market-Specific Pairs]

```

### Detection of Disagreement (Condition b)

1. The panel independently runs the step decomposition using sociological and historical market records for Japan, Korea, and the United States.
2. **Disagreement Threshold:** A disagreement exists if the canonical step list differs structurally (e.g., a required formal protocol step exists in Japan that does not exist in the US) OR if the derived $W_J$ across markets differs by more than **$0.08$**.
3. If detected, this triggers formal review under **Condition (b)**, allowing market-specific pairs to be published instead of a single global pair.

## Section 6 — The revision procedure

Revision is restricted strictly to fixed conditions (a) and (b). Tuning weights to match market price outputs is prohibited.

```
                      [Proposed Weight Revision]
                                  │
                 Does the revision meet Condition (a)
                         OR Condition (b)?
                                  │
                  ┌───────────────┴───────────────┐
                 YES                             NO
                  │                               │
        [Accept & Log Entry]             [REJECT IMMEDIATELY]
                  │                  (Prohibited Outcome Tuning)
                  ▼
     [Maintain Immutable Record]
     - Old readings: Preserved
     - New readings: Re-baselined

```

### Revision Record Requirements

Every revision must generate an immutable audit log containing:

1. **Trigger Type:** Condition (a) [Definition Change] or Condition (b) [Cross-Market Disagreement].
2. **Initiator:** Senior Research Fellow or Decision-Maker (External requests rejected).
3. **Evidence Package:**
* *For (a):* Historical/technological proof that a step has been added to or removed from the baseline practice definition.
* *For (b):* Comparative ethnographic study demonstrating that the practice steps or criticality in Market $X$ differ fundamentally from Markets $Y$ and $Z$.


4. **Impact Analysis:** Comparative table showing old vs. new weight pairs.

### Historical Reading Policy

* **Past published readings remain untouched.** Historical series are never retroactively restated; doing so would corrupt the record.
* The revision date is marked as a **Structural Break Event**. Subsequent series publish under the revised pair, with a published conversion index at the break point for long-term tracking.

## Section 7 — Failure modes

Ranked from hardest to easiest to detect post-facto:

| Rank | Failure Mode | Description | Detection Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Teleological Weight Tuning** | Evaluators adjust criticality weights ($c_i$) to nudge $W_E/W_J$ toward an expected price curve. | **Extreme** — Looks like legitimate scoring; requires auditing rationale notes. |
| **2** | **Granularity Bias** | Over-decomposing execution (creating many small physical steps) to artificially inflate $W_E$, or vice versa. | **High** — Requires auditing whether steps meet the "irreducible canonical" threshold. |
| **3** | **Era Contamination** | Infiltrating the fixed definition with modern technological ease (e.g., treating handwritten inscription as "slow and obsolete" rather than analyzing its structural function). | **Medium** — Detectable by checking if step definitions reference post-1996 technologies. |
| **4** | **Cultural Myopia** | Scoring criticality based solely on Western usage patterns while applying the result globally to JP/KR markets. | **Low** — Easily flagged via cross-market comparative audits. |

## Objections

The assumption that execution and judgment shares remain strictly immutable over thirty years (Fixed Point 3) creates an unavoidable analytical tension when a technology wave redefines the boundary of the practice itself. While fixing the weights isolates exposure shifts clean of moving-target bias, it forces the procedure to rely on Condition (a) structural revisions whenever a practice evolves. Consequently, the procedure must treat the core essence of a practice as an idealized static baseline, even as the real-world human experience of that practice changes beneath it.
