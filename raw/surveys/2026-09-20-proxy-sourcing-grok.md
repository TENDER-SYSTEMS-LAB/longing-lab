# Section 2 — Definitions that do not match

This is the failure mode. Same English title, different object.

### Mobile telephony
- **Subscriptions ≠ people.** Japan TCA/MIC and Korea MSIT count **contracts/lines**. US CTIA counts **connections** (phones + tablets + watches + much M2M). ITU tries to count mobile-cellular subscriptions with an “active prepaid in last 3 months” rule, and historically under-counts some M2M relative to CTIA.
- **Multiple SIMs.** All three markets have dual-SIM and secondary data lines. Penetration >100 per 100 inhabitants is normal and is **not** unique-user penetration.
- **What is in the total.** Japan often publishes cellular and **BWA** on separate lines; summing them without a note double-counts some users who hold both. Korea monthly tables may include IoT/M2M in “wireless” totals depending on the tab (operator 6-K filings cite MSIT totals that include MVNO and M2M). FCC 477 mobile **voice** is narrower than CTIA connections.
- **Base.** Per 100 inhabitants uses UN/national population; per adult is a different series. Never mix.

### Fixed broadband
- **Speed floor.** ITU/World Bank: **≥256 kbit/s** advertised downstream, unchanged as the inclusion threshold even after real speeds moved to 100 Mbps–1 Gbps. US FCC “broadband” for policy is a different object: long 4/1 then **25/3**, later **100/20** Mbps. A US “broadband adoption” number from FCC is not the ITU fixed-broadband number.
- **Household vs individual vs subscription.** Fixed lines are shared. 40 subscriptions per 100 inhabitants can mean near-universal household access. Pew measures **adults who say they subscribe at home**. NTIA measures **households**. MIC and MSIT measure **contracts** and separately **household access**.
- **FWA / 5G home.** Increasingly counted as fixed in some national tables and as mobile in others. OECD notes 5G FWA as a growing share of new “fixed” adds in the US.

### Internet users
- **Recall window.** ITU: used the Internet **in the last three months**, any device, any location.
- **Age floor.** Korea official survey: **age 3+**. Japan MIC individual: typically **6+**. Pew: **18+**. ITU/WDI: population of all ages in the denominator, numerator from national surveys that do not share an age floor.
- **Japan WDI series is jumpy.** Third-party extracts of `IT.NET.USER.ZS` for Japan show year-to-year drops (e.g. around 2020–2021) that do not appear in the MIC survey’s smoother path. That is a **reconciliation problem**, not a real collapse in use. Do not splice WDI Japan to MIC Japan without a note.

### Social web
- **“Use”** in Pew is “ever use this platform.” MIC SNS is a category whose member list changes (LINE, Twitter/X, Instagram, Facebook, etc.). Korea official “SNS usage” **excludes** or separately reports instant messenger; KakaoTalk is counted as messenger, while a press-foundation survey treats KakaoTalk as the top “social media.” A 65% Korean “SNS” rate and a 98% KakaoTalk rate are not contradictions; they are different questions.
- There is **no official unique-user census** of “the social web” in any of the three countries.

### Smartphones
- Japan MIC **household** smartphone ownership (~90% in mid-2020s) ≠ MIC **individual** ownership (~80%) ≠ Docomo Institute **share of mobile owners** (97–98%). Korea individual ownership on the age-3+ survey is in the mid-to-high 90s. Pew is % of adults. These cannot be plotted as one line.

### Messaging
- Korea: near-universal KakaoTalk among internet users.
- Japan: LINE is the analogue in MIC tables.
- US: no equivalent official messenger; SMS is ubiquitous but not surveyed as “users”; WhatsApp is a minority adult platform in Pew (~32% in 2025). Cross-country “messaging adoption” is not one quantity.

### Generative AI
- “Ever used,” “used in last 3 months,” “weekly,” “daily,” “used an AI service” (including non-generative IoT), and “used ChatGPT specifically” are all in circulation. Korean official sources in 2025–2026 already disagree with each other by tens of points depending on sample (age 3+ internet survey vs adult panel vs value-added telecom survey of ages 19–69). See §4.

# Section 3 — Breaks, revisions and discontinuities

Only breaks I can source or that are standard in these series. Not exhaustive of every footnote in a yearbook.

**ITU / WDI (all three countries)**  
- Internet-user definition standardized to “last 3 months”; earlier national figures were sometimes “regular users” or ISP-based estimates. Pre-2000s points are not methodologically identical to 2010s points.  
- Mobile-cellular: prepaid activity rule (typically 90 days) adopted unevenly by national regulators; revisions move history.  
- Mobile-broadband as a distinct ITU series starts in the late 2000s; do not backcast it.  
- Population denominators revise after censuses (Japan 2015/2020, Korea 2015/2020, US 2010/2020), which moves per-100 series without any change in subscriptions.  
- WDI republishes ITU with lag; vintage of a download matters.

**Japan**  
- PHS included with mobile in some older totals, then separated, then shut down (service ended; contracts → 0).  
- 2G closed 2012; 3G runoff in the 2020s. Generation-split totals are not a single good “mobile adoption” line.  
- BWA (UQ etc.) reported beside cellular; white-paper “mobile ultra-high-speed” sums LTE+5G+BWA.  
- Communications Usage Trend Survey: household vs individual questionnaires; prefecture-level household sample from 2010; device list changes (smartphone item 2010; PDA folded into mobile 2009).  
- Internet-user % in WDI vs MIC diverge; treat as a break if you switch source.

**Korea**  
- Regulator/ministry name changes (MIC → MSIP → MSIT) without destroying the monthly subscription file, but table layouts and IoT inclusion change.  
- 5G commercial launch April 2019; 5G counts initially handsets-only, later second devices/IoT rules changed (KT 6-K notes MSIT criterion change, applied retroactively from 1Q23 for 5G handset disclosure).  
- Internet Usage Survey universe age 3+ is unusual internationally; comparisons to adult-only foreign surveys are invalid without restriction.  
- SNS / messenger question batteries change as platforms appear.

**United States**  
- Pew **mode break 2023**: phone (2000–2021) → web/mail (2023) → mixed web/mail/phone (2024–2025). Pew marks this on charts. Levels around the break are not strictly comparable.  
- Pew broadband question wording changed in the mid-2010s; Pew itself warns this affects 2015–2018 variance.  
- FCC Form 477: interconnected VoIP added 2008; mobile internet thresholds changed; broadband collection is migrating toward the Broadband Data Collection. Speed-threshold changes redefine “has broadband.”  
- CTIA survey scope expanded from cellular-only to CMRS; connections concept expanded with connected devices. 1985–2000 and 2001–2024 are documented as one published table but the object grew.  
- NTIA CPS supplement is **not annual**; missing years are not zeros.  
- ACS and CPS internet questions are another official track and do not match Pew or NTIA exactly.

# Section 4 — The gap after the last measurement (generative AI)

**There is no continuous, industry-wide, official time series of generative-AI usage volumes for Japan, Korea, or the United States.**

What exists:

1. **Repeated cross-section surveys** (best available official/quasi-official)  
   - **US — Pew Research Center.** Chatbot / generative AI use among adults, multiple waves from 2023 through 2025–2026 reports (ever-use, frequency, ChatGPT vs others). Copyrighted; adult universe; question text evolves.  
   - **Japan — MIC** Communications Usage Trend Survey / 情報通信白書. 2026 white paper reports 2025-survey ever-use and frequency, with a four-country comparison. Short run (essentially 2023–2025).  
   - **Korea — several official surveys that do not agree.**  
     - MSIT/NIA Internet Usage Survey: generative AI ever-use reported rising (one 2026 write-up: 33.3% → 44.5% on that survey’s universe).  
     - KMCC/KISDI intelligent-information user panel, smartphone-daily users 18–72: 38.9% ever-use in 2025 vs 24% in 2024.  
     - MSIT value-added telecom survey, adults 19–69: **78.1%** generative AI users in 2025.  
     These are not the same series. Publishing one number labelled “Korea gen-AI adoption 2025” would be a fabrication of comparability.

2. **Vendor point-in-time disclosures**  
   OpenAI, Google, Meta, Naver, Kakao, LINE publish MAU/WAU or product anecdotes. They are not national official statistics, not continuous public microdata, and not consistently split by country for all years.

3. **Traffic / panel proxies**  
   Similarweb-style chatbot reach (used in some OECD.AI notes) and app-panel firms. These are commercial estimates with panel bias, not statistical-office series.

4. **Closest durable proxies, with limits**  
   - Internet-user % (ITU) — necessary condition, not AI use.  
   - Smartphone ownership — necessary condition.  
   - Cloud / AI-service items already in Korean and Japanese ICT surveys — broader than generative AI.  
   - Electricity or GPU-cluster stats — production, not diffusion among residents.

**Last measurement vs extrapolation.** For a honest publication calendar: treat gen-AI as **survey-based from first official question (~2023) to the latest published wave**, and mark every later year as **not measured**. Do not interpolate a monthly AI-adoption line.

# Section 5 — A normalisation proposal

Goal: one comparable scale across JP / KR / US and 1996–last measured year, given §2.

**Recommended spine (the only series that is continuous, public, three-country, and definitionally aligned by a single compiler):**

> \( u_{c,t} = \) ITU “individuals using the Internet (% of population)” for country \(c\) and year \(t\).

Implementation rule, precise enough to replicate:

1. Download ITU DataHub or WDI `IT.NET.USER.ZS` for JPN, KOR, USA, years 1996 through the last year both ITU and the national office treat as final (as of this check, treat **2024 as last fully measured common year**; 2025 as estimate where flagged).  
2. Freeze one vintage (record download date). Do not mix WDI vintages.  
3. Do **not** replace Japan’s ITU line with MIC’s survey line inside the spine, even if MIC is locally better. If you must show MIC, plot it as a satellite series.  
4. For any year ITU is missing but the national survey exists, **leave a gap** or mark `interpolated from national survey after converting to the ITU age-denominator` — and if you convert, publish the conversion. Default: **gap**.  
5. After the last measured ITU year, the series stops. Extrapolation is out of scope for sourcing; if later work extrapolates, the week after last measurement must be printed on the chart.

**Satellite series (within-country only, not on the same axis as \(u_{c,t}\) without rescaling):**

- Mobile: ITU mobile-cellular per 100, **or** national contracts / mid-year population, but pick one family and stay in it.  
- Fixed broadband: ITU fixed-broadband per 100 (same 256 kbps rule in all three).  
- Smartphones / SNS / messaging / AI: national surveys, each kept in its native universe.

**Optional intensity index for a single country (not for cross-country levels):**

For technology \(k\) in country \(c\),

\[
x_{c,t}^{k} = \frac{s_{c,t}^{k} - s_{c,t_0}^{k}}{s_{c,t_1}^{k} - s_{c,t_0}^{k}}
\]

where \(s\) is the native series, \(t_0\) is first year \(s\) is defined, \(t_1\) is last measured year. This makes 0 = first observation, 1 = last.  

**What this assumes**  
- ITU’s last-3-months Internet-use concept is stable enough after the early-2000s.  
- Population denominators are acceptable.  
- “Used the Internet” is a tolerable proxy for the institution’s “how far communication technologies had spread.”

**What it distorts**  
- Korea’s age-3+ official rates will sit above Pew’s adult rates if someone later swaps sources.  
- Subscription series above 100 look like “more than complete diffusion” while unique-user internet use is still below 100.  
- Japan ITU internet-use wiggles will be treated as real movement if not footnoted.  
- Messaging: Korea/Japan near-saturation vs US WhatsApp-minority will be incomparable if forced onto one “messaging” axis.  
- Gen-AI cannot join the spine.

**Comparisons that remain invalid after the rule**  
- Cross-country smartphone % in a given year if one source is household, one is individual, one is adult.  
- Cross-country mobile “penetration” if one is CTIA connections and one is ITU subscriptions.  
- Cross-country SNS % if Korea’s figure is messenger-inclusive and Japan’s is MIC SNS and the US figure is “any of Pew’s listed platforms.”  
- Any 2025–2026 gen-AI ranking across the three countries using unmatched surveys.

# Section 7 — Refusals and confidence

**Declined.**  
- Inventing annual values where a table was not opened.  
- Smoothing or backfilling silent gaps.  
- Saying what the fictional index should do with the series.  
- Treating CTIA, Pew, MIC, and ITU numbers as interchangeable.  
- Picking a single “Korea gen-AI 2025” rate.

**Can a defensible thirty-year, three-country diffusion series be assembled?**

**Yes, with a narrow object and hard caveats. No, if the object is “all listed waves as one measured thing.”**

What *can* be assembled honestly:

- A **1996–2024** (possibly 2025 estimate) three-country series of **ITU Internet use** and **ITU mobile-cellular and fixed-broadband subscriptions**, downloaded from one vintage, with national satellites plotted separately.  
- National **survey tracks** for smartphones and SNS from about 2010–2011 (Japan, Korea, US Pew), not from 1996.  
- Messaging only from the early 2010s in JP/KR; US only as platform ever-use.  
- Generative AI only as a **post-2022 survey layer**, last measured year marked, no pre-history.

Caveats that must travel with any published line:

1. Subscriptions overcount people; surveys undercount children or use different age floors.  
2. ITU Japan Internet-use and MIC Japan Internet-use are not substitutes.  
3. US Pew has a 2023 mode break.  
4. Broadband “adoption” depends on the speed law in force.  
5. Korea public-data licence (공공누리 4) may block unmodified commercial reuse of the national tables.  
6. After the last official annual (treat **end-2024 / survey-2025** as the measurement edge unless a later final is cited), the series is no longer measured.

That is the honest boundary. Everything beyond it is design, which this round was not for.
