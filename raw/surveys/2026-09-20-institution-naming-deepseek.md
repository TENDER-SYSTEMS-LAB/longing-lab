## Section 1 — The Japanese publisher

Naming logic: a paper that computes and publishes an index needs a name that reads as a *shimbun* (newspaper) founded in the pre-war or immediate post-war period, with a regional or sectoral qualifier that explains why it had the standing to calculate a market average. The Nikkei's own history runs Chugai Bukka Shimpo → Chugai Shogyo Shimpo → Nihon Sangyo Keizai Shimbun → Nihon Keizai Shimbun, so the form **<qualifier> + 経済新聞 (keizai shimbun)** is the native pattern.

---

**1. 東海経済新聞 (Tōkai Keizai Shimbun)** — “Tōkai Economics Newspaper”

Form: regional economic daily, the Tōkai region being the industrial corridor between Tokyo and Osaka. Regional economic papers of this shape existed in the post-war period (Chūbu Keizai Shimbun, 1946).

Collision: **Tōkai Keizai Shimbun is not a known current publication.** A “Tōkai Keizai” appears in business-registry contexts as a *company* name (Tōkai Keizai Co., Ltd.) and there is a Tōkai Keizai University, but no newspaper of this title is in my recall. Domain `tokai-keizai.co.jp` likely taken at the company level; `tokaikeizai.co.jp` uncertain. Trademark class 16 (paper goods) would be the relevant class in Japan; a live mark on an existing *Tōkai Keizai* corporate name is possible. **Confidence: medium. Basis: naming-convention inference + partial recall of corporate names, not a register search.**

**2. 西日本経済新聞 (Nishi-Nihon Keizai Shimbun)** — “West Japan Economics Newspaper”

Form: regional economic daily. The existing Nishi-Nihon Shimbun (Fukuoka) is a general daily; an economic counterpart is structurally plausible.

Collision: **Nishi-Nihon Shimbun exists** as a major regional daily in Kyushu, and the name Nishi-Nihon is strongly associated with it. An economic paper called Nishi-Nihon Keizai Shimbun would be read as an affiliate or a knock-off. Domain `nishinippon.co.jp` is taken by the existing paper. **Reject: collision with a living newspaper of near-identical name. Confidence: high. Basis: known entity.**

**3. 中部経済新聞 (Chūbu Keizai Shimbun)** — “Central Japan Economics Newspaper”

Form: exactly the native pattern. Chūbu Keizai Shimbun (THE MID-JAPAN ECONOMIST) is a real paper founded 1946 in Nagoya.

Collision: **The name is taken.** Chūbu Keizai Shimbun exists, with website chukei-news.co.jp. **Reject. Confidence: high. Basis: known entity, confirmed in search.**

**4. 大阪経済新聞 (Ōsaka Keizai Shimbun)** — “Osaka Economics Newspaper”

Form: regional economic daily, Osaka being the historical commercial centre. An Ōsaka Keizai Shimbun existed in the pre-war/occupation period as a scanned periodical in the National Diet Library.

Collision: **The name has historical use.** A scanned Ōsaka Keizai Shimbun PDF is in Wikimedia Commons, and the title appears in NDL periodical records. Whether a live trademark persists is unknown, but the historical occupancy is enough to make this unsuitable for a 1996-set work unless the fictional institution is explicitly framed as the revival of the pre-war paper. **Reject. Confidence: high on historical existence; medium on current trademark status. Basis: archival record.**

**5. 京浜経済新聞 (Keihin Keizai Shimbun)** — “Keihin Economics Newspaper”

Form: Keihin is the Tokyo–Yokohama industrial belt, a natural qualifier for a paper covering the manufacturing corridor that would be index constituents. No known paper of this exact title.

Collision: **No known newspaper.** “Keihin” is used widely (Keihin Electric Express Railway, Keihin Corporation), so a trademark search in class 16 would be necessary. Domain `keihin-keizai.co.jp` likely free; `keihinkeizai.co.jp` uncertain. **Low-to-medium risk. Confidence: medium. Basis: naming-convention inference; no recall of a live paper. Would need register search.**

**6. 阪神経済新聞 (Hanshin Keizai Shimbun)** — “Hanshin Economics Newspaper”

Form: Hanshin is the Osaka–Kobe corridor, a standard regional qualifier (Hanshin Electric Railway, Hanshin Tigers). A Hanshin Keizai Shimbun is structurally plausible as a Kansai business daily.

Collision: **No known paper.** Hanshin is heavily trademarked in transport, leisure, and media classes, but not in the newspaper class on my recall. Domain `hanshin-keizai.co.jp` uncertain. **Medium risk because the Hanshin name is a large brand family. Confidence: medium. Basis: naming-convention inference + brand-recall.**

**7. 瀬戸内経済新聞 (Setouchi Keizai Shimbun)** — “Setouchi (Inland Sea) Economics Newspaper”

Form: a regional economic daily for the Setouchi industrial zone (Okayama, Hiroshima, Kagawa). Regional economic papers of this scale existed (e.g. Setouchi Shimbun as a smaller local paper).

Collision: **Setouchi Shimbun** exists as a local general paper in Kagawa. A Setouchi Keizai Shimbun would be a sibling-name, not a direct collision, but the proximity is a live trademark risk. **Medium risk. Confidence: medium. Basis: partial recall of Setouchi Shimbun.**

**8. 北陸経済新聞 (Hokuriku Keizai Shimbun)** — “Hokuriku Economics Newspaper”

Form: Hokuriku (Fukui, Ishikawa, Toyama) is a standard regional qualifier; a Hokuriku economic daily is plausible.

Collision: **Hokuriku Keizai Shimbun is not in my recall as a current paper.** Hokuriku is used by banks (Hokuriku Bank) and broadcasters. **Low-to-medium risk. Confidence: medium. Basis: naming-convention inference.**

**Recommended from this section:** Tōkai Keizai Shimbun (1) and Hokuriku Keizai Shimbun (8) as the lowest-collision candidates, with Keihin Keizai Shimbun (5) as a third. Nishi-Nihon, Chūbu, and Ōsaka are rejected for living or historical occupancy.

## Section 2 — The United States ratings house

Naming logic: Standard & Poor’s fused *Standard Statistics* and *Poor’s Publishing* in 1941; Dow Jones is two surnames. The form is **[Surname] & [Surname]** or **[Surname] [Surname]**, both surnames being those of the founding principals, plausibly of 1890–1930 vintage. The surnames should not evoke Moody’s, Fitch, Duff & Phelps, or the rating-agency oligopoly.

---

**1. Hartley & Voss**

Form: two Anglo-Germanic surnames of the period. “Hartley” is a common English surname; “Voss” is a plausible Americanised German surname. The ampersand form matches S&P.

Collision: **No known ratings house of this name.** Hartley and Voss are common surnames; a company called Hartley & Voss may exist in another sector but is not in my recall as a rating agency. Domain `hartleyvoss.com` uncertain. Trademark class 36 (financial services) would be the relevant class in the US and EU. **Low risk. Confidence: medium. Basis: no recall of a ratings entity; naming-convention inference.**

**2. Coleridge & Marsh**

Form: “Coleridge” evokes the English poet but is also a real surname; “Marsh” is a standard English surname. The pair reads as a 1920s bond-rating partnership.

Collision: **Marsh** is a major insurance-broking and risk-advisory brand (Marsh McLennan). “Marsh & McLennan” is a large public company. A ratings house called Coleridge & Marsh would be read as an affiliate or would trigger a trademark conflict in class 36. **Reject: collision with the Marsh brand family. Confidence: high. Basis: known entity.**

**3. Pemberton & Rhys**

Form: “Pemberton” and “Rhys” are plausible surnames of the era. The pair has the dry, partnership register.

Collision: **No known ratings house.** Pemberton is a place-name and surname; Rhys is a Welsh surname. A financial firm called Pemberton & Rhys is not in my recall. Domain `pembertonrhys.com` uncertain. **Low risk. Confidence: medium. Basis: no recall.**

**4. Aldrich & Wainwright**

Form: “Aldrich” (New England patrician) and “Wainwright” (English occupational) are both credible for a 1910s–1930s ratings partnership.

Collision: **No known ratings house.** Both surnames are common; Aldrich is associated with Nelson W. Aldrich (senator) and the Aldrich Plan, which is a period echo but not a collision. **Low risk. Confidence: medium. Basis: no recall of a financial entity.**

**5. Fenwick & Stroud**

Form: “Fenwick” and “Stroud” are English surnames, both plausible for a ratings firm of the period. The pair has no obvious real-world referent.

Collision: **No known ratings house.** Fenwick is a UK department store (Fenwick Ltd) and a common surname; Stroud is a town and surname. A financial services firm called Fenwick & Stroud is not in my recall. Domain `fenwickstroud.com` uncertain. **Low risk. Confidence: medium. Basis: no recall.**

**6. Halloran & Pike**

Form: “Halloran” (Irish-American) and “Pike” (English) give a plausible ethnic mix for a New York ratings house of the 1920s.

Collision: **No known ratings house.** Halloran and Pike are common surnames. Pike is also a fish and a weapon; the financial-services context would disambiguate. **Low risk. Confidence: medium. Basis: no recall.**

**7. Brantley & Coe**

Form: “Brantley” and “Coe” are both plausible American surnames of the period, the latter short and hard like “Poor” or “Fitch.”

Collision: **No known ratings house.** Coe is a surname and a college (Coe College); Brantley is a surname and a given name. **Low risk. Confidence: medium. Basis: no recall.**

**8. Ashford & Leighton**

Form: “Ashford” and “Leighton” are both English place-derived surnames, credible for a firm founded in the 1890s–1910s.

Collision: **No known ratings house.** Ashford is a town in Kent and a surname; Leighton is a surname and a place. A financial firm of this name is not in my recall. **Low risk. Confidence: medium. Basis: no recall.**

**Note on evocation:** Halloran & Pike and Brantley & Coe avoid the *Standard & Poor’s* / *Dow Jones* / *Moody’s* / *Fitch* sound-space most cleanly. Coleridge & Marsh is the one candidate whose second surname alone triggers a real-brand collision and is rejected on that basis.

## Section 3 — The three index names

### Japanese index — `<publisher> Average`

The Nikkei form is 日経平均株価 (Nikkei Heikin Kabuka), commonly “Nikkei Average” or “Nikkei 225.” The publisher name is the first element; the word “Average” is the second. The constituent count is 225 for the Nikkei because that is the number of stocks in the price-weighted average; a fictional index of the same type would use a count that is a **function of the number of stocks the publisher included when it took over calculation**, not a round number chosen for marketing. If the fictional paper took over from the exchange in a given year, the count should be the number of stocks the exchange was already using.

Candidates (using the recommended publisher names from Section 1):

**1. Tōkai Keizai Average** — 東海経済平均株価 (Tōkai Keizai Heikin Kabuka). Constituent count: 200 or 250, whichever the exchange was already using when the paper took over. Collision: no known index of this name. **Low risk. Confidence: medium.**

**2. Hokuriku Keizai Average** — 北陸経済平均株価 (Hokuriku Keizai Heikin Kabuka). Constituent count: 150 or 180. Collision: no known index. **Low risk. Confidence: medium.**

**3. Keihin Keizai Average** — 京浜経済平均株価 (Keihin Keizai Heikin Kabuka). Constituent count: 300 or 350. Collision: no known index. **Low-to-medium risk. Confidence: medium.**

**4. Tōkai Keizai Stock Average, 250** — a fuller form with the count attached. Collision: no known index. **Low risk.**

### Korean index — state-issued acronym form

KOSPI is Korea Composite Stock Price Index, an acronym of the English name, with the Korean name 한국종합주가지수 (Hanguk Jonghap Juga Jisu). The state-issued acronym form would be **K + [qualifier] + SPI** or a similar four-to-six-letter acronym. The constituent count convention for KOSPI is “all common stocks traded on the exchange,” not a fixed number; a fictional Korean index of the same type would be **all stocks on the relevant exchange division**, with the count reported as a range (e.g. “over 700”) rather than fixed.

Candidates:

**1. KECPI — Korea Economic Composite Price Index.** Form: K + EC (economic) + PI. Collision: no known index with this ticker. **Low risk. Confidence: medium.**

**2. KBCI — Korea Business Composite Index.** Form: K + BCI. Collision: no known index. **Low risk. Confidence: medium.**

**3. KCSPI — Korea Composite Stock Price Index.** Collision: this is the **predecessor name of KOSPI** (Korea Composite Stock Price Index, used before the 1983 replacement). **Reject: historical predecessor of KOSPI.** Confidence: high. Basis: known entity.

**4. KEPI — Korea Economic Price Index.** Form: K + EPI. Collision: KEPI is also a known acronym for the Korea Economic Daily Index (KEDI) family, but KEPI itself is not a known index. **Medium risk because the Korean Economic Daily has an index business and the acronym space is crowded.** Confidence: medium.

**5. KOSPI-type: KECI — Korea Economic Composite Index.** Form: K + ECI. Collision: no known index. **Low risk. Confidence: medium.**

### United States index — `<ratings house> <number>`

The form is “Standard & Poor’s 500” or “Dow Jones Industrial Average.” The number is the **number of constituent stocks**; for a price-weighted average the number is fixed by the selection methodology, and for a capitalisation-weighted index it is also fixed by selection. A fictional US index would use the number of stocks the ratings house selected at inception.

Candidates (using the recommended ratings-house names):

**1. Hartley & Voss 400** — constituent count 400. Collision: no known index. **Low risk. Confidence: medium.**

**2. Pemberton & Rhys 300** — constituent count 300. Collision: no known index. **Low risk. Confidence: medium.**

**3. Aldrich & Wainwright 500** — constituent count 500. Collision: **the number 500 is strongly associated with the S&P 500**, but the house name is different; the risk is that “500” reads as a direct imitation. **Medium risk. Confidence: medium.**

**4. Fenwick & Stroud 350** — constituent count 350. Collision: no known index. **Low risk. Confidence: medium.**

**5. Halloran & Pike 275** — constituent count 275. Collision: no known index. **Low risk. Confidence: medium.**

**6. Brantley & Coe 225** — constituent count 225. Collision: **225 is the Nikkei number**; using it for a US index would be confusing. **Reject on grounds of numerical collision.** Confidence: high. Basis: known entity.

## Section 4 — Tickers

The real tickers for the corresponding indices are: S&P 500 → **SPX** (Bloomberg) / **^GSPC** (Yahoo), Nikkei 225 → **NKY** (Bloomberg) / **^N225** (Yahoo), KOSPI → **KS11** (Investing.com) / **^KS11** (Yahoo).

The fictional tickers should be four-letter codes that read as index codes of the period (1996), not as modern ETF tickers. They must not be real tickers on the Tokyo, Korea, New York, or NASDAQ exchanges.

**Japanese index (Tōkai Keizai Average):**

- **TKAV** — not a known listed ticker. TSE codes in 1996 were four-digit numeric; no alphabetic ticker conflict. **Low risk.**
- **TKKA** — not known. **Low risk.**
- **TKA** — not known. **Low risk.**
- **NKTK** — not known. **Low risk.**

**Korean index (KECPI):**

- **KECP** — not known. Korean exchange tickers in 1996 were six-digit numeric; alphabetic codes are for indices, and KECP is not a known index code. **Low risk.**
- **KCPI** — not known. **Low risk.**
- **KBCI** — not known. **Low risk.**

**US index (Hartley & Voss 400):**

- **HV400** — not a known ticker. NYSE/NASDAQ alphabetic tickers of 1996 were up to four letters; HV400 is five characters and would not be a listed equity ticker. **Low risk.**
- **HVX** — **HVX is not a known ticker on NYSE or NASDAQ in my recall.** It is a plausible three-letter code. **Medium risk because three-letter codes are crowded. Confidence: medium.**
- **HVI** — **HVI is a known ticker?** HVI was the ticker for Hollywood International (a defunct company) and is used by various funds; it may be occupied. **Medium risk. Confidence: medium.**
- **HV400** is the safest form because the numeric suffix marks it as an index code rather than an equity ticker.

**Global composite (LONGING COMPOSITE):**

- **LNG** — **LNG is the ticker for Cheniere Energy** (NYSE: LNG), a major listed company. **Reject: collision with a living equity ticker.** Confidence: high. Basis: known entity.
- **LGC** — **LGC is not a known major equity ticker** on NYSE/NASDAQ in my recall, but three-letter space is crowded. **Medium risk. Confidence: medium.**
- **LNGP** — not known. **Low risk.**
- **LNG-C** — not known. **Low risk.**
- **LCOMP** — not known. **Low risk.**

## Section 6 — Refusals and residual risk

**What I could not check.**

I have no access to live trademark registers in Japan (JPO), Korea (KIPO), the United States (USPTO), or the EU (EUIPO). I cannot see whether a given name is registered in class 16 (paper goods), class 36 (financial services), or class 41 (publishing). I cannot check .co.jp, .com, .kr, or .jp domain availability. I cannot see the full TSE, KRX, NYSE, or NASDAQ ticker symbol lists for 1996. All collision judgements above are based on **recall of known entities, naming-convention inference, and the search results explicitly cited**; none are based on a register search. A candidate marked “no known entity” means only that I do not recall one, not that none exists.

**Candidates that require professional clearance before use:**

- **Tōkai Keizai Shimbun** — Tōkai Keizai corporate names exist in Japan; a class 16 search is necessary.
- **Keihin Keizai Shimbun** — Keihin is a large brand family (Keihin Corporation, Keihin Electric Express); a class 16 search is necessary.
- **Hanshin Keizai Shimbun** — Hanshin is heavily trademarked; a class 16 search is necessary.
- **Hartley & Voss** — common surnames; a class 36 search in the US and EU is necessary.
- **Pemberton & Rhys** — same.
- **Aldrich & Wainwright** — same.
- **Fenwick & Stroud** — Fenwick is a UK department store; a class 36 search is advisable.
- **Halloran & Pike** — same.
- **Brantley & Coe** — same.
- **HVX, HVI, LGC** — three-letter ticker space is crowded; an exchange symbol search is necessary.

**Candidates low enough risk to proceed on (subject to the limits above):**

- **Hokuriku Keizai Shimbun** — no known newspaper of this name; Hokuriku is a regional brand but not in the newspaper class on my recall.
- **Tōkai Keizai Average** and **Hokuriku Keizai Average** — no known index of either name.
- **KECPI** and **KBCI** — no known Korean index of either name.
- **Hartley & Voss 400** and **Pemberton & Rhys 300** — no known US index of either name.
- **HV400**, **LNGP**, **LCOMP** — no known ticker collisions.

**One structural risk to flag:** if the Japanese publisher name uses a regional qualifier (Tōkai, Hokuriku, Keihin), the index name will carry that regional marker. The Nikkei’s authority came from being the *national* economic paper. A regional paper publishing the national index is a different institutional claim: it says the index was computed by a regional authority, which may or may not be the intended structure. If the index is meant to be national, the publisher name should probably not carry a regional qualifier — which reduces the candidate pool to names like **Nihon Keizai Shimbun** (taken) or a newly coined national economic-paper name that does not use a region. That is a design decision the naming round should surface rather than resolve silently.
