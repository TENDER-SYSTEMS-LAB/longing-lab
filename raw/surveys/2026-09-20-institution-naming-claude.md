## Section 1: The Japanese publisher

**Structural warning first.** The form `<place> + 経済新聞` is occupied as a class. The "Minna no Keizai Shimbun" network publishes dozens of titles of that shape, such as Shibuya Keizai Shimbun and Yokohama Keizai Shimbun [R, confident]. I have excluded the whole pattern.

The name also needs a two-character contraction, as 日本経済 contracts to 日経, because the index is named from that contraction.

**J1. 夕刊経済新報 / Yūkan Keizai Shinpō / "Evening-Edition Economic Report" → 夕経 (Yūkei)**
- *Form:* 夕刊 plus a sector plus 新報 is the standard shape of a postwar evening paper.
- *Collision:* I recall no such paper [R]. One search on the full name and on 夕経平均 returned nothing of that name [W]. "Yukei" is a homophone of 有形 ("tangible") and also a given name, so yukei.com and .jp are probably taken [I].
- *Trademark:* [U]; classes 16, 41, 36 and 9 would matter.
- *Confidence it is unoccupied:* moderate.

**J2. 秋津経済新報 / Akitsu Keizai Shinpō / "Akitsu [archaic name for Japan] Economic Report" → 秋経 (Shūkei)**
- *Form:* poetic names for the country were normal for Meiji-founded papers such as やまと新聞 and 萬朝報.
- *Collision:* I know of no such paper [R]. Akitsu is a real place name and a railway station, and small firms use it [R], so domains are probably partly taken [I]. The contraction is a homophone of 集計 ("tabulation"), which is harmless and dry.
- *Trademark:* [U].
- *Confidence:* moderate.

**J3. 兜町夕報 / Kabutochō Yūhō / "Kabutochō [Tokyo's securities district] Evening Report" → "Kabuto"**
- *Form:* a district-named financial sheet, as *The Wall Street Journal* is.
- *Collision:* the place name itself is not protectable, but small securities tip-sheets with 兜町 in the title very plausibly exist or existed [I]. A helmet manufacturer holds KABUTO as a trademark in unrelated classes [R].
- *Confidence:* low to moderate; [U].

**J4. 内外経済新報 / Naigai Keizai Shinpō / "Home-and-Abroad Economic Report" → 内経 (Naikei)**
- *Form:* 内外 is a period-correct prefix.
- *Collision:* 内外タイムス was a real tabloid [R]. A prewar trade paper of nearly this exact name is quite plausible [I].
- *Confidence:* low; [U].

**J5. 東京毎夕経済 / Tōkyō Maiyū Keizai / "Tokyo Every-Evening Economic" → 毎夕 (Maiyū)**
- *Form:* mirrors 毎日 and 毎朝.
- *Collision:* I believe a 東京毎夕新聞 existed historically [R, moderate]. It is defunct and the trademark risk is low, but the name is not clean.

**J6. 実業夕報 / Jitsugyō Yūhō / "Business Evening Report"**
- *Form:* 実業 is Meiji-era commercial vocabulary.
- *Collision:* 実業之日本社 is a live publisher [R]. The contraction is poor, and 夕報 is a rarer ending in Japan than 夕刊.
- *Confidence:* [U].

**J7. 昭和経済新聞 / Shōwa Keizai Shinbun / "Shōwa Economic Newspaper" → 昭経 (Shōkei)**
- *Collision:* 昭和 appears in hundreds of company names, and I have a vague sense that periodicals titled 昭和経済 existed [R, weak].
- *Status:* probably occupied. Kept out of the recommended tier.

**J8. 経済夕報 / Keizai Yūhō / "Economic Evening Report"**
- *Collision:* too generic to protect or to check. Near-identical titles exist in Chinese-language markets (經濟日報) [R].
- *Status:* weak.

**Period facts that support the forms used above:**
- A paper titled 経済時事新報 was among the Tokyo economic dailies compulsorily merged into what became the Nikkei in 1942. The 〜経済〜新報 form is therefore authentic, and that exact title is taken.
- The Sankei began in 1933 as 日本工業新聞, published by an evening-paper company, 夕刊大阪新聞社. An evening publisher behind an economic paper therefore has real precedent.

## Section 2: The United States ratings house

**Class-level warning.** Almost any pair of Anglo surnames is the name of some small law or accounting firm somewhere [I]. That matters little for classes 36 and 16, but it means no pair should be called clear.

**U1. Whitcomb & Thayer (W&T)**
- *Collision:* A commercial firm of exactly this name appears in the 1875 Boston directory at 129 Summer Street. [W] It is long defunct. This helps plausibility, but the name is not virgin.
- *Near-misses:* Thayer evokes Thorndike, Doran, Paine & Lewis and other Boston counsel only faintly [R]. W&T Offshore is a listed energy company with ticker WTI [R].
- *Confidence no live financial firm uses it:* moderate.

**U2. Dwight & Sessions (D&S)**
- *Collision:* I recall no firm [R]. One search found nothing [W].
- *Near-misses:* Sessions is a clock brand and the surname of a 1987–93 FBI director, neither of which is financial.
- *Confidence:* moderate.

**U3. Hadley & Stroud (H&S)**
- *Near-misses:* Barr & Stroud was a UK optics firm, and Heidrick & Struggles shares the initials [R].
- *Confidence:* moderate; [U].

**U4. Halstead & Cobb (H&C)**
- *Collision:* Halstead was a prominent New York real-estate brokerage [R], and real estate sits next to class 36.
- *Status:* flagged. One search found nothing on the pair [W].

**U5. Crandall & Moffat (C&M)**
- *Collision:* no firm recalled [R]. Crandall evokes the 1990s American Airlines chief executive. The natural ticker stem, CMX, reads as COMEX.
- *Confidence:* moderate; [U].

**U6. Kittredge & Ames (K&A)**
- *Collision:* Ames was a live department-store chain in 1996 [R].
- *Status:* mild risk.

**U7. Ledyard & Howe (L&H)**
- *Collision:* Carter Ledyard & Milburn is a real Wall Street law firm, and the initials match Lernout & Hauspie [R].
- *Status:* flagged.

**U8. Sewall & Kemp (S&K)**
- *Collision:* "SK" collides with SK Group, which is serious for a work that covers Korea [R]. Kemp is also close to Kemper.
- *Status:* not recommended.

## Section 3: The three index names

**Korea (state-issued acronym).**

In 1996 the plausible issuer was the national statistics office, 통계청, rather than an exchange [R]. The Korean full names below are my own coinages.

- **KOLPI**: Korea Living Practices Index / 한국생활관행지수 / 콜피. No entity recalled [R]; [U]. My first choice.
- **KORPI**: Korea Practice Index / 한국관행지수. "Korpi" is a Finnish word and surname [R]. TradingView's code for South Korea's corruption index is KRCORPI, which is an uncomfortable near-miss [W].
- **KOMPI**: Korea Composite Mores Price Index / 한국종합풍속가격지수. It is close to KOMPSAT, the national satellite programme [R]; [U].
- **KCPPI**: Korea Composite Practice Price Index. The form is justified because KOSPI replaced an earlier Dow-style index abbreviated KCSPI in 1983. The acronym embeds "PPI" (producer price index), so it is suitable only as the predecessor series.

**Japan (`<publisher> Average`).**

The full form should mirror 日経平均株価, that is, the contraction plus 平均 plus a noun.

- **Yūkei Average / 夕経平均** (from J1)
- **Shūkei Average / 秋経平均** (from J2)
- **Maiyū Average / 毎夕平均** (from J5)
- **Kabuto Average** (from J3). This one is weaker, because it is named after a place and not after the publisher's contraction.

A count is optional, in the way "Nikkei 225" is only the informal form.

**United States (`<house> <number>`).**

- Whitcomb & Thayer 90
- Dwight & Sessions 90
- Hadley & Stroud 60

The number should be the constituent count at the base date. It stays fixed by a replacement rule (when one constituent leaves, another enters), not by market size.

Avoid 30, 40, 50, 100, 225, 300 and 500, all of which belong to real flagship indices [R]. S&P's own composite had 90 names from 1926 until it became the 500 in 1957 [R]. That makes 90 period-authentic. It is also unowned today, as far as I recall.

## Section 5: Rejected
