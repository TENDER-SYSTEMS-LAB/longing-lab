## A. Definitions

- STRG: "Taking up what they answer" could mean merely considering the answer or allowing it to change one's decision; the latter gives step 5 substantially more importance.
- INTR: Directly introducing two parties and asking a mutual acquaintance to introduce them assign the judgment of compatibility to different people.
- RECH: "Unprompted" contact and contact "on their day" can differ fundamentally when a calendar reminder or platform notification supplies the occasion.
- MEND: Apologising, negotiating a repair, and forgiving someone are different acts, potentially performed by different parties, with different distributions of judgment.
- MAKE: Making the object oneself and commissioning someone else to make it leave the physical labour with different people; one fixed importance for step 3 may not represent both.
- LERN: Learning a skill and learning a story are materially different processes; watching, copying, practising, and judging mastery do not necessarily apply equally.
- TCHR: Teaching a skill and transmitting a story place different demands on observation, correction, and deciding when to let the learner proceed independently.
- CARE: Caring for someone's body, managing their days, and representing their voice contain substantially different proportions of physical work and consequential decisions.
- VIST: A planned visit, an arranged meal, and walking someone home differ in how much travel, joint activity, and continued presence constitute the practice.
- GTHR: Joining a social group and participating in civic deliberation can involve radically different degrees of substantive judgment during participation.
- PLAY: A game governed by predetermined rules and improvisational music involve different amounts of consequential choice during the act of playing.
- TRET: "Leaving the turn open" could mean either keeping an informal account of who pays next or deliberately declining to settle a reciprocal obligation; the distinction changes the importance of steps 3 and 4.
- LEND: Lending "without a contract" does not necessarily mean lending without repayment terms; an informal verbal agreement remains possible.
- ETRS: Entrusting a key, secret, child, name, or period of time involves different forms of custody, responsibility, and verification; the objects may not support one stable split.

## B. Steps

- INTR: Seeking the parties' willingness to be introduced is missing; deciding whether disclosure or contact is appropriate can be distinct from recognising a suitable match.
- RECH: Step 5, "Keep the thread over time," introduces an ongoing relationship-maintenance activity beyond the definition's single act of making contact.
- TELL: Explicitly asking for the other person's view is missing; deciding what advice or response to solicit is distinct from deciding what personal information to disclose.
- MEND: Step 5 combines offering forgiveness and accepting forgiveness, potentially assigning two different parties' decisions to one step.
- VGIL: Steps 2 and 4, "Be there" and "Stay through," overlap as acts of presence; they need a distinction between arriving/presenting oneself and sustaining presence to avoid counting duration twice.
- VIST: Step 5, "Spend the time," is much coarser than arranging or travelling; meaningful conversation, responding to the other person, and adapting the visit are compressed into one execution step.
- GTHR: Step 3, "Take part," is unusually broad, especially for civic activity; contributing an opinion, responding to disagreement, and carrying out agreed actions are not equivalent operations.
- PLAY: Step 4, "Respond to the others," is already contained within step 3, "Play," for many games and musical activities, creating a possible double count.
- ASK: Steps 3 and 4 omit the other person's agreement to help; the transition from request to accepted obligation is important if the practice is evaluated as a reciprocal interaction.
- ETRS: Step 5, "Keep it or give it back," belongs to the recipient of the entrusted object, whereas steps 1–4 describe the entrusting person's actions; the procedure changes actors without declaring a second leg.

## C. Classes

- RECH, step 1 — judgment → execution, conditionally: Spontaneously thinking of someone is not necessarily choosing among live alternatives; the judgment occurs when deciding to act on the thought rather than ignore it.
- RECH, step 3 — judgment → execution, conditionally: Choosing a communication channel is manner unless the recipient reads that particular choice as part of the message; the definition does not establish that condition for every contact.
- WRIT, step 4 — judgment → execution: "Put it into a medium" describes implementation, not a consequential choice; selecting handwriting or another medium specifically to communicate something would qualify for the stated exception.
- CHSE, step 5 — judgment → execution: Giving the already-chosen object is execution unless choosing how, when, or in what circumstances to present it changes what the recipient understands.
- MAKE, step 4 — judgment → execution: Handing over the finished object is execution under the stated rule; a meaningful choice of presentation would need to be separately identified.
- GTHR, step 3 — execution → judgment, where substantive participation is intended: Deliberating, voting, advocating, or choosing a contribution involves alternatives that change collective outcomes; routine attendance or predetermined tasks remain execution.
- PLAY, step 3 — execution → judgment, where play involves live creative decisions: Choosing moves, improvising music, or adapting a shared performance can change its product; mechanically carrying out a predetermined sequence remains execution.
- ASK, step 4 — execution → judgment, conditionally: Accepting an offered favour rather than declining it can change the reciprocal relationship; simply receiving help after acceptance has already been decided is execution.
- VIST, step 3 — judgment → execution: Travelling is execution; the decision to undertake a meaningful journey belongs to the decision to visit, unless a particular means of travel is itself understood as part of the message.
- ETRS, step 5 — execution → judgment, if retained: Choosing whether to keep or return something can change the practice's meaning, but this remains a separate problem from the step's assignment to the wrong participant.

### Three least credible author splits

1\. STRG — Execution 0.47 / Judgment 0.53

The definition includes both addressing someone and taking up what they answer. Yet the author assigns importance 4 to speaking, 3 to hearing, and only 2 to weighing the answer.

The combined mechanical exchange receives more weight than the person's subsequent consideration of what was learned. Choosing the stranger also receives only 1.

This makes the practice resemble obtaining information from an unfamiliar source rather than entering a brief interpersonal relationship in which another person's judgment might matter.

My Part 1 split, under the given classes, is execution 0.22 / judgment 0.78. The difference is primarily substantive weighting, not classification.

2\. LSTN — Execution 0.67 / Judgment 0.33

The definition requires both listening to another person's trouble and answering it.

The author assigns importance 9 to making time and listening through, but only 5 to understanding and selecting a response combined. Confidentiality receives 1.

Presence and duration can be essential without displacing interpretation and response as the principal source of interpersonal judgment.

With these weights, a system that reproduces attentive presence but performs weakly at understanding the speaker could appear to replace most of the practice's execution while leaving relatively little judgment exposed.

My Part 1 split is execution 0.55 / judgment 0.45. Even this figure depends on retaining the stipulated classification of listening through and confidentiality as execution.

3\. GTHR — Execution 0.76 / Judgment 0.24

This security explicitly includes civic acts, yet its steps allocate importance 13 to going, taking part, and returning, versus only 4 to choosing a group and taking on a role.

The author gives taking on a role an importance of just 1.

For gatherings involving civic deliberation, collective commitments, or contested decisions, participation cannot reliably be reduced to the execution of an already-set purpose. Important choices occur after joining.

Unlike STRG, the credibility problem is therefore both a step-boundary problem and an importance problem. "Take part" contains activities that satisfy the judgment rule alongside those that satisfy the execution rule.

My Part 1 split is execution 0.61 / judgment 0.39 under the supplied classes. A valid decomposition of civic participation could change it further, but the present procedure does not justify a revised numerical split.
