## A. Definitions

- **INTR**: The two branches (making the introduction vs. asking someone else to make it) are different acts by different people. The steps fit only the first, and the split would differ by branch.
- **WRIT**: "Defined by act, not medium" gives no rule for where a written birthday message, apology or confidence belongs. WRIT overlaps RECH, TELL and MEND.
- **RECH**: "No practical reason, or on their day" mixes two branches. The "on their day" branch is calendar-prompted, which contradicts "unprompted" in step 1, and the split depends on which branch is meant.
- **MEND**: It does not say whose side. Steps 1–4 are the harmer's, and the harmed party has only step 5.
- **CHSE**: The definition names no occasion, yet step 1 presupposes one. An unprompted gift has no step 1.
- **MAKE**: "Or having its maker make it for them" merges making by hand with commissioning. What the recipient reads differs between the two, and that moves step 3 (see C).
- **CARE**: "Their voice" is unclear to an audience (speaking for them? helping them speak?).
- **VIST / VGIL / CARE**: A bedside visit to a dying relative fits all three, and "in body" appears only in VIST.
- **VIST / DEC**: "A meal arranged together" is also DEC.
- **GTHR**: "Civic acts enter here" is a catch-all (voting, jury service, protest) that are not "joining others", so the split covers heterogeneous acts.
- **ASK / HELP**: "Someone near" is undefined (physically? socially?), so remote or paid help is left open.
- **LEND**: It does not say what is lent (money, an object, a key), and it overlaps ETRS ("something of one's own").
- **ETRS**: It is defined by a list of objects (secret → TELL, time → ASK/HELP, child → CARE), not by an act. "Without proof" is the only act-level feature.

## B. Steps

- **STRG**: No step chooses what to say, so "Say it" absorbs the wording choice (cf. TELL 3, WRIT 3).
- **INTR 1/2**: Seeing that two would suit each other and choosing whom to recommend are the same choice counted twice.
- **INTR 5, RECH 5, DEC 5**: Follow-up, keeping the thread and carrying out the decision lie outside the defined act, and each adds execution weight.
- **TELL**: The definition includes asking for their view, but no step does.
- **LSTN**: There is no "decide to listen" step, though TELL 1, VGIL 1, HELP 1 and ASK 1 all start with a decision. "Make time" stands in for it.
- **RECH 1**: Thinking of someone unprompted is an occurrence, not a choice. The missing step is "decide to act on the thought".
- **MEND**: The harmed party's steps (hearing the apology, taking it in) are missing.
- **DEC 3/4**: Weighing options and settling on one are the same choice counted twice.
- **CHSE 2/3**: What they would value largely fixes the thing chosen, and 5+5 counts it twice.
- **MAKE**: It is not parallel to its sister form CHSE (no occasion step, no obtaining materials), so comparing the two forms is biased.
- **LERN**: There is no "decide what to learn", while TCHR 1 has "decide what to pass on", so the legs are asymmetric.
- **VGIL 2/4**: "Be there" and "Stay through" are the same act at two durations.
- **GTHR 2/3/4**: Joining, taking part and keeping coming are one act (attending) at three time scales, carrying 13 of 17 importance.
- **PLAY 3/4**: "Play" and "Respond to the others" overlap, since responding is playing.
- **ETRS 5**: "Keep it or give it back" is the trusted party's step in a non-leg security, so the side the share measures is unclear.
- **Granularity**:
  - Judgment layers are cut fine (WRIT has four judgment steps and one execution step), while the core act is one coarse step (GTHR 3, PLAY 3, VIST 5, VGIL 2, LSTN 2).
  - MEND and VIST have 6 steps against 4 in MAKE, HELP, VGIL, TRET and LEND.

## C. Classes

- **STRG 3 Say it** (E → mixed): No earlier step chooses what to say, so the wording choice, which differs in meaning, sits inside it.
- **STRG 5 Weigh the answer and act on it** (J → split): It is conjoined. Weighing is judgment, and acting on the answer is a separate choice or doing.
- **INTR 4 Make the contact** (E → J/mixed): The means (forward, cc, in-person) is read by both parties as part of the recommendation. Under the exception this is judgment, cf. RECH 3 and WRIT 4.
- **WRIT 4 Put it into a medium** (J → mixed): Medium choice is judgment under the exception (handwritten vs. text), but the step also covers mere formatting, which is execution.
- **TELL 4 Say it** (E → mixed): Face to face vs. letter vs. message is read by the listener, and no step carries the means choice.
- **ASK 3 Ask** (E → mixed): Same reason as TELL 4.
- **MEND 4 Say it** (E → mixed/J): The means of apology (in person, letter, via a third party) is read as part of it, and importance is 5.
- **LSTN 1 Make time** (E → mixed): Deciding to give time is judgment (cf. VGIL 1, HELP 1), and only scheduling is execution (cf. HELP 2). At importance 5 it drives the 0.67.
- **LSTN 5 Keep it to oneself** (E → J): Whether to disclose is a live option that differs in meaning (betrayal), and ETRS 4 treats the same restraint as judgment.
- **ETRS 5 Keep it or give it back** (E → J): The step names two options that differ in meaning.
- **CARE 4 Check back** (E vs. ETRS 4 "Refrain from checking" J): The same act is classed oppositely, so one of the two is wrong under the rule.
- **VIST 3 Travel** (J → E): Route and mode are manner, and nobody reads them as message.
- **DEC 2 Gather the options** (E → mixed): Searching is execution, but which options enter the set changes the outcome.
- **CHSE 5, MAKE 4 Give it** (J → mixed/E): The handover is execution (cf. LEND 2, ETRS 3), and only the manner and moment, read by the recipient, are a choice.
- **MAKE 3 Make it** (E → J): Handmade-ness is a choice of means the recipient reads as part of the message, which is the exception's paradigm case. Making also involves form choices.
- **GTHR 2 Go and join** (E → mixed): "Go" is execution, but "join" (committing) is a live choice.
- **GTHR 3 Take part** (E → mixed): It is execution only as bare attendance. In civic acts, what one contributes or votes differs in meaning.
- **PLAY 3 Play** (E → J): Moves and musical phrases are live options that differ in outcome, and step 4 names the same stream.
- **PLAY 5 Finish or agree to play again** (E → mixed): "Agree to play again" is a live choice.
- **ASK 4 Accept the help** (E → J): Accepting, declining, or letting the helper do it their way is a live option.
- **RECH 5, MEND 6, GTHR 4** (E → J): Each can lapse at every occasion, so each repetition is a live choice, and the same reasoning applies to VGIL 4. By contrast, TRET 4, LEND 4 and ASK 5 are memory or waiting with no option. The author should state one rule for "keeping" steps.

**Three least credible splits**

1. **LSTN 0.67 execution.** "Understand what is meant" (2) and "Choose what to say back" (3) are exactly what a system does when it answers someone's trouble. "Make time" (5) is a mixed step. Counting it as judgment gives 0.33, and also counting step 5 as judgment gives 0.27.
2. **GTHR 0.76 execution.** It rests on one act (attending) counted three times (13 of 17), and the definition sweeps in civic acts where the choice is the practice. Attending and taking part cannot be handed over with the choice kept, so the number does not describe what could actually be handed over.
3. **CARE 0.24 execution.** Giving help and checking back are what a hired carer or robot does with the choice kept, which is the clearest case of handing over the doing. Rating them 3 and 1 against 5 and 4 for noticing and deciding understates that. My own values give 0.42.
