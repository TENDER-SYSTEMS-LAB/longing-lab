## A. Definitions
- WRIT: "Choosing words for one person..." leaves open whether drafting for a superior (execution-heavy) or a lover (judgment-heavy) is meant, moving the split.
- RECH: "...with no practical reason, or on their day" is ambiguous; "on their day" could mean a birthday (high judgment) or a random Tuesday (high execution).
- VIST: "Going to be with someone in body..." mixes highly structured (meal) and unstructured (walking home) acts, which have fundamentally different judgment/execution ratios.

## B. Steps
- INTR: Step 4 ("Make the contact") and Step 5 ("Follow up") are both execution, but "Follow up" is often a distinct judgment of whether the match succeeded, making it coarser than TELL.
- DEC: Step 2 ("Gather the options") is execution, but in joint decisions, gathering options is often a judgment of what is viable, making it finer than LSTN's "Make time".
- CHSE: Step 4 ("Obtain it") and Step 5 ("Give it") split the physical transfer, whereas MAKE combines making and giving into fewer steps, making CHSE artificially finer.
- LERN: Step 2 ("Ask to be taught") is a trivial execution isolated as a step, whereas TCHR lacks a corresponding "Agree to teach" step, creating asymmetry.

## C. Classes
- WRIT Step 4: Classified as judgment, but unless the medium itself is the message, it is execution; the definition doesn't specify the medium is part of the message.
- RECH Step 3: Classified as judgment, but unless the means is read as part of the message, it is execution under the stated exception.
- VIST Step 3: Classified as judgment, but travel is purely a physical route (execution) unless the mode of travel is the message, which isn't stated.
- LSTN Step 1: Classified as execution, but choosing to prioritize this person over other demands is a live option affecting the practice's meaning, thus judgment.
- TRET Step 3: Classified as judgment, but this is the default state of not asking for repayment, making it an execution of the TRET form rather than a live choice.
