# 5. What no source covers

**Years not covered.** For execution restored, the pre-1996 years are outside the span, but even within 1996–2026 there is no continuous, comparable series for any market. The patent and product timelines cover the span as availability markers, not as exposure measures. Postal records cover the span as volume measures, not as letter-writing measures. For judgment substituted, the span is almost entirely uncovered before 2022. Generative AI adoption surveys begin in 2023. Pre-2022 judgment substitution — older forms of assisted writing, translation, and summarisation — is not systematically measured in any source I found.

**Markets not covered.** Korea is the weakest market for execution-restored sources. There is no verified Korean equivalent of TAIS or ABLEDATA with a continuous series. Korean disability surveys exist, but the writing-specific and letter-specific modules are not attested. Japan has the richest assistive-technology registry data but almost nothing that observes personal letter-writing as a practice. The United States has postal volume and educational writing assessments, but no personal-letter survey series across the span.

**Exposures not covered.** No source I found directly observes **execution restored for letter-writing** — that is, the restoration of the graphomotor act, the addressing, and the physical release of a personal handwritten message. The closest sources are speech-to-text adoption (which restores spoken communication, not handwriting) and assistive-device registries (which observe product existence, not use). No source directly observes **judgment substituted for letter-writing** — that is, the handover of the deliberation over what to say in a personal message. The closest source is the press-release AI-detection study, but the practice is corporate, not personal.

**What kind of data would cover the gaps.** For execution restored in letter-writing: a longitudinal disability survey with a module on **writing and sending personal messages**, including assistive-technology use for handwriting and postal sending. Such a module would need to separate the act of composing from the act of sending, and would need to be repeated across the span. For judgment substituted in letter-writing: a **corpus study of personal letters** with provenance metadata — who composed the content, who selected the words, who generated the text. The press-release study demonstrates the method; the corpus does not exist for personal letters. For the boundary between restoration and substitution: **platform-level logging** of acceptance and edit rates for writing-assistance features, with a distinction between typo correction, grammatical suggestion, structural suggestion, and full content generation. No platform publicly discloses this at the required granularity.
