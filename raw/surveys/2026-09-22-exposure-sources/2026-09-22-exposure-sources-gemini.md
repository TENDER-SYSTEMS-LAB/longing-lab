### Section 3 — Sources that Observe Both, or the Boundary Between Them

The boundary between restoration and substitution hinges on whether technology enables the user's intent to be expressed (restoration) or formulates/alters the intent itself (substitution).

#### 1. Mobile Input Method Editors (IMEs) & Autocomplete Telemetry

* **Source:** Platform IME Analytics (Apple iOS Keyboard, Google Gboard, Naver SmartBoard, ATOK JP).
* **Restoration Signal:** Character-by-character prediction or stroke-to-kanji/hangul conversion where the user types key prefixes and selects the exact word they intended to spell. The system eliminates physical input friction.
* **Substitution Signal:** Next-word prediction accepted across 3+ consecutive words, auto-suggested emoji/sticker insertions, or keigo/honorific level auto-conversions accepted without manual overrides.
* **Boundary Metric:** *Keystroke Savings Ratio vs. Semantic Length.* If keystrokes decrease while maintaining character output without changing word choices, it is Restoration. If the accepted output deviates in vocabulary or tone from the user's historical typing baseline, it is Substitution.

#### 2. Voice Synthesis & Voice Cloning Data

* **Source:** AAC Device Logs & Consumer Voice Cloning APIs (ElevenLabs, Descript, Tobii Dynavox).
* **Restoration Signal:** Text-to-speech (TTS) engines reading user-typed text verbatim, or custom-trained synthetic voices replicating an ALS patient's natural lost voice to speak their explicitly dictated text.
* **Substitution Signal:** Generative speech systems where the user inputs a brief text bullet or mood selection, and the voice model expands it into a fully articulated, emotive vocal message.
* **Boundary Metric:** *Text Input Compression Ratio.* The ratio of source text characters input by the user to the speech tokens output by the engine. A 1:1 text-to-speech mapping indicates pure Restoration; a low ratio (e.g., 5-word prompt yielding a 50-word vocal message) indicates Substitution.

#### 3. Real-Time Translation Engines

* **Source:** Machine Translation Telemetry (Google Translate, DeepL, Naver Papago).
* **Restoration Signal:** Literal or direct syntactic translation of a fully composed message, restoring the user's ability to communicate across a language barrier they cannot speak or write.
* **Substitution Signal:** Dynamic tone adjustment, honorific re-leveling (e.g., switching casual English into formal Japanese *Keigo* or Korean *Jondetmal*), or localized idiom replacement generated automatically by the engine.
* **Boundary Metric:** *Semantic & Tonal Variance Score.* Comparing back-translation fidelity. If the translation strictly preserves input semantics without adding structural politeness, it remains Restoration. If the engine independently determines societal/cultural tone conventions not specified in the source text, it crosses into Substitution.

#### 4. Inline Writing Assistants & Smart Compose

* **Source:** Grammarly, Google Smart Compose, Kakao AI Writing Helper.
* **Restoration Signal:** Spelling correction, typo fixes, and motor-error smoothing (e.g., correcting double-pressed keys or accidental spaces).
* **Substitution Signal:** Accepting full-sentence completions, structural rewrites, or automated tone changes (e.g., "make this sound more polite").
* **Boundary Metric:** *Edit Distance & Insertion Types.* Differentiating character deletions/swaps (Restoration) from token additions and vocabulary substitutions (Substitution).

### Section 4 — A First Sort by Quantity and Quality

#### Category A: Official Statistics & Logistics Records

* **Quantity:** High (70–90 year-market cells). National postal and telecom statistics cover nearly all 30 years across US, JP, and KR.
* **Quality:** Low-to-Medium directness. 3 to 4 inference steps. They count macro volumes (total letters, total TTY calls) without demographic breakdowns, requiring inferences to isolate restored or substituted personal correspondence. Definition is steady across the 30-year span.

#### Category B: Clinical & Assistive Device Registries

* **Quantity:** Medium (40–60 year-market cells). US data (CMS/Medicare) spans most of 1996–2026. JP and KR national health registries are structured post-2000.
* **Quality:** High directness for Execution Restored (1 step), but zero coverage for Judgment Substituted. Definition remains steady, limited strictly to reimbursed medical hardware/software.

#### Category C: Big Tech Platform & OS Telemetry

* **Quantity:** Low-to-Medium (15–45 year-market cells). Virtually empty for 1996–2007. Begins with smartphone adoption (2008+) and generative AI features (2018+). Covers all 3 markets uniformly.
* **Quality:** Extremely high directness (1 step) for both Restoration and Substitution (measures exact keystroke acceptances and auto-replies). However, definition stability across the span is poor due to rapid software iterations.

#### Category D: Academic Longitudinal Panels & Time-Use Surveys

* **Quantity:** Medium (30–50 year-market cells). ATUS (US) starts in 2003; JP and KR time-use surveys run quinquennially (every 5 years). Longitudinal panels (HRS, KLoSA, JSTAR) run biennially.
* **Quality:** Medium directness (2 inference steps). Self-reported survey data requires inferring technology's specific role in restoration/substitution from general "computer/communication time."

#### Category E: App Store Metrics & Search Query Analytics

* **Quantity:** Medium (45–55 year-market cells). Google Trends / Naver DataLab / Search logs start around 2004–2008. App metrics start around 2008.
* **Quality:** Low directness (3 inference steps). Observes interest and downloads, not actual daily usage or long-term behavioral exposure.

#### Category F: Patent & R&D Registries

* **Quantity:** Maximum (90 year-market cells). Patent class filings across USPTO, JPO, and KIPO cover 1996–2026 without interruption.
* **Quality:** Very low directness (4 inference steps). Measures technical capability and commercial intent, not actual user exposure or practice transformation.

### Section 5 — What No Source Covers

1. **The Pre-Smartphone Granular Baseline (1996–2006 for Judgment Substituted):**
* *Gap:* No source captures early auto-correct, spell-check, or email template selection at the individual decision level prior to centralized platform telemetry.
* *Ideal Data:* Continuous keylogger or software usage logs from representative consumer desktop panels in 1996–2006 tracking spell-check acceptances versus manual edits.


2. **Unassisted Consumer Off-the-Shelf Restorative Usage (1996–2010):**
* *Gap:* People using consumer technology (e.g., standard keyboards, digital macro pads) to overcome non-diagnosed motor difficulties or distance before official accessibility APIs were integrated into OS metrics.
* *Ideal Data:* Long-term demographic accessibility telemetry tracking non-clinical device modifications for personal writing.


3. **Cross-Platform Interpersonal Decision Boundaries:**
* *Gap:* Identifying whether a person composed a letter/message across multiple devices (e.g., drafting an email outline on a phone with heavy autocomplete, then hand-refining it on a PC).
* *Ideal Data:* Cross-device unified telemetry tracking single-document provenance from initial thought-draft to final delivery.


4. **Private Personal Handwritten Correspondence Volume (1996–2026):**
* *Gap:* Stamped letter mail data includes bills, marketing spam, and official documents. No source isolates purely personal, handwritten letters sent between individuals year-by-year across all three markets.
* *Ideal Data:* National postal sampling audits classifying mail piece content by sender type (individual vs. corporate) and production method (handwritten vs. printed).
