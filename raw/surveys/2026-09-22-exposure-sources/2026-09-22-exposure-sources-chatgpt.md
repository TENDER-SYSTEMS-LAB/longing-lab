## 1 — Sources for execution restored

**This catalogue contains 48 candidate rows.** It includes verified sources, plausible but unverified archives, and data structures for which no holder was found. None is selected or recommended.

**Dating and coverage conventions.** My training cutoff is **December 2025**. A **†** marks a subsequent observation, release, or availability statement: it is **outside training-based attestation and included only on the basis of web verification**. The collection date is **September 22, 2026**.

There is an arithmetic discrepancy in the brief: **1996–2026 inclusive contains 31 years, hence 93 country-year cells**. Section 4 therefore reports coverage against **1996–2025’s 90 cells**, with **2026 separately**, preserving the requested endpoints.

In the tables, first and last years are the **bounds established in this collection**, not necessarily the source’s complete lifespan. **“Pub.” means publication year, not observation year; “?” means not established.** `verified` confirms the named source and stated scope, not the existence of a complete exposure series. For `probable` records, proposed fields are explicitly conditional.

For LETTER, evidence about producing text, speaking, accessing a network, or sending mail does not by itself establish the complete act: **personal composition, physical handwriting, a particular recipient, and release through a delivery route**. The inference column keeps those missing links visible.

## 2 — Sources for judgment substituted

Here, **content provenance is not identical to decision provenance**. A sender can transmit words written by somebody else; a person can also accept a machine suggestion that merely expresses a choice already made. The missing distinction is whether the system supplied the **substantive choice**, not simply the characters.

Under the stipulated definition, **delegating translation of a complete interpersonal expression counts as judgment substitution**, even when the user subsequently reviews or approves it.

## 4 — A first sort by quantity and quality

**Quantity conventions.** Counts below concern the candidate’s **own observations**, not validated exposure estimates. **Q = n/90** means that many historical country-year or explicitly identified benchmark cells. **“≤” is a ceiling, not a claim of complete data. “?” means the relevant count was not established.** Publication markers, platform-years and global aggregates are identified separately rather than silently turned into national observations. Multiple waves within one country-year do not increase the cell count.

**Quality conventions.** **L ≈ n** is my approximate count of inference links between the observation and the requested exposure, **not a statistical quality score**. Typical links are: observation → actual assistance used → restored ability or delegated substantive choice → complete LETTER → population exposure. Links already observed by a source need not be inferred. Definition stability is stated separately.

The groups below are unranked. Counts overlap and **must not be added together**.

### Official access, skills and population surveys

* **E01 — ITU:** **Q = ?; ceiling 90**, depending on indicator and missingness. **L ≈ 4–5.** Broad access concepts persist, but technology categories, affordability baskets and reporting completeness need harmonization.
* **E02 — NTIA:** **Q ≥ 2 verified endpoint wave-years; total unenumerated.** **L ≈ 4.** Survey questions and the meaning of internet/computer use change; a continuous annual series is not established.
* **E03 — Japan communications survey:** **Q = ?; ceiling 30.** **L ≈ 4.** Annual programme existence is verified, but relevant items and their continuity across editions were not audited.
* **E04 — Korea digital divide:** **Q = 9 annual survey-years for 2016–2024; earlier coverage from 2002 is additional but unenumerated.** **L ≈ 3–4.** The 2016 index replacement and subsequent sample/method changes prevent an assumption of a single stable definition. ([지표누리][4])
* **E05 — NHATS:** **Q = 14 annual study-years, 2011–2024**, not necessarily 14 years of every technology item. **L ≈ 3.** Longitudinal disability measurement helps, but item changes, attrition and the older population limit interpretation.
* **E06 — ATUS:** **Q = 23 annual study-years, 2003–2025.** **L ≈ 4.** Activity coding is more stable than product-specific questions, but technology attribution is absent; 2025 also has a documented fieldwork interruption. ([Bureau of Labor Statistics][38])
* **E07 — PIAAC:** **Q = 7 benchmark cells** using wave-end dating: three countries in 2012, US in 2017, three countries in 2023. **L ≈ 4.** These are not annual estimates; assessment frameworks and problem-solving constructs change between cycles. ([OECD][7])
