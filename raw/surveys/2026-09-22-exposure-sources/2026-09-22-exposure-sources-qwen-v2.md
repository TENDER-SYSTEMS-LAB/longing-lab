### Section 3 — Sources that observe both, or the boundary between them

1. **Generative Voice Synthesis / Voice Cloning (e.g., Apple Personal Voice, ElevenLabs)**
   * **Execution Restored:** An ALS patient uses their cloned voice to "speak" or dictate a LETTER.
   * **Judgment Substituted:** The AI infers prosody, emotion, and emphasis, effectively deciding *how* the words are delivered.
   * **Boundary Metric:** Word Error Rate vs. Prosody generation metrics; manual vs. AI emphasis tagging.
2. **Speech-to-Text (Dictation) with Auto-Punctuation and Formatting**
   * **Execution Restored:** Motor-impaired user dictating a LETTER.
   * **Judgment Substituted:** The NLP model deciding where commas and periods go, structuring the syntax of the thought.
   * **Boundary Metric:** Ratio of user corrections to auto-punctuation vs. raw character generation rate.
3. **LLM-Powered Translation with Tone Adjustment (e.g., DeepL Write, Papago Honorifics)**
   * **Execution Restored:** Allows someone who doesn't speak Korean to write a LETTER to a Korean elder.
   * **Judgment Substituted:** The system automatically selects the correct level of honorifics, deciding the social relationship dynamics.
   * **Boundary Metric:** User override rate of honorific levels or formality sliders.

### Section 5 — What no source covers

* **Years:** The period **1996–2005** lacks granular digital telemetry for judgment substitution (predictive text and auto-compose did not exist in measurable forms). Execution restoration lacks digital granularity before the smartphone era, relying almost entirely on physical hardware sales rather than usage data.
* **Markets:** **Korea (KR)** lacks public granular data on prison inmate digital messaging compared to the US. **Japan (JP)** lacks detailed public API usage reports for local LLMs/Keyboards compared to US tech giants.
* **Exposures:** We lack sources that measure the **qualitative intensity** of the substitution. Current sources measure adoption (e.g., feature toggled on) or volume (e.g., API calls), but they do not measure the *loss of deliberation* directly. We cannot tell from these sources whether a user agonized over an AI prompt to craft a deeply personal LETTER, or simply clicked "send" on an auto-generated reply. We also lack sources for the **boundary cases** where the user accepts AI text but heavily edits it, retaining some judgment. A source recording keystroke-level edit histories post-AI generation would be required to cover this.
