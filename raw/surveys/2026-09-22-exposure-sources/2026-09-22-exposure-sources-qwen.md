### Section 3 — Sources that observe both, or the boundary between them

These sources capture the same act, where the line between *execution restored* and *judgment substituted* depends on intensity, user configuration, or the specific feature used.

1. **AAC (Augmentative and Alternative Communication) Device Logs (e.g., Tobii Dynavox, PRC-Saltillo)**
   - *What it observes*: Telemetry on whether a user selects a pre-programmed, whole-phrase icon (e.g., "I love you") versus spelling a novel message letter-by-letter using a grid.
   - *How to tell them apart*: Letter-by-letter spelling with a switch or eye-gaze represents **execution restored** (the user is doing the composing, the tech only provides the motor bridge). Selecting a pre-baked phrase represents **judgment substituted** (the clinician or family pre-decided the available sentiments, and the user merely triggers one). 
   - *Status*: Probable (manufacturers have this data, but public, anonymized, year-by-year release is rare).

2. **Email "Smart Compose" / Predictive Text Telemetry (e.g., Gmail, Outlook)**
   - *What it observes*: The rate at which users accept a grayed-out suggested completion versus the rate at which they type over it or delete it.
   - *How to tell them apart*: Accepting a full-sentence suggestion without editing is **judgment substituted** (the model chose the phrasing and intent). Using the suggestion as a starting point and heavily editing it is **execution restored** (the tech overcame the blank-page barrier or typing fatigue, but the user retained compositional judgment).
   - *Status*: Verified (aggregate metrics exist in academic and corporate transparency reports).

3. **Voice Banking and Synthesis Services (e.g., VocaliD, Model Talker)**
   - *What it observes*: Number of hours of voice samples donated by a patient vs. the number of novel utterances generated by the synthetic voice post-loss.
   - *How to tell them apart*: The act of recording one's own voice for future use is **execution restored** (preserving the *doing* of vocal identity before illness takes it). When the system later generates a *novel* sentence the patient did not explicitly record, using a generic language model to fill the gaps, that is **judgment substituted** (the algorithm decided the phonetic and prosodic realization of a new choice).
   - *Status*: Verified (program databases track these metrics).

4. **Machine Translation with "Edit Distance" Metrics (e.g., DeepL, Google Translate)**
   - *What it observes*: The initial translated output vs. the final user-edited output before sending.
   - *How to tell them apart*: If the user sends the raw translation, **judgment is substituted** (the system decided the tone, formality, and phrasing). If the user heavily edits the translation to adjust politeness levels (crucial in Japan and Korea), the tech merely **restored execution** (bridging the language gap), while the user retained judgment.
   - *Status*: Probable (requires access to internal product analytics, but observable in controlled UX studies).

### Section 4 — A first sort by quantity and quality

*Grouped by kind of data. Quantity = estimated number of year-market cells filled (max 90). Quality = inference steps (1 = direct measure of exposure, 3+ = highly indirect) and definition stability.*

**Assistive Technology & Disability Registries**
- *NHIS (US), MHLW (Japan), KOSIS (Korea)*: Quantity: ~70/90 (US starts 1996, Korea ~2000). Quality: 2 steps. Stable definition, but infers "restoration" from "device ownership," not usage.
- *Medicare/RESNA Clinical Data*: Quantity: ~30/90 (US only). Quality: 2 steps. Highly stable clinical definition, strong proxy for ALS/illness reference case.

**Telecom & Infrastructure Penetration**
- *ITU, MIC, MSIT, FCC*: Quantity: 90/90. Quality: 3 steps. Infers restoration of execution from macro-level access/cost data. Definition of "communication" is too broad (includes passive consumption).

**Literacy & Education**
- *PIAAC / NCES / KISA*: Quantity: ~40/90 (PIAAC is periodic, not annual). Quality: 3 steps. Infers need for restoration from literacy scores, but does not measure technological intervention directly.

**Postal & Physical Mail Services**
- *Japan Post, USPS Special Services*: Quantity: ~60/90. Quality: 1-2 steps. Very direct observation of execution restored for physical LETTERs, but volume is extremely low and may not be reported with annual granularity for disabled-specific services.

**AI Writing & Predictive Text Telemetry**
- *Smart Compose / Keyboard Telemetry (Academic & Corporate)*: Quantity: ~50/90 (strong post-2015, weak pre-2010). Quality: 1-2 steps. Highly direct measure of judgment substitution (acceptance rate), but proprietary and often aggregated, masking personal vs. professional use.
- *Gartner/IDC Market Reports*: Quantity: ~40/90 (post-2015). Quality: 3 steps. Measures market size, not actual compositional substitution intensity.

**Gig Economy & Outsourcing**
- *Upwork/Fiverr*: Quantity: ~40/90 (US-heavy, post-2010). Quality: 2 steps. Directly observes outsourcing of judgment, but misses informal family/friend ghostwriting.

**Boundary Cases (AAC Logs, Voice Banking, Translation Edit Distance)**
- *AAC / Voice Banking*: Quantity: ~20/90 (niche populations). Quality: 1 step. The most precise, behavior-level data available for separating execution restoration from judgment substitution in the exact reference case (illness-induced loss).
- *Smart Compose Edit Distance*: Quantity: ~30/90. Quality: 1 step. Excellent conceptual fit, but data is tightly held by platform monopolies.

### Section 5 — What no source covers

Despite broad collection, significant blind spots remain for the 1996–2026 span across the three markets:

1. **Granular, pre-2010 data on "Judgment Substituted" in personal correspondence**: Before the advent of modern LLMs and Smart Compose (pre-2015), there is almost no systematic, annual tracking of how much compositional judgment was ceded to technology in *personal* (non-enterprise) contexts. Template usage is the only proxy, and it is a weak one.
2. **Korea-specific, annual data on "Execution Restored" for physical LETTER writing (1996–2005)**: While KOSIS has disability data, there is no verified annual series specifically tracking assistive *writing* or *postal* assistance for the disabled in Korea during the early part of the span. 
3. **Differentiation of "Who Decided" vs. "Who Typed" in historical data**: Almost all historical telecom and device usage data conflates the two. A source showing an elderly person sent 100 emails does not reveal whether they dictated it (execution restored), typed it with heavy autocorrect (mixed), or had a grandchild type and decide the content (no tech restoration, just human delegation). 
4. **Robotics / Automated Handwriting**: There is no verified annual data source tracking the use of robotic handwriting devices (e.g., automated plotters writing personal letters for those with motor loss). This remains a speculative or highly niche clinical data point, not a standardized statistical series.
5. **Cross-market harmonized metrics**: No single source applies the exact same definition of "assistive communication use" or "AI writing adoption" across the US, Japan, and Korea simultaneously year-by-year, requiring patchwork inference that introduces methodological noise. 

*What would have covered them*: A longitudinal, cross-national panel study specifically designed to track "communication agency" (e.g., the WHO or OECD sponsoring a decadal module on "Technological Mediation in Personal Correspondence"), or mandated, privacy-safe telemetry sharing from major keyboard/messaging providers broken down by user demographic and edit-acceptance behavior.
