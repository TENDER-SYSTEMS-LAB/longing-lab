**Training cut-off:** end of May 2026. Anything after that is outside what I can attest. I did not check these sources online for this response, so every status below reflects my training knowledge only. "Verified" means I am confident the source exists under that name with roughly that coverage. It does not mean I have confirmed a specific figure. Where a series continues past early 2026, its last year is given as the latest edition I know of.

Abbreviations: JP = Japan, KR = Korea, US = United States.

## Section 3: Sources that observe both, or the boundary between them

| act | source | what in the source could tell restoration from substitution |
|---|---|---|
| Predictive text | T9 (1995); POBox, Japanese predictive conversion (Masui, 1996–); Gboard federated-learning papers (e.g. Hard et al. 2018, which report suggestion click-through) | Length of the accepted span relative to the whole message. Completing a single word restores the doing. Accepting a whole clause substitutes the content. Public papers give click-through, not span length. **probable** |
| AAC word and phrase prediction | Keystroke-savings literature in AAC research; Kane et al. 2017 (CHI, self-expression through AAC devices); Valencia et al. 2023 (CHI, language models and AAC users) | Whether the user picks among candidates or accepts generated text. The Valencia study directly discusses trading speed against ownership of what is said, which is the exact boundary. **verified** |
| Speech neuroprosthesis with language model | Card 2024; Willett 2023 | Word error rates, and the language model's influence on the decoded output. Where the model "corrects" the person, the system's guess stands in for their intent. Willett 2021 decodes at the character level, where model influence is small. **verified** |
| Voice synthesis from a learned voice | Apple Personal Voice, voice banking | The voice is execution only, as long as the text comes from the person. It crosses into substitution only when paired with generated text. Usage logs are not public. **probable** |
| Translation | VoiceTra, Google Translate conversation mode, Papago | Selecting from a fixed phrase list vs translating free input. Both count as substitution under the prompt's definition, but phrase-list selection hands over more of the choice. Vendors may hold this distinction in logs. **speculative** (as data) |
| Suggested replies | Smart Reply | A candidate sent unedited vs edited before sending. Google's 2016 paper reports the sent share. **verified** (point estimate) |
| Summarisation | Apple Intelligence notification summaries (2024); Gmail summaries | This applies on the reading side: the system decides what in a message matters. No public data separates skimming from delegated judgment. **speculative** |
| General LLM delegation | Anthropic Economic Index collaboration modes; OpenAI's editing vs generating split | The closest existing decision-level tags. Directive delegation corresponds to substitution; validation or iteration on the person's own text corresponds to restoration or assistance. **verified** |
| Greeting card plus handwritten line | Japanese New Year-card surveys | A printed template with a handwritten line added is a mixed act. The share of cards with an added line could track the boundary. **probable** |
| Dictation | OS dictation features | Execution only. Useful as a control, since it restores doing without touching choice. **verified** (existence) |

## Section 5: What no source covers

**Judgment substituted, 1996–2014, all markets, for LETTER.** I found nothing that measures who composed personal correspondence. Card and template volumes are the only proxy. What would cover it: a longitudinal household survey asking "who wrote the words in the last card or letter you sent," or coded archives of dated personal letters.

**Decision-level data before 2025, all markets.** The first sources that tag delegation of the choice itself (the Anthropic and OpenAI studies) start in 2024–25. Vendor logs of suggestion acceptance by span length would cover roughly 2015–2024, but they are not public.

**Execution restored for ALS-type speech loss, JP and KR, most years.** No annual count of people using speech-generating devices or BCIs exists that I know of. The US has partial coverage through Medicare claims from about 2013 only. Covering the gap would need national device registries or insurer claims by device code.

**Korea, 1996–2001, for both exposures.** Little beyond volume data. The earliest communications survey starts in 1999, the digital-divide survey in the early 2000s, and disability data comes from the 2000 wave.

**Japan, AI-era substitution.** Only adoption-level items from 2023 onward, with no author/sender separation.

**Translation, any market.** Launch dates are known, but usage volume by year is not public.

**GIFTING and ASKING AFTER SOMEONE, substitution in all markets.** Recommendation-driven gift choice and system-prompted contact (birthday reminders) are held in platform logs only.

**2025–2026 in general.** Most series have not published these years as of my cut-off, and anything after May 2026 is outside what I can attest.

**Author/sender separation for handwritten LETTER specifically.** No source separates the physical writer from the composer (for example, handwritten copying of generated text), in any market or year.
