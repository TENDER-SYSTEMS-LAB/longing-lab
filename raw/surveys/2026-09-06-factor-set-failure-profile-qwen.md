Section 1 — Failure profile, one per set, six times
SET A — 8 factors
(a) Mispricing.
Solitude is overvalued when Social Atomization is high: the set reads declining community as more demand for solitude, but past a threshold atomization converts solitude into loneliness. Serendipitous Discovery is underpriced in decline because no mediation factor captures platform-controlled discovery.
(b) Unattributable events.
A platform demotes slow, unoptimized content: damage spreads across several securities but must be split among Variance Elimination, Attention Scarcity, and Synthetic Substitution, with no platform-governance line.
A city closes parks and libraries: the shock hits Aimless Walking, Unplanned Visit, Live Performance, and Solitude, but lands as diffuse Physical Grounding / Social Atomization rather than a discrete infrastructure loss.
(c) Identification failure at eleven securities.
Temporal Compression and Variance Elimination collapse across Waiting for a Reply, Unplanned Phone Call, and Serendipitous Discovery. Social Atomization and Physical Grounding collapse across Unplanned Visit, Live Performance, and Aimless Walking.
(d) Double counting.
Digital substitution of Handwritten Letter enters through Synthetic Substitution, Temporal Compression, and Physical Grounding. Smartphone navigation for Aimless Walking enters through both Temporal Compression and Variance Elimination.
(e) What this set can never say.
It cannot say that an intermediary captured the terms of a practice: “a platform governs discovery, access, or rent.”
(f) Falsification.
Film photography revival is at risk if cohort-driven, because no cohort factor exists. A structural winner is at risk because most structural lines are negative pressures; winners must come from Friction Premium or Performative Legibility. Collinearity is at risk for TC/VE and SA/PG. Attribution can sum exactly. The model can contradict the artist via negative Friction Premium or Performative Legibility.
SET B — 12 declared factors, ten priced lines; F4 and F9 contribute zero
(a) Mispricing.
Unplanned Visit is underpriced in decline because F4 Coordination Formalization and F9 Generalized Trust are unpriced; the move is forced into F7, F8, and F5, misdescribing a permission change as distance/tie/dematerialization drift. Aimless Walking can be overvalued when F7 improves, even if F3 and F10 make walking more routed and commodified.
(b) Unattributable events.
A viral image of a celebrity handwriting a letter: one cultural shock splits across F12 Status Coding, F6 Attentional Slack, and F10 Commodification.
A large employer adopts a four-day week: the extra discretionary time hits many securities, but no priced factor names labor-time endowment; attribution spreads across F6, F7, F8.
(c) Identification failure at eleven securities.
F1 Latency Collapse and F3 Predictive Pre-selection collapse for Waiting for a Reply, Unplanned Phone Call, and Serendipitous Discovery. F5 Dematerialization and F7 Spatial Proximity collapse for Live Performance and Unplanned Visit; F7 and F8 Tie Density also move together.
(d) Double counting.
Streaming’s displacement of Physical Media enters as F2 substitution, F5 dematerialization, and F10 enclosure. Live Performance decline can enter through F5, F7, F8, and F10 for one underlying trend.
(e) What this set can never say.
While F4 and F9 are zero, it cannot say: “the social permission for unannounced contact was withdrawn.” It can describe adjacent conditions, not the norm change itself.
(f) Falsification.
Film photography revival is at risk if driven by cohort entry rather than status/friction. A structural winner is possible but often must emerge from Conditions or Valuation, not Displacement. Collinearity is at risk for F1/F3 and F5/F7/F8. Exact attribution is strained by interaction between priced factors and security-level mechanisms. The model can contradict the artist via F11, F12, and positioning.

(c) Identification failure at eleven securities.
FE and AD collapse for Handwritten Letter, Serendipitous Discovery, and Unplanned Phone Call. MD and FE collapse for Handwritten Letter, Physical Media, and Film Photography.
(d) Double counting.

(f) Falsification.

A structural winner is at risk because four factors are pressure factors and only SR can revalue positively. Collinearity is at risk for FE/AD and MD/FE. SET E — 12 factors in three layers
(a) Mispricing.
Film Photography can be overvalued in a cohort-led revival if security-specific supply collapses (film stock, labs); the factor-saturated system has little room for a local negative shock. Handmade Object can be overvalued when E1/E3 are positive but craft-skill transmission fails locally; the set lacks room for security-specific supply erosion.

S1 Time Compression and S4 Uncertainty Compression collapse across Waiting for a Reply, Unplanned Phone Call, and Serendipitous Discovery. E4 Economic Time Scarcity and E6 Attention Scarcity collapse across most time-intensive securities. It cannot say that a security moved for its own local reason. With twelve factors, nearly every move is factor-attributed; genuine idiosyncrasy is structurally crowded out.
(f) Falsification.
Film photography revival is strongly expressible, including via E5 cohort replacement. A structural winner is possible. Collinearity fails or is severely at risk because several factor pairs overlap and factor count exceeds security count. Exact attribution is mechanically easy but causally weak. The model can contradict the artist via R1 positioning and negative experiential regimes.

P7 is so broad that it correlates with P1, P3, and P5 across social-contact securities. (f) Falsification.

Section 2 — Shared versus specific failure
Shared failures, present in every set:
Time/uncertainty overlap. Every set contains at least one pair resembling temporal compression and uncertainty suppression. Across Waiting for a Reply, Unplanned Phone Call, and Serendipitous Discovery, these exposures are nearly parallel.
Digitalization double counting. The same shift from physical/slow/local practices to digital/fast/mediated equivalents can enter multiple factor lines in every set. Attribution can sum while misdescribing one cause as several.
Discrete exogenous shocks are hard. Policy changes, infrastructure investment, housing shocks, pandemics, and viral cultural moments do not fit cleanly into slow structural factors or cyclical valuation regimes.
Security-specific idiosyncrasy is pressured. Low-factor sets absorb local shocks into broad factors; high-factor sets leave little room for security-specific fundamentals.
The eleven-security universe is restrictive. Identification is fragile whenever two factors share similar exposures across the same communication, discovery, and physical-presence securities.
Specific failures:
SET A: no mediation/platform factor; Social Atomization is monotonic where Solitude is non-monotonic.
SET B: F4 and F9 priced at zero remove the cleanest lines for contact-permission and trust; security-level mechanisms complicate exact causal attribution.
SET C: too few factors; no spatial/community/trust factor; friction premium demoted to security level.
SET D: no labor-time endowment factor; several valuation-adjacent factors overlap.
SET E: factor count exceeds security count; idiosyncrasy is crowded out; collinearity becomes structural.
SET F: P7 is a mega-factor that preserves a phenomenon but destroys causal specificity.
Section 3 — Failure cost under this work’s purpose
Collinearity. Statistically severe; artistically tolerable unless it makes the ledger unreadable or prevents the model from disagreeing with the artist. The artwork can survive correlated factors if the attribution lines still produce intelligible sentences.
Double counting. Statistically severe; artistically mixed. If the ledger shows one cause entering three times, that may feel analytically false. But it may also dramatize how many systems press on one fragile practice. It becomes artistically severe only if it makes every explanation feel interchangeable.
Missing structural truth. Artistically severe. If the set cannot say “a platform governs the practice,” or “trust disappeared,” or “people have no discretionary hours,” the work loses one of its main powers: naming a pressure exactly.
Unattributable events. Statistically severe if residuals appear; artistically severe if forced attribution produces a false story. A small residual may be tolerable; a precise but wrong cause is not.
Loss of idiosyncrasy. Artistically severe. The securities must be able to move for their own reasons. If every move is explained by shared factors, the securities cease to feel like distinct fragile practices.
Inability to produce a structural winner. Artistically severe. The model must be able to generate a genuine bull market for one practice while others decline. If all structural lines only press downward, the work loses its capacity to surprise.
Inability to contradict the artist. Artistically severe. The work depends on the model being able to turn against the human bias.
Statistical severity is not artistic severity. A highly collinear set may still produce compelling weekly sentences. Conversely, a statistically clean set may be artistically dead if it cannot name platform power, trust loss, or security-specific life.
Section 4 — Minimal repair
SET A. Add one priced Intermediation / Platform Governance factor. Cost: a ninth factor; it will compete with Synthetic Substitution and Variance Elimination for attribution and reduces parsimony. It complicates the set by making platform events visible.
SET B. Promote F4 Coordination Formalization to priced status. Cost: the set’s own thin-support rule is violated; F4 will correlate with F7 and F8, and the ledger becomes eleven priced lines while F9 remains absent. It breaks the initial execution rule but gives Unplanned Visit a permission-norm line.
SET C. Add one priced Spatial / Community Infrastructure factor. Cost: a sixth factor; it will correlate with MD and FE and weaken the set’s compression. It complicates identification but allows distance, third places, and proximity to be named.
SET D. Add one priced Discretionary-Time Endowment factor. Cost: a tenth factor; it will correlate with F1 and F7 and tightens the factor-to-security ratio. It complicates identification but allows work-hour and labor shocks to be named.
SET E. Demote S4 Uncertainty Compression to a security-level or unpriced state variable. Cost: uncertainty events are absorbed by S1 or security mechanisms; Layer 1 loses a priced line. This reduces one collinear pair but does not fully solve factor saturation.
SET F. Split P7 into one narrower spatial/third-place factor and one contact-permission/trust factor. Cost: an eleventh factor; the structural/valuation balance is disturbed, and the new lines may correlate with P3 and P1. It preserves encounter analysis while restoring causal specificity.
Section 5 — What would settle it
Run a fixed simulation and attribution tournament using the eleven securities only.
Construct a library of concrete events: platform algorithm change, trust shock, transit investment, rent shock, four-day work week, cohort entry, AI substitution, viral status event, pandemic-like gathering risk, and infrastructure closure.
Assign each event an external causal label: which real mechanism is primary, which are secondary, and which securities are affected.
For each set, generate weekly attribution ledgers under those events.
Measure:
whether Film Photography can enter a multi-year bull market without violating the set;
whether at least one security can emerge as a structural winner;
factor-pair collinearity across the eleven securities;
whether ledgers sum exactly without forced or absurd attribution;
whether the model can produce a price path that contradicts the artist’s human bias;
how often a single event is split across factors in a way that misnames the cause.
The discriminating evidence would be not which set is most elegant, but which set produces the least false attribution under the fixed eleven-security universe.
Section 6 — Refusals
I did not rank the six sets. I did not name a preferred set, preferred factor count, or preferred architecture. I did not attempt to identify authorship. I did not recommend expanding the security universe. I did not propose a seventh set or a hybrid, except for the single-change repairs permitted in Section 4.
I do not find the framing of this round wrong in general. One caveat: the test that “at least one structural winner must emerge” may press some sets toward valuation-regime winners rather than structural winners; that is a property of the eleven-security universe and the nature of the practices, not necessarily a defect of any one set.
