# Institution Naming Review

The naming round asked seven models for candidates for the two fictional institutions [[DEC-009-three-markets-and-convergence]] requires — a Japanese economic paper that computes the Japanese index, and a United States ratings house of two surnames — plus three national index names and four tickers, and made the **collision report** the deliverable rather than the names. `LONGING COMPOSITE` was given as fixed. Seven responses were collected: ChatGPT, Claude, DeepSeek, Gemini, GLM, Grok and Qwen. Kimi was not consulted.

**No name is selected by this page, and no name is cleared.** No reviewer could see a trademark register, and every one said so. What the round produced is a map of the occupied ground — which forms, morphemes, surnames, acronyms and codes are taken or dangerous — and a short list of candidates that survived each reviewer's own screen, all of which still need a professional clearance search. All candidates are `llm-proposed`; this comparison is `llm-synthesis`.

## Scope and source quality

The prompt required every collision judgement to carry the basis actually used. Four responses comply throughout: **Claude** (recall, inference, three web spot-checks, unknown — each tagged), **GLM** (recall, convention, unknown), **Grok** (web-visible entities, with a working set explicitly *not cleared*), and **ChatGPT**, which ran the most verification — archival hits on Japan Search and a Saitama library catalogue, publishers' own sites, and the Nasdaq Trader listed-security directories dated 2026-09-18 — and still reports low confidence on non-occupation for everything it advances. ChatGPT's closing rule is worth keeping: *a domain, a stock symbol, a company name and a trademark are different things; evidence in one category should not be promoted into proof about another.*

Three responses are weaker on exactly the point the prompt cared about. **Gemini** marks candidates *Clean* and claims a code is *unassigned on Bloomberg / Refinitiv*, which it cannot know, and proposes a Japanese index at 225 constituents. **Qwen** reports *Confidence: High* on recall alone for nearly every entry, uses 225 and 500, and invents institutional histories for its counts (*a 1950 baseline survey*, *the 500 most frequently observed behavioral instances*) — the kind of backstory the prompt asked reviewers not to write. **DeepSeek** checks its rejections well (Chūbu Keizai confirmed by search, Ōsaka Keizai in National Diet Library records) but grades its survivors *low risk* on absence of recall. Their candidates are recorded; their confidence labels are not carried.

The two Claude captures in this round keep the one-line chat message that preceded the report. Gemini's response, unlike its factor-round response, is in English.

## The occupied ground

This is the round's durable output. It is what any future candidate must be checked against before the register search.

**Japanese publisher.**

- **The `<place>経済新聞` pattern is occupied as a class.** The みんなの経済新聞 network publishes dozens of city-scale titles of that shape — 渋谷経済新聞, 横浜経済新聞, 藤沢経済新聞 — so any regional-qualifier economic paper reads as a member of it. Claude excluded the whole pattern; GLM and Grok flag it; DeepSeek proposed three regional titles without noticing. Grok's 京浜経済新聞 was checked against the network's member list and not found, which is the correct check.
- **The Nikkei lineage is closed.** 中外物価新報, 中外商業新報, 日本産業経済新聞, 経済時事新報 (merged into it in 1942), 東洋経済新報社, 産業経済新聞社 (Sankei's legal name), 中部経済新聞 (real, Nagoya, 1946, known as 中経), 日本証券新聞, 株式新聞, 日刊産業新聞. Anything morphologically adjacent — 東京市況新報, 本邦商業新報 — would be flagged by a clearance lawyer for proximity even if the string is free (Grok).
- **The contraction is the mark.** 日経 is built from 日本経済新聞, and the index is named from the contraction, so a candidate needs a two-character short form that is itself free and dry (Claude, GLM). Contractions that fail: 中経 (中部経済新聞, 中経出版), 日商 (Japan Chamber of Commerce), 東商 (homophone of 東証, the Tokyo exchange), 帝経 (one vowel from 帝京大学; 帝国データバンク adjacent), 日産 (Nissan), 東邦 (Toho, the film studio), 国経 (homophone of 滑稽, *comical*), 全経 (a real bookkeeping-education body, small).
- **Well-known fictional papers are taken too.** 東都新聞, 東西新聞, 帝都新聞, 毎朝新聞 recur in film, manga and television (Claude); 東都経済新聞 appears as a newspaper in a published web-fiction chapter (ChatGPT, verified). A fictional collision is a real collision for this work.
- **Historical papers surface on search.** 関東経済新聞 — an issue dated 23 August 1950 on Japan Search (ChatGPT); 大阪経済新聞 in NDL records (DeepSeek); 東京毎夕新聞 (Claude, moderate recall); 東亜経済日報, a real Japanese paper on Korea–Japan economic affairs (Gemini); 内外タイムス; 金融日日新聞, recalled as a Meiji financial daily (GLM, unverified). Pre-war and occupation-era trade papers are numerous and poorly indexed, and every reviewer names that as the gap they could not close.
- **Chinese-language collisions.** 商報 and 通商日報 are occupied in Hong Kong, Taiwan and China (Grok); 經濟日報 exists (Claude). A Japanese title that is also a Chinese title is not clean internationally.
- **A structural question the round surfaced rather than resolved** (DeepSeek, Section 6): the Nikkei's authority came from being the *national* economic paper. A regional qualifier — 東海, 北陸, 京浜 — makes a different institutional claim, that a regional authority computed the national index. Whether that is intended is a design decision, not a naming one. The evening-paper form has its own ground: an evening paper has first claim on closing prices, which is the operational basis of its right to measure (GLM), and the Sankei began under an evening publisher, 夕刊大阪新聞社 (Claude).

**United States ratings house.**

- **Surnames in the exact trade are excluded by everyone.** Standard, Poor, Dow, Jones, Moody, Fitch, Best, Dun, Bradstreet, Duff, Phelps, Kroll, Egan. Also C. E. Hooper (audience ratings, absorbed by Nielsen), Putnam, and Coffin & Burr (a real Boston house).
- **Any pair of Anglo surnames is some law or accounting firm somewhere** (Claude, Qwen). The exposure is evocation rather than ownership, and no pair should be called clear; ChatGPT found a UK accountancy (Marden & Co), an Irish talent advisory (Barden) and an investment bank (Keene Advisors) inside three of its own six pairs.
- **Individually occupied surnames** that recur across reviewers: Marsh and Mercer (Marsh McLennan — DeepSeek, Gemini, ChatGPT); Sterling (famous fictions, and generic in finance — Claude, Gemini, Qwen); Crane (Crane Co. — Grok, Qwen); Bell; Ames (a department-store chain live in 1996 — Claude, GLM); Cabot (Cabot Corporation); Emerson; Halstead (New York real estate, adjacent to class 36 — Claude, against Qwen's Halsted & Corwin); Wainwright (Qwen rejects it as a financial name; DeepSeek proposes Aldrich & Wainwright at low risk — an unresolved disagreement); Pike (DeepSeek's Halloran & Pike at low risk; ChatGPT rejects Harlan & Pike on a surfaced fictional advisory — same disagreement). Kemp is close to Kemper.
- **Initials are part of the name.** Pairs abbreviating to P&L, M&A, M&S are accidental jokes and count as winks (Claude). S&K collides with SK Group, which is serious for a work that covers Korea. W&T is W&T Offshore. L&H is Lernout & Hauspie. WHX was Handy & Harman's holding company.
- **No pair was proposed by two reviewers.** ChatGPT declines to recommend any of its own and would *leave the US ratings house unresolved rather than quietly promote a surname pair with known adjacent financial associations*. GLM's Thayer & Biddle — Nicholas Biddle of the Second Bank as an on-theme authority — is the one candidate a reviewer argued for on grounds beyond absence of recall.

**Korean index.**

- **The issuer in 1996 was plausibly the statistics office**, 통계청, not an exchange (Claude). KOSPI carries no constituent count, and neither should its analogue (Claude, GLM, Grok).
- **Typo-shadows of KOSPI are worse than echoes.** KOPSI, KOSBI (anagram or one letter off, read as errors); **KOCPI — one letter from KOSPI, and it embeds CPI** (Grok and Claude, independently). KOCPI is the placeholder [[DEC-009-three-markets-and-convergence]] carries, and two reviewers reject it. KOSCI: proposed by GLM, rejected by Grok as a citation-index echo and a KOSPI typo. KCPPI embeds PPI.
- **Real Korean bodies and products occupy the space.** KOSPI, KOSDAQ, KONEX, KRX 300, KOSEF, KODEX, KOSIS, KOPIS, KOPRI, KOICA, KPX, KORI (the atomic-energy institute), KOREX (Korea Express); KCSPI is KOSPI's own predecessor name (DeepSeek rejects it; Claude allows it only as a predecessor series). KIX reads as Kansai airport. KOSEC reads as *Korea-SEC*, importing the US regulator.
- **Hangul readings matter.** KOPPI and KOPI read as 코피, *nosebleed* (Claude). A candidate needs a Hangul expansion given alongside, and several reviewers coined one: 한국생활관행지수 (KOLPI), 한국국가종합지수 (KONCI), 한국종합거래지수 (KOCTI), 한국행위종합지수 (KOPCI), 한국공인주가지수 (KOPIX).
- **Whether the category word leaks is disputed.** Grok rejects any acronym expanding to *practice, waiting, letter, solitude, call*; ChatGPT builds KOPCI on 행위, *act / conduct / practice*, on the ground that it is administrative rather than sentimental. This is a register judgement the round did not settle.

**Japanese and United States index names.**

- **Numbers carried by real indices are off limits**: 30, 100, 225, 400, 500, 2000, and the band 200–260 sits audibly close to 225 (GLM). Gemini and Qwen used 225 and 500 anyway; DeepSeek rejects 225 for a US index as a Nikkei collision.
- **What the number is a function of** — the round agrees, against Gemini and Qwen: the count of issues *the house itself graded* at the base date, fixed by a replacement rule, never a function of market size or a breadth aspiration (Claude, GLM, Grok). Claude notes S&P's own composite ran at **90** names from 1926 until it became the 500 in 1957, which makes 90 period-authentic and, as far as recalled, unowned. Grok argues for a specialist magnitude, 80 or 120. ChatGPT reads the count literally from the universe as **11**, the number of US-listed practices, and asks whether it is a fixed target or a reconstitution count — a question that belongs to [[Q-002-listing-lifecycle]].
- **For the Japanese form** the count is optional and informal, as *Nikkei 225* is (Claude); the constituent count belongs in the methodology, and *Average* must correspond to a defined aggregation, not decoration (ChatGPT). Do not put *Dow* in the Japanese title, as Nikkei did from 1975 to 1985 and then dropped (Grok).

**Tickers.**

- **Occupied, verified or recalled by several reviewers:** `LNG` (Cheniere Energy — all seven), `LONG` (eLong's former Nasdaq ticker), `COMP` (Nasdaq Composite; also Compass Inc.), `LGC` (LGC Group; HOSE-listed CII Bridges & Roads), `LGX` (Luxembourg Green Exchange), `LGCX` (a Chinese industrial-software firm, domain occupied), `NKY` / `N225` / `NI225`, `KS11` / `KS200` / `KQ11`, `SPX` / `INDU` / `DJI` / `OEX` / `NDX`, `WTI`, `BR` (Broadridge), `MP` (MP Materials), `CR` (Crane), `NTR`, `KOR`, `ZK` (a 2024 crypto asset — a different namespace, noted).
- **Assume every purely alphabetic three- or four-letter code is occupied somewhere** and use the alphanumeric or prefixed convention real index codes use — `N225`, `.KS11`, `^GSPC` (Claude, DeepSeek). The composite lists nowhere; its code is a feed convention only, and should sit beside the covering institute's own code if it has one (GLM).
- **Composite candidates** — one per reviewer, none shared: `.LCOMP` / `LCOMP` (Claude, DeepSeek), `LGCP` (GLM), `LGXC` (Grok), `LNGCMP` (ChatGPT, not found in the Nasdaq directories), `LCMP` (Qwen), `^LNGX` (Gemini). Grok rejects `LCOM` for the *composite* echo; `LGCI` surfaces as church initials (ChatGPT).

**Rejected for being clever at the work's expense.** Every reviewer generated some and discarded them, which is what the prompt asked. Recorded so they are not proposed again: any publisher built on 待, 便り, 黄昏 or 文通; *Bearer Gazette*, *Blind Trust Nippō*, *Coverage Daily*; *Still & Wait*, *Trueheart & Vail*, *Longley & Voss*, surnames like Lovelace, Hart, Penn; *Residual Average*, *The Waiting Index*, *The Uncontacted Average*, *The Absence Composite*, *Affections Index*, *Solitude 500*; any Korean acronym that expands to a word of feeling; *Aldrich & Vreeland*, a real 1908 statute, as borrowed cleverness; *Standard & Moody's*, *Dow & Poor's* as pastiche.

## What was refused, and what remains

Every reviewer refused to assert any candidate clear. The lists of what could not be checked agree: live registers at J-PlatPat, KIPRIS, USPTO and EUIPO; WHOIS; Bloomberg, Reuters, KRX and TSE mnemonic tables beyond public headline codes; pre-1945 Japanese dailies and regional Korean press; US state registries and common-law use; fiction in unindexed print. ChatGPT adds that **`LONGING COMPOSITE` itself was never screened** — the prompt fixed it, and no reviewer checked the phrase — which folds into the unperformed check on the work's own name in [[DEC-001-project-name-longing]].

Three things this round leaves for the user rather than for another round:

- ~~Whether the Japanese publisher is national or regional.~~ **Decided 2026-09-20: national**, after the assistant set out what each would mean — a national paper carries the Nikkei's kind of authority and makes the later absorption into the composite weightier, at the cost of the hardest naming ground; a regional one needs a line of backstory and reads as a member of the 経済新聞 network. The user chose national. Regional candidates (東海, 北陸, 京浜) drop out of the table below; the surviving forms are the ones without a place name — 夕刊経済新報, 秋津経済新報, 全国経済新聞, 商工日報, 商況日報, 首都経済新聞, 夕刊商業, 日本取引新聞 — and 帝都経済新聞 stays flagged for the fictional 帝都新聞. See [[DEC-009-three-markets-and-convergence]].
- **Whether the category word may appear.** *Practice* in an acronym's expansion is administrative to one reviewer and a leak to another.
- **The count convention for the US index**, which now has three incompatible readings — a period-authentic 90, a specialist 80 or 120, or the literal 11 — and which depends on what [[Q-002-listing-lifecycle]] decides about reconstitution. The user's answer on 2026-09-20: *the world need not be followed so exactly* — period authenticity is not a constraint on the number. `user-originated`; the number itself is still unselected.

On 2026-09-20 the user deferred the category-word question and the `KOCPI` replacement to a later discussion, and asked what a national versus a regional publisher would mean; the answer, and the decision that followed it, are in [[SRC-2026-09-20-japanese-publisher-is-national]].
