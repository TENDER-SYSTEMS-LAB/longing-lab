# Academic Models and Cross-domain Designed Systems

A first survey identifies six explicit models and one mechanism-identification critique. It supplies inspectable alternatives and failure modes, not a validated LONGING model. Equations, units, numerical estimates, assumed examples, page references, and primary links are preserved in [the research notes](../../raw/surveys/2026-09-06-academic-model-research-notes.md). The research brief's account of user directions is a secondary handoff record, not a verbatim conversation.

## Broader discovery catalog

A second user-requested pass expands from disappearing practices to mathematical descriptions and designed systems across social science, psychology, biology, mathematics, art, and engineering. The [120-entry cross-domain catalog](../../raw/surveys/2026-09-06-cross-domain-model-survey.md) records authors or teams, original publication/reference dates, source types, primary or official links, and one-sentence modeling targets.

There are **117 substantive model/system entries, one bibliographic lead, and two adjacent data-mapping artworks**. This is a breadth-first discovery map, not a full-text systematic review or a validation of those systems. Five earlier surveyed papers recur for navigation. Reviews, books, working papers, software, and artworks are explicitly distinguished. The catalog's reading limits explain publisher-preview and bibliographic-only cases.

The grouping is editorial. A model that describes observed behavior, a mechanism that changes incentives, and an artwork that generates a virtual world serve different purposes. In particular, *Emotional Stock Exchange* has no public causal pricing specification verified in this pass, and *Pulse Room* maps recorded pulse data rather than explaining cardiac dynamics. Neither is counted among the 117 substantive entries. Leontief remains a bibliographic lead.

The later request asked only what each work models. No equations were imported, no coefficients calibrated, and no LONGING mechanism adopted. The first survey's more detailed cards and proposals below retain their earlier scope. The research gap is now better populated, especially for cultural transmission and designed institutions; applicability to LONGING remains unresolved.

## What each model could contribute

All mappings and tests in this section are **llm-proposed**. None selects a factor set or alters the adopted project architecture.

| Model | Quantity to distinguish | Possible LONGING use | Evidence needed before use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bass: adoption | Cumulative first adoption versus current occasions | Security-specific adoption baseline | First-use cohorts, repeat use, exits, eligible population |
| Abrams–Strogatz: competition | Share and switching versus absolute participation | Competition between substitutable practices | Transition counts, dual use, population denominator, independent status measures |
| Rinaldi: linear feedback | Personal affect versus public behavior | Explanatory analogy for persistence and reinforcement | Repeated personal measures and identifiable inputs; currently uncalibrated |
| Loewenstein: anticipation | Experienced delay versus preference for waiting | Valuation hypothesis for waiting-related experiences | Delay variation, comparable outcomes, repeated valuations and uncertainty |
| Candia: attention decay | Public attention versus practice or personal memory | Observation-layer baseline for an event's attention | Age/cohort-aware attention data, renewed inputs, alternative fits |
| Rohlfs: network demand | Connected membership versus generic popularity | Participation thresholds for network-dependent practices | Contact structure, fees, operating costs, observed join/leave behavior |

These are candidates at different analytical levels. Turning all six into additive common factors would mix states, parameters, observation processes, and utilities. A shared factor requires separately observed variation across relevant securities; a security-specific parameter does not become common merely by appearing in several equations. Factors may outnumber securities. Identification must be argued from measurements and restrictions, not a count ratio.

## What the survey changes

The literature gives names and equations to several mechanisms previously discussed informally. It also exposes three checks to add to model assessment:

1. **Check the sign implied by the equation.** Bass adoption saturation is not abandonment. The baseline collective-attention system has no endogenous revival. The competing-language model has different stability regimes. Selecting a model can silently impose a narrative before any data are fitted.
2. **Separate a curve from its mechanism.** Frank's equivalent memory curves reinforce the distinction in [[factor-set-failure-profile-review]] between exact forward accounting and inverse identification. Several explanations can reproduce the same observations.
3. **Keep valuation separate.** Anticipatory utility and network service fees do not determine a security's unit or quotation. [[Q-004-unit-of-account]] remains unresolved. Declining practice frequency can coexist with increasing personal preciousness; no surveyed equation supplies the missing price bridge.

No numerical coefficient in the notes is a LONGING calibration. Bass and Candia report estimates, Abrams–Strogatz reports fitted cross-region results, while the Rinaldi and Rohlfs cards are theoretical and Loewenstein's structural anticipation parameters remain unfitted. Illustrative values are explicitly labeled. Reported fit metrics are not interchangeable or prospective validation.

## Proposed next research step

Choose one observable mechanism before choosing a factor architecture. For example, compare active practice participation, related attention, and valuation over the same dates. Record each variable's population, unit, sampling frequency, missingness, and measurement error. A yearly rate must be converted before weekly use; normalizing a series does not erase its original meaning.

Compare the candidate against a simple trend and a plausible alternative mechanism on later periods or held-out cohorts. Report uncertainty and failures, including cases where fewer observations cannot distinguish models. An exact ledger decomposition alone does not pass this test. The six models need not be combined into one engine, and none has been implemented or calibrated in this survey.

Remaining literature gaps include intergenerational cultural transmission, active-use substitution, infrastructure retirement, chance encounters, contested value, and index construction. This bounded first pass completes an initial academic survey; it does not close those questions or prove an absence of closer prior work.

## Evolution — from model teaching to application questions

After beginner-oriented explanations and worked equations, the user endorsed separating new starts from discontinuation and stated that feedback is necessary. These are user directions; no generator, loop, factor set, or price rule has been selected. The no-real-data case is the current design scenario, not a permanent rejection of observed data. The original accessible teaching dialogue is preserved in [the partial dialogue export](../../raw/conversations/2026-09-06-academic-model-dialogue.md); hypothetical examples do not calibrate LONGING. The API omitted the latest turns; their user positions and assistant clarification were subsequently recovered as original message excerpts from the local task log. The earlier compiled continuation remains preserved with its original provenance limits.

The user finds the two-option Abrams–Strogatz baseline difficult to apply and raises an n-item extension as a possibility. The assistant's further distinction among substitution, complementarity and independent coexistence remains `llm-proposed`. More options alone do not settle overlap.

The requested application clarifications yielded two unconfirmed proposals:

- **Loewenstein:** separate anticipation enjoyment from delay burden in film development. Faster processing might increase participation while removing a valued waiting experience. Waiting duration, expected outcome value, savoring tendency, urgency and experience value are proposed adaptation variables, not the paper's original specification. Uncertain replies require additional modeling; the price effect is open.
- **Rohlfs:** examine meaningful counterparties and availability in spontaneous calls or correspondence. Scheduling pressure could weaken successful contact, continued participation and the shared norm. A broader participation burden is an adaptation of monetary fees. This is selective to relational practices, not a universal solitude model or physical-infrastructure decay law.

The difference is between how waiting feels and whether a meaningful participation network persists. Valuable-but-hard and feasible-but-not-chosen remain distinct. See [[pricing-model]] for the pending design questions and [[data-sources]] for synthetic-data status.
